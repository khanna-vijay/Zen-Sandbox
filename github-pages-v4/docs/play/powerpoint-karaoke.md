# 🎤 PowerPoint Karaoke

> **Absurd premises, impeccable logic.** One person gives a completely confident presentation
> over a slide deck they have **never seen before**. They do not know the next image until it
> appears. The slides keep advancing. They keep talking.

That is the whole game. The comedy is not in the pictures — it is in watching somebody explain
an absurd image with total institutional conviction, as though it obviously belongs in their talk.

## The rule that makes it work

**Never break, never apologise, never say "I don't know what this is."** The performer's job is
to make the slide make sense.

1. **You have never seen the deck.** Nobody shows it to you and you do not get to look first.
   That is not a handicap, it *is* the game.
2. **Every slide is yours.** You made it. You chose it. You put it in this talk on purpose.
   Explain it that way.
3. **Never break.** No apologising, no laughing at your own deck, no asking the room what a
   slide means. Breaking is the only way to actually lose.
4. **Never describe the picture.** "This is a photo of a horse" is nothing. "Q3 *is* the horse"
   is the game.
5. **Keep the thread.** Each slide connects to the one before it, however absurd the join. You
   are giving one talk, not narrating ten unrelated images.
6. **Land the ending.** The deck finishes on a Thank You card. Finish on an ask, then take
   questions *in character*.
7. **Audience rule:** laugh, do not heckle, and never tell the performer what a slide "really" is.

!!! quote "The whole game in one line"
    **The slide is never wrong — only your explanation is missing.**

## How to run a round

1. Press **SPIN THE WHEEL**. It lands on a talk format and deals a deck behind the scenes.
2. The card shows two things: the **format**, and the **shape** the talk follows —
   *name it → set the scene → show the numbers → make the ask*. Read the shape out to the
   performer. It is the only help they get, and it is what stops the talk becoming a list.
3. Want a premise as well? Press <kbd>S</kbd> for a scenario — who they are and what the
   occasion is — and read it aloud. Optional; plenty of groups prefer to shout one out instead.
4. Press **▶ Start presenting** and hand over. Go fullscreen with <kbd>F</kbd>.
5. Advance with <kbd>→</kbd>. Either drive it yourself every 20–30 seconds, or let the performer
   say "next slide" when they run dry. **Driving it yourself is harder and funnier.**
6. At the Thank You card, take two questions in character, then <kbd>Esc</kbd> and spin again
   for the next player.

!!! tip "New group?"
    Leave **Slide assistant text** ticked for the first few rounds. The small corner cue tells
    the performer what each slide is *for* and stops anyone freezing. Untick it once they have
    the hang of it.

!!! warning "Nobody previews the deck — not even the facilitator"
    There is deliberately no thumbnail view. You cannot spoil a slide you have not seen, and the
    person running the laptop reacting honestly is half the fun for the room.

## The eight formats

Every spin re-rolls the format, the premise **and** the slides together. Landing on the same
format twice still deals a completely different deck.

| Format | The feel | Slides | Difficulty |
|---|---|:--:|:--:|
| **Board Room Presentation — Annual Report** | A quarterly review going wrong | 8–12 | 2 |
| **Shark Tank — Raising Funds** | Maximum energy, zero shame | 8–12 | 2 |
| **Story (Disney Format)** | Storybook causality — every slide *because of* the last one | 8–12 | 2 |
| **Product Launch** | Unveiling something nobody asked for, beautifully | 8–12 | 2 |
| **TED Talk** | Thought leadership, status work | 8–12 | 3 |
| **Motivational Keynote Speaker** | Slow, reverent, faintly threatening | 8–12 | 3 |
| **Mission Brief** | Villains, spies, evacuation drills | 8–12 | 3 |
| **PhD Thesis & Research Breakthrough** | Precision, hedging, over-citation | 8–12 | 4 |

The app holds **207 slides** and **42 premises**, and never deals at random: each deck is drawn
one slide per **narrative beat**, so an accidental deck still tells a plausible story. Slides
from the last eight decks are held back from the next draw, and the draw favours whichever
images have been shown least — so a class of twelve sees roughly 129 different images with about
half of one repeat. Type a seed in **⚙ Settings** to reproduce a deck exactly (which switches
the no-repeat memory off — a seed has to be deterministic).

## Why it is worth playing

It is a comedy game, but it trains a specific and unusually transferable set of skills.

- **Spontaneity under observation.** You cannot pre-plan. The next slide is genuinely unknown,
  so the brain has to stop drafting and start responding — the single hardest habit to unlearn
  in public speaking.
  → [Unfiltered Spontaneity](../theory/01_the-self/01_S1__unfiltered-spontaneity.md)
- **Justification.** Improv's core muscle: take whatever you are given and make it deliberate.
  Every slide is an unwanted offer you must accept and integrate — *"yes, and"* against a
  picture instead of a partner.
  → [Justification](../theory/03_the-scene/03_S6__justification.md) ·
  [Justify the absurd](../theory/03_the-scene/03_S6_T1__justify-the-absurd.md)
- **Recovering out loud.** Players learn that a stumble is survivable, because the deck does not
  wait and the audience does not mind.
  → [Self-Recovery](../theory/01_the-self/01_S6__self-recovery.md)
- **Structure without a script.** The performer is told the deck's *shape* — problem, insight,
  plan, ask — so they practise landing a narrative arc while improvising all of the content.
  → [Narrative Architecture](../theory/03_the-scene/03_S3__narrative-architecture.md)
- **Status and commitment.** Playing a high-status expert who is completely wrong is a safe,
  funny way to practise vocal authority.
  → [Status Modulation](../theory/02_the-partner/02_S2__status-modulation.md) ·
  [Commit 100%](../theory/01_the-self/01_P1__commit-100.md)
- **Listening to yourself.** Callbacks only happen if you remember what you said three slides
  ago. It builds working memory for your own material.
  → [Thematic Synthesis](../theory/04_the-ensemble/04_S5__thematic-synthesis.md)

Useful for improv classes, presentation-skills training, team offsites, onboarding icebreakers
and conference warm-ups.

## Group sizes, timing and variants

| | |
|---|---|
| **Solo turn** | 8–12 slides, roughly 3–5 minutes |
| **A class of 12** | about 60–75 minutes including laughs and notes |
| **Tag-team variant** | two performers, swap every third slide — the incoming player must continue the outgoing player's argument |
| **Expert-and-interpreter** | one performer talks, a second "translates" for the audience; doubles the justification pressure |
| **Q&A variant** | after the last slide the audience asks two questions in character — this is where the best material usually is |

## About the slides

Every image in the library was generated for this project across ten slide types (acronym,
abstract art, call-to-action, diagram, data visualisation, illustration, map/timeline,
photographic, quote, symbol/ritual) crossed with six registers, then hand-screened for content
safety — everything is suitable for a ten-year-old audience, because it runs in classrooms.

The game is **MIT licensed** — code *and* images. Teach with it, run it at a corporate offsite,
charge for the workshop, fork it. The build pipeline that ideates, prompts, renders and
compresses the slides lives in this repository under `game-source/powerpoint-karaoke/`.
