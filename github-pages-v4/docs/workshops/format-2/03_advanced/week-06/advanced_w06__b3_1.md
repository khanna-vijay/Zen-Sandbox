# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Redline

![Redline](../img/advanced_w06__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room builds a stamping pulse, cuts it dead at the top, and holds the silence before restarting.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 06 run-sheet](../week-06.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 6 is about pacing, edits and the shape of a set. This is the first thing the room does, before any scene work. It puts the edit in the body — a build, a hard stop, a held silence — so that later, when someone calls an edit in a montage, they already know in their feet what cutting on the rise feels like and what a held gap does to a room. It feeds the sweep-edit and shape-of-a-set blocks that follow.

## What students actually learn

- A cut at the top of a rise feels good; a cut after the peak feels late. They stop needing that explained.
- Silence has length, and different lengths do different things. Eight seconds is not a mistake.
- Anyone can call the stop. The permission gets used in scene edits later that day.

## Setup

Clear floor, everyone standing in a loose circle so each person can see the others' feet. No chairs in the way. Check the floor is not a shared wall with another class — stamping travels. Have one or two chairs at the edge for anyone who prefers to sit and slap thighs. You need nothing else.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Demonstrate the engine — alternate stamps, thighs slapped on the beat, body loose. Offer clapping-only or seated as equal options. Bring everyone in at slow walking tempo. |
| 1 | Drive the tempo up by degrees toward the edge. Clap once. Everyone stops dead. Hold three seconds. Restart slow. Repeat once. |
| 1 | Same build, but vary the holds. Cut and hold one second. Build, cut, hold eight. Say nothing about the difference. |
| 1 | Hand over the cut. Anyone may clap to stop; anyone may restart at any tempo. Let four or five cuts happen. Stay out of it. |
| 1 | Take it back. Build once more, cut at the top, hold the longest silence yet. Move straight into the next warm-up without comment. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| As the room joins the engine | *"Loose knees. Loose jaw."* |
| Mid-build, round one | *"Up a gear. Stay together."* |
| Just before you clap | *"Cut on the rise."* |
| During a held silence | *"Hold it. Don't fill it."* |
| Restarting after a hold | *"Slow. Right down."* |
| When handing over the cut | *"Anyone's clap. Take it."* |
| If nobody cuts for a while | *"Someone stop us."* |
| Final hold | *"Longer than's comfortable."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The stamps land together without anyone counting. The stop is instant — no straggler beat, no laugh to cover it. The room stands in the silence without shuffling or catching eyes. When the cut is handed over, at least three different people use it, and at least one restarts at a tempo nobody expected. Faces are alert rather than amused.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Two or three extra stamps after the clap, every time. | They are listening to each other rather than to you, so the stop ripples outward. | Make the clap sharper and higher. Say "stop is one beat, not three" once, then rebuild. |
| Laughter breaks every silence at about two seconds. | The gap is uncomfortable and they are discharging it. Normal in a first pass. | Don't scold it. Just hold the next one longer. By the eight-second hold the laughing usually stops on its own. |
| Nobody claps when you hand the cut over. | Uncertainty about whether they are really allowed, plus nobody wanting to go first. | Say "someone stop us" and wait. Do not rescue it by clapping yourself — waiting three awkward seconds teaches more than your clap would. |
| Tempo builds into chaos with no shared pulse. | You pushed the acceleration too fast, so people are racing rather than driving together. | Cut immediately, restart slow, and climb in smaller steps. The edge is only useful if they reach it together. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Run it exactly as written. In the handover round you will likely get five or six cuts because everyone can see everyone — let them all happen. |
| **14–17** | One circle, still. Stand in the ring rather than the centre so your clap reaches both sides. Trim the handover round to four cuts so the final build still fits inside the minute. |
| **18–20** | One circle, but widen it and clap with hands above head height so the far side sees as well as hears. Expect the stop to ripple; accept one beat of lag. In the handover round, name three people who have not yet cut and tell them the clap is theirs. |

## Variations

**Clap engine** — Drop the stamping entirely. The whole room runs the pulse on claps and thigh-slaps only, standing or seated.  
*Easier — lower impact, quieter for shared buildings, and removes the coordination load of the alternating feet.*

**No signal** — Remove the clap. The room must find the stop together, with no leader — they cut when they collectively sense the top of the rise.  
*Harder — forces genuine listening and shared attention rather than reaction to a cue.*

**Restart with a tone** — Whoever restarts the engine must first make one sustained vocal sound, and the room matches its energy in the new tempo.  
*Different emphasis — shifts from timing to offer-and-match, and connects the pulse to voice for later scene work.*

## Debrief

1. **Which hold felt right, and which one felt wrong?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* They describe the one-second hold as feeling snatched or unfinished, and the eight-second one as uncomfortable at first then settling. Someone says the long one made them pay more attention. Move on as soon as two people disagree about which was better.

2. **When you cut the room, how did you decide when?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* "It felt like it was about to fall apart" or "it was still climbing and I wanted it to stop while it was good." You want the word before, not after. If everyone says "I don't know, I just did", that is also fine — take it and go.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity. Stamping is the only physical demand: name at the top that clapping-only, standing or seated, is a full version and not a lesser one, and say it before anyone has to ask. Watch for anyone with knee, ankle or back issues and check the floor is not concrete over a hard surface. Loud, sudden noise can be difficult for some people — flag at the start of the session that this warm-up involves rhythmic stamping and a sharp clap, and let anyone step to the edge and keep the pulse on their thighs. Stamping carries through buildings; check with neighbouring rooms.

## Why it works

Pacing is usually taught as a note after the fact — "that scene ran long". This puts the decision in the body while it is happening. The build creates a rising energy the whole room can feel physically, so the moment of the cut is not an abstract judgement but a bodily one: they know when it is climbing and when it has peaked. Holding the silence afterwards makes the gap into a thing with a shape and a duration rather than an absence. Handing the cut over transfers the authority before any scene is at stake, so when the cue "cut on the rise, let the quiet one breathe" appears in a montage later, both halves have already been rehearsed with no content attached.
