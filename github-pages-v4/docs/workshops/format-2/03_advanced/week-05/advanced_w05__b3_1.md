# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Two Threads

![Two Threads](../img/advanced_w05__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> Two impulses travel opposite ways round the circle and whoever they collide on has to make them one thing.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 05 run-sheet](../week-05.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 5 is about weaving — callbacks, mapping, theme. This is the first thing they do, before any scene work. It puts two unrelated streams in motion and forces a physical merge, so the day's central move happens in the body before it is discussed. It feeds the mapping and callback work later in the session, and the fused word gives you a live seed to reincorporate in the piece.

## What students actually learn

- They stop treating two unrelated offers as a problem to be solved and start treating the collision as the interesting bit.
- They discover fusion can be instant and physical — it does not require a clever idea first.
- They hold two tracks of attention at once without dropping either, which is what long-form asks of them all afternoon.

## Setup

Clear floor, one circle, everyone standing. No chairs in the ring. Nothing to hand out. Know your own launch — pick a stomp and a blunt sound before class so you do not hesitate. Check the ceiling and floor will take jumping; if not, swap the jump for a hard clap.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Circle up, start the bounce, name the two directions and have everyone point each way. Launch thread one clockwise — stomp plus sound — and run it half a lap, then stop. |
| 1 | Launch thread two anticlockwise from the far side — word plus arm gesture, passed by association. Run it half a lap. Stop. Tell them what happens on a collision: freeze, then fuse. |
| 2 | Run both threads at once. Collision player freezes, shouts one move-and-word built from both, room copies it with a jump, then that player relaunches both directions. Aim for three or four fusions. |
| 1 | Call the last fusion. Take the fused word, say it back once as a full room, and tell them you will ask for it later in the session. Move straight on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| As the circle forms | *"Bounce. Keep bouncing — the bounce doesn't stop."* |
| First thread launching | *"Take what you got. Change it a little. Send it."* |
| Both threads running | *"Two things at once. Don't watch only one side."* |
| A collision player hesitates | *"Freeze. Now smash them together. Any answer."* |
| After a fusion lands | *"Whole room — copy it, jump."* |
| Fusions getting careful or clever | *"Faster. Ugly is fine."* |
| Energy sagging mid-round | *"Louder. Bigger arm."* |
| Final call | *"Hold that word. It's coming back."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The circle is noisy and slightly out of control. Threads distort fast, so what arrives at the collision bears little relation to what you launched. Collision players freeze for barely a beat, then bark something half-formed, and the room copies it without judging. Three or four fusions happen inside two minutes. When you ask for the final word back, everyone says it at once and grins.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The two threads pass through each other and nobody claims the collision. | Players are watching only the side they receive from, so neither person notices they got both. | Stop. Walk the collision once in slow motion with two volunteers. Then restart and call "you've got both" out loud the first two times. |
| Long pauses while a collision player thinks of a good fusion. | They think the fusion has to be witty or make sense. | Call "any answer, now" and count them in: three, two, one. Praise the ugliest fusion out loud so the standard drops. |
| The threads barely change — the same stomp goes all the way round. | Copying feels safer than distorting, and nobody wants to break the chain. | Side-coach "change it a little" every few passes. If it persists, tell them the copy must be wrong on purpose. |
| The bounce dies and the circle goes still and quiet. | Attention has shifted to getting it right rather than keeping it moving. | Bounce visibly yourself and call "bounce" once. Do not explain. Physical energy comes back before the game does. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Threads meet fast, so run half a lap on the solo threads and expect four or five fusions in the two-minute round. Keep the launches further apart than feels natural. |
| **14–17** | One circle. Standard as written — three or four fusions. Launch the second thread from directly opposite you so the collision lands near the quarter points. |
| **18–20** | One circle still — do not split, the collision is the point. Shorten the solo thread runs to a quarter lap each so you protect the full two minutes of double running. Expect three fusions, not four. |

## Variations

**One thread first** — Run a single thread all the way round, twice, before introducing the second direction at all. Cut the double round to ninety seconds.  
*Easier — removes the split attention for groups who are new to each other or still waking up.*

**Third thread** — After the second fusion, you launch a third thread from a random point in either direction. Collisions can now involve three inputs.  
*Harder — the fuser must synthesise more than two things and cannot pre-plan a pairing.*

**Meaning threads** — Drop the sounds and gestures. Thread one is a place, thread two is an emotion, each passed on by association. The fusion is a spoken image, not a move.  
*Different emphasis — shifts from physical merge to thematic merge, which maps directly onto the callback work later.*

## Debrief

1. **When you were the fusion, what did you actually do to make one thing out of two?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something concrete and non-clever: "I just did both at the same time and the word came out." If they describe deciding, ask who found it easier not to decide.

2. **What made the fusions land for the room?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* They point to commitment and speed rather than fit. Someone notices the room copied everything, so nothing could fail. Move on once that lands.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this game. Anyone who does not want to bounce or jump can stay flat-footed and clap instead — say this out loud at the start, not as an exception granted later. Loud sudden noises are part of it; flag that early for anyone sensitive. Voices can stay at a comfortable volume; commitment is not volume. If someone freezes on a collision and nothing comes, take the fusion yourself and pass it on. Do not wait them out.

## Why it works

Reincorporation feels like a mental trick when you meet it in a scene — you have to remember a thing and find a place for it. Here the two threads arrive on your body at the same instant, so the merge is a motor problem, not an intellectual one. You cannot plan a fusion because you do not know what will reach you. That removes the choice between a good synthesis and a fast one, and the room copying every fusion removes the cost of a bad one. By the time they get to mapping and callbacks in scene work, they have already felt that joining two unrelated threads is a physical reflex they possess. The final word, banked and returned to later, makes the cue literal: the thing planted in minute five pays off in hour three.
