# 🏟️ Showcase round — Borrowed Memories

![Borrowed Memories](../img/advanced_w05__b9_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The audience writes down small true things that happened to them; the cohort turns two of them into scenes and then shoots one as a short film.

`Players 10–20` · `~40 min` · `Complexity 4/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact incidental` · `Props: required`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 05 run-sheet](../week-05.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 5 has drilled callbacks, mapping and theme in short exercises. This is the showcase where those tools meet material the cohort did not invent. The card is fixed and outside them, so reincorporation cannot be faked by repeating a joke — it has to reach back to something real. It feeds the final week's full piece, where the source is an audience suggestion held for twenty minutes.

## What students actually learn

- That a true detail carries further than an invented one, and that they can build on someone else's life without mocking it.
- That obeying a hard external call inside two seconds produces sharper structure than negotiating the next move.
- That reincorporation reads as satisfying because the audience recognises the return, not because the return is clever.
- That handling other people's material carries an obligation, and that obligation improves the work.

## Setup

Chairs in a loose arc facing an open playing space. Index cards and pens for everyone. One bowl or hat. A chair to the side for the Teller and later the Director. Clear the floor of bags. If you have lights, set a fade cue for CREDITS; if not, agree that you clap once and everyone drops the picture. Write the five calls — WIDE, CLOSE-UP, CUT TO, FLASHBACK, CREDITS — somewhere visible.

## How to run it — 40 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 4 | Everyone writes one small true memory, under sixty words, no names. State the rule: nothing you would not want read aloud by a stranger. Collect, shuffle into the bowl. |
| 3 | Teller draws, reads the card exactly as written, then adds a smell and a sound. Stop any joking. Teller sits. Let three seconds of silence sit before anyone moves. |
| 9 | Run the reel: three scenes. Each starts with a player stepping out on an image or feeling from the card. Edit by sweep or tap. Cut each scene at its strongest turn. |
| 2 | Director names one thread from the reel — a character, place or want — and names a genre aloud. Write both up. No discussion, no vote. |
| 4 | Teach the five calls with a thirty-second demo of each. Drill WIDE and CUT TO twice with the whole group so obedience is physical, not theoretical. |
| 10 | Shoot the feature. Director side-calls; players move inside two seconds. Push for at least one FLASHBACK. Call CREDITS while the picture is still alive, not after it dies. |
| 5 | Reset the space and take notes. Name one thing from the card that stayed honest, one that turned into re-enactment. Take two observations from the floor. Close. |
| 3 | Return to the cue. Ask what came back at the end and whether the room felt it land. Keep it short and finish on time. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| Teller starts adding opinion or a laugh line | *"Read it, don't sell it."* |
| First scene stalls at the top of the reel | *"Play the feeling, not the plot."* |
| Scene explains the card instead of using it | *"You're illustrating. Start somewhere else."* |
| Editing gets slow between reel scenes | *"Sweep it. Now."* |
| Players hesitate on a Director call | *"Two seconds. Move."* |
| WIDE is called and half the room stays put | *"Everybody in the picture."* |
| Feature drifts from the named thread | *"Back to the thread."* |
| The final image is building | *"Hold the picture. Credits."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The card is read flat and lands. Scenes start on an image — a kitchen light, a wet coat — and the plot arrives later. Players hit calls without looking at the Director for permission. The feature has recognisable genre grammar without becoming parody. At CREDITS, the frozen picture contains something from the card, and the room recognises it before anyone says so. Notes are specific: a line, a look, a moment where someone re-enacted instead of played.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Players re-enact the memory beat for beat, narrating what the card said. | They treat the card as a script rather than a source of images. | Stop and restart the scene from a detail that was never in the card — the weather, a sibling, the drive home. Say: use the temperature, not the events. |
| The room laughs at the memory during the Teller's read. | Nerves, plus no framing about the card's status. | Say clearly before the draw that the card is a gift, not a bit. If a laugh happens, name it plainly and move on. Do not scold. |
| The feature ignores the Director; calls arrive and nothing changes. | Players negotiating the next move rather than obeying. | Pause, re-drill CUT TO three times fast with the whole group standing. Restart the feature. Tell the Director to call louder and sooner. |
| Cards come back blank or joke-only. | People are protecting themselves, reasonably. | Model one yourself out loud during the writing minute — something small and dull, like a bus stop. Say small and dull is the target, not dramatic. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One reel of three scenes, everyone eligible to step out. One Director, one Teller. Shoot one feature with the full group; WIDE genuinely means all of them. Keep timings as written. |
| **14–17** | Reel scenes cap at three players each so the sides stay clear. Two Directors share the feature — swap at the five-minute mark on your call. Everyone still plays WIDE. |
| **18–20** | Split for the reel: half the room plays, half sits as first audience, then swap for a second short reel inside the same nine minutes (four minutes each, one minute to change over). Reunite for the feature. Two Directors, swapping at five minutes. Cut the debrief to four minutes and take one observation instead of two. |

## Variations

**Told twice** — Before the reel, a second player retells the same card from memory, getting it slightly wrong. Play the reel off the second version.  
*easier — the distortion gives permission to depart from the facts and kills re-enactment before it starts*

**Silent card** — The Teller reads the card silently, then only speaks the two sensory details. The players never hear the memory.  
*harder — forces the cohort to build from atmosphere alone, and the Teller must reincorporate the unheard content late*

**Two bowls** — Draw a second card at the eight-minute mark of the feature. The Director must weave its image into the ending.  
*different emphasis — shifts from thematic depth to live reincorporation under time pressure*

## Debrief

1. **What came from the card and stayed honest?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* A named moment with a detail attached — 'the pause before he answered the phone' — not 'the whole thing felt real'.

2. **Where did we start re-enacting instead of playing?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone identifies a specific line that repeated the card's information, and can say what they could have played instead.

3. **What returned at the end, and did you feel it before you thought it?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Two or three people name the same returning image and describe recognising it in the body — a laugh, a breath — before naming it.

4. **Whose card was that, and does it matter?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* The room accepts that anonymity held, or the writer volunteers and says the scene did not embarrass them. Either is fine. Move on quickly.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    State the card rule twice: nothing you would not want read aloud by a stranger. Cards are anonymous and no one is asked to claim one. Anyone may write something deliberately trivial — say so. Offer a pass: a player may sit out the feature and watch without explanation, and WIDE never requires anyone to be touched. No contact between players unless both have agreed to it in the moment; keep hands off shoulders and waists by default. If a card turns out to hold real distress, cut the scene, thank the writer generically, and draw another. Bin all cards yourself at the end, in front of the room.

## Why it works

Reincorporation is the punchline of structure because the audience is holding an open loop without knowing it, and closure lands as pleasure. Borrowed material sharpens this. When the source is a real memory from outside the cohort, players cannot quietly rewrite it to suit their scene, so every return is checkable — the room either recognises the thing or does not. The hard side-calls remove negotiation, which is where thematic material usually leaks away into discussion. Two seconds is short enough that players reach for what is already resonant rather than what is clever, and what is already resonant is the card. Synthesis then happens under pressure rather than by committee.
