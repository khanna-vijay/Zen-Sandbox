# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Tide and Rock

![Tide and Rock](../img/advanced_w07__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> Half the room surges like a house full of people while the other half keeps its own tempo, reading the surge out loud without obeying it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 07 run-sheet](../week-07.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 7 opens the audience contract. Before they play a set with a real room watching, they need one physical rehearsal of noticing a crowd without being steered by it. This warm-up gives the sensation in miniature, so later blocks on holding a scene through a quiet house have something to point back to.

## What students actually learn

- That a room's noise can be named out loud instead of absorbed silently as panic or permission.
- That changing what you're doing is a choice you make, not something a laugh does to you.
- That silence from a crowd is data with several possible meanings, not a verdict.

## Setup

Clear a strip of floor wide enough for two facing lines about three metres apart. No chairs in the gap. Stamping is part of it — check the floor and the neighbours below. Nobody needs to have read anything beforehand.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Form the two lines. Name Line A the house, Line B the players. Drill the house on SURGE, LIFT and DROP twice each. Push for volume — a polite house is useless here. |
| 1 | Players start a repeating full-body action at their own tempo. Call surges at random intervals. Players hold tempo and speak their reading aloud each time: 'they've gone', 'they want more'. |
| 1 | Keep surging. Call 'ride once'. Each player makes one deliberate change, then returns to their original tempo. Repeat the call once more if there's time. |
| 1 | Swap the lines. New house drills the three calls once. New players start actions. Surge at random; players hold tempo and read aloud. |
| 1 | Call 'ride once' for the new players. Then bring both lines to the centre. Five unison stamps, stop dead. Land the cue and move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During the house drill, if it's quiet | *"Louder. A real house is rude."* |
| As players begin their actions | *"Whole body. Pick a tempo you can keep."* |
| First surge, when a player speeds up | *"Tempo stays. Only your mouth moves."* |
| When readings go vague | *"Name it. Three words."* |
| Just before the ride call | *"Ride once — choose it, don't catch it."* |
| After the ride | *"Back to your tempo. It was always yours."* |
| On a DROP, if players deflate | *"Silence isn't a no. Keep going."* |
| On the final stamps | *"Five, and stop dead."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Players stay on tempo through a full-volume surge and still say something about it. The readings are specific and quick. When 'ride once' lands you see a clear, chosen change and then a return, not a slow drift. Both lines are breathing hard by the end.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Players speed up every time the house roars, then apologise for it. | The surge is being taken as an instruction, which is the exact habit the block exists to expose. | Let it happen once, then call 'tempo stays'. Have them count their action out loud under their breath to anchor it. |
| The house is polite — a soft murmur instead of a roar. | Adults in a new room won't be loud first. They're waiting for permission. | Drill the house alone for fifteen extra seconds. Join in yourself at full volume. Don't ask twice, just get louder. |
| Readings all come out as 'they liked it' or 'they didn't like it'. | They're grading themselves instead of describing the room. | Ban the words good and bad. Ask for what the crowd wants, not what it thinks: 'they want more', 'they're bored', 'they've moved on'. |
| The ride change is so small you can't see it from three metres. | Caution — they're trying not to overreact, and overcorrecting. | Call for a change you can see from the back. Bigger, slower or sharper by half again, not by a fraction. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One pair of lines, five or six per side. Everything runs as written; you'll have time for two 'ride once' calls per side. |
| **14–17** | One pair of lines still works — seven or eight per side. Cut the second drill repetition for the swapped house to keep the clock. One ride call per side. |
| **18–20** | Split into two pairs of lines running side by side, about five each. Give both the same calls from the middle of the room so surges land simultaneously. One ride call per side; skip the second drill repetition entirely. |

## Variations

**Sound only** — Players keep the action but drop the spoken reading. Instead they nod once when they clock a surge.  
*Easier — removes the split attention of speaking and moving, for groups that seize up when asked to narrate.*

**Mixed signals** — Split the house down the middle. Half surges, half drops, at the same time.  
*Harder — the reading can no longer be one word, so players must describe a divided room.*

**Two players, one scene** — Two players share the strip and keep a simple mimed task going together while the house surges. They read aloud to each other, not to the room.  
*Different emphasis — shifts from solo tempo-holding to keeping a partner's rhythm intact under crowd pressure.*

## Debrief

1. **What did a DROP feel like from the player's side?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone names the physical drop — stomach, shoulders, slowing down — and then notes they kept going anyway. If they only say 'bad', ask what their body did.

2. **When you rode once, what made you choose that particular change?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* A reason from the action itself — 'I'd been fast for ages so I went slow' — rather than 'they cheered so I went bigger'.

3. **Where does this show up in a set?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Concrete: chasing a laugh that already happened, or dumping a good scene because the room went quiet for eight seconds.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact at any point and nobody crosses the gap — say that before you start. Stamping is optional; tell anyone with knee, back or hearing sensitivity to clap or step in place instead, and offer it to the whole room so nobody has to declare anything. Anyone who'd rather not be in the player line can stay in the house for both rounds. Volume peaks are brief but real — warn the room once at the top.

## Why it works

The principle is that audience energy is information, not instruction. That's hard to teach as a note because the reflex is faster than the thought. Here the reflex is made visible: the player's tempo is a public, physical baseline, so any drift under a surge is seen by everyone including the player. Speaking the reading aloud forces the crowd's noise through language before it can reach the body, which inserts a gap where a decision can go. 'Ride once' then trains the decision separately from the trigger — you change because you chose to, and you return to your own tempo afterwards, proving the tempo was never the crowd's to set.
