# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Tail End

![Tail End](../img/advanced_w07__b3_3.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room makes a wave of noise, and one player places a flat line in its fade — not too early, not too late.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 07 run-sheet](../week-07.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 7 opens the audience contract. Everything after this block asks players to read a live room without being bossed by it. This warm-up puts that reading into the body first, at the smallest possible scale: one noise, one decay, one placement. It feeds the longer scene work later in the session, where the same judgement decides when to top a laugh, when to sit in it, and when to let a scene turn.

## What students actually learn

- They start hearing a laugh as a shape with a beginning, a peak and a tail — not as a single event that either happened or didn't.
- They stop rushing the next line the moment noise appears.
- They find out that silence is a legitimate choice, and that the room can tell when it was the right one.
- They feel the difference between responding to the room and obeying it.

## Setup

Open floor, horseshoe of chairs or standing. No props. You need a clear sightline to every player and enough room-volume tolerance for thirty seconds of loud group noise — check the neighbouring space beforehand. Have your demo line ready and deliberately dull.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Set the horseshoe. Explain the wave and the task in three sentences. Practise the wave twice, hand up and hand down, and insist on the three-second fade rather than a cut-off. |
| 1 | Demonstrate. One wave with your flat line landing in the tail. One with the line over the peak. One with the line after silence. Teach the three hand signals. Do not discuss. |
| 2 | Go round the horseshoe at speed. Wave, line, signal, next. Roughly eight to ten seconds each. Keep your hand doing the work and your mouth shut between turns. |
| 1 | Last pass with the silence option, then one debrief question with sentence answers. Cut the debrief on time even mid-hand. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During the practice wave, when the noise stops dead | *"Let it fade. Don't switch it off."* |
| First few turns, when players speak over the peak | *"Later. Wait for it to thin."* |
| When a player waits until full silence | *"That's the next scene now. Come in sooner."* |
| When lines start getting clever | *"Duller. Boring is the job."* |
| When the room's waves get half-hearted | *"Give them a real wave to land in."* |
| During the silence pass | *"You may say nothing. Choose it, don't freeze."* |
| Between turns, when discussion breaks out | *"Signal only. Next."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The waves are full-throated and fade rather than stop. Most players land somewhere in the last second of decay and the room's palms go flat. Lines are flat and ordinary. Turns take under ten seconds each with no chat between them. At least one player uses the silence option and the room agrees it was right.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The wave is a polite one-second 'ha' with no fade to land in. | Self-consciousness about making noise in front of each other, usually in the first ten minutes of the day. | Run two waves with no speaker at all and ask for it louder and longer. Join in yourself at full volume. Only then start the round. |
| Players try to be funny with their line and get an actual laugh. | They've heard 'improv exercise' and defaulted to joke-making. | Call 'duller' and give three sample lines aloud — a bus timetable, a receipt, a fridge item. Reset the frame: the line is a measuring stick, not a contribution. |
| Signals are all flat palms regardless of what happened. | The group is being kind rather than accurate, or they can't hear the tail from where they stand. | Tighten the horseshoe. Say you want disagreement in the signals and that a thumb is not criticism. Give one visibly early attempt yourself and require thumbs forward. |
| You run out of time with a third of the group untried. | Discussion between turns, or fades allowed to run to five seconds. | Cap the fade at three seconds and move to the next player the moment the signals go up. Note who missed and start with them next week. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One horseshoe. Everyone gets a turn in the two-minute round at roughly ten seconds each. Run the full silence pass with three or four volunteers. |
| **14–17** | One horseshoe. Shorten the fade to two seconds and cut the 'too late' demonstration to buy fifteen seconds. Everyone still gets one turn. Silence pass drops to two volunteers. |
| **18–20** | Split into two horseshoes at opposite ends of the room. Name a hand-raiser in each and let both run simultaneously — the overlap is tolerable and the tails are still readable. You float, watching one then the other. Silence pass runs in the split circles too. Debrief back as one group. |

## Variations

**Coach the tail** — You call 'now' out loud for the first six players, cueing the exact moment to speak. Drop the calls for the rest.  
*Easier — removes the judgement so the body learns the timing first.*

**Two-hander tail** — Two players share the tail. First lands a flat line, second must answer inside the same fade before silence.  
*Harder — the window shrinks and the second player must listen to noise and partner at once.*

**Cold tail** — The room builds silence-tension instead of noise — held breath, stillness — and the player places the line as the tension thins.  
*Different emphasis — reads a room that is quiet rather than loud, which is where most scenes actually live.*

## Debrief

1. **When you landed it right, what told you the moment had come?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something sensory and specific — the noise thinned, individual voices dropped out, they stopped hearing a crowd and started hearing people. Not 'I just knew' and not a theory about comedy.

2. **Did the room ever want a line you didn't owe them?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* A player naming the pull to fill the peak, and choosing not to. Any answer that separates what the room was doing from what the player decided to do about it.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity. It is loud: warn the group before the first wave and tell anyone with sound sensitivity they can step back, cover their ears, or mouth the rumble silently — participation in the wave is not required to take a turn speaking. Check the adjoining room or corridor before you start. Anyone who prefers not to speak alone in front of the horseshoe can take the silence option for their turn; that is a full turn, not a pass.

## Why it works

The session skill is audience-energy management and the cue is that their energy is information, not instruction. A laugh gives you two different signals at once: its peak says 'they're with you', its fade says 'they're ready'. Players who read only the peak either talk over it or wait it out entirely. By stripping the exercise down to a manufactured wave and a deliberately dull line, you remove content from the problem — nothing about the line can succeed or fail on its own merits, so the only variable left is placement. The room's three signals give immediate, non-verbal, low-stakes feedback on that one variable, twelve to twenty times in two minutes. That repetition is what shifts the judgement from thinking to hearing.
