# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Three Seeds

![Three Seeds](../img/advanced_w04__b3_3.jpg){ .infographic }

> Plant three images in a silent circle, then bring each one back twice — later each time — with only one voice allowed at a time.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 04 run-sheet](../week-04.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week four opens the Harold. Before anyone runs three beats of three scenes, they need to feel a return in their body: same material, moved forward, altered by what happened last time. This five minutes gives them that shape with no scene, no characters and no pressure to be funny. It feeds directly into the first Harold beat-two work later in the session, and gives you a shared phrase — 'later and truer' — to side-coach with for the rest of the day.

## What students actually learn

- A return is not a repeat. The image comes back changed and further along.
- Second beats carry the first beat's consequence — they stop treating beat two as a fresh start.
- Waiting for the gap is a legitimate move. Speaking over someone costs the group the beat.
- Three unrelated images can hold in the head at once without being planned into a story.

## Setup

Open floor, one circle, no chairs, no props, no paper. Everyone standing with space to take one pace forward. Know your three call-outs — 'Seed one', 'Seed two', 'Seed three' — and keep the order fixed. Have a clock visible to you only.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Circle up. Explain the shape: three seeds planted, each returning twice, later each time. Set the one-voice rule and the clash penalty. Take three seeds from three volunteers — one concrete image each, one breath. |
| 1 | Twenty seconds of stillness, eyes soft, all three images held, no sentence planned. Then call seeds one, two, three. One person per seed steps forward, gives one sentence set later in time, steps back. |
| 2 | Second pass. Same three seeds, same order. Each return lands later still and must acknowledge what the first return changed. New speaker each time. If two voices clash, both step back and that seed waits for the next call. |
| 1 | Silence for twenty seconds. Ask each person to pick, silently, the one seed they would carry into a Harold today. No sharing. Drop the circle and move straight into the next block. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| Collecting the seeds, if a volunteer starts building a scenario | *"Just the image. One breath."* |
| During the stillness before the first pass | *"Hold all three. Plan nothing."* |
| First return, if someone gives a sentence set in the present | *"Move it forward in time."* |
| Two people speak at once | *"Both back. The seed waits."* |
| Opening the second pass | *"Later still. And carry what changed."* |
| Second return, if someone ignores the first return | *"Answer what already happened."* |
| A seed sits unclaimed for four or five seconds | *"Let it sit. Someone will step."* |
| Just before you break the circle | *"Pick your seed. Keep it to yourself."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Sentences are short and land in a gap, not on top of each other. Each seed's second return is visibly further along than its first and refers to it. There are pauses of three or four seconds before someone steps forward, and nobody fills them nervously. Six different people speak. Nobody tries to be clever with the kettle.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Every return is a joke about the object, and the group laughs each time. | The group has read 'return' as 'callback' and is chasing the laugh. | Stop, restate: later in time, not funnier. Take one seed and demonstrate a flat, unfunny return yourself. Restart that pass. |
| Constant clashes — two or three people speaking together on nearly every call. | People are pre-writing sentences during the stillness and firing them on the call. | Enforce the penalty exactly once, visibly. Then add two seconds of your own silence after each call before anyone may move. |
| Second returns are just louder versions of the first, no time has passed. | They are matching the wording rather than the consequence. | Call 'What happened after that?' before the second pass and name one concrete first return as the thing to answer. |
| The same three or four confident people take all six returns. | No speaker rule was enforced, and the gaps close too fast for quieter people. | State plainly that anyone who spoke in pass one sits out pass two. Let the silence stretch. Do not rescue it. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Three seeds, two returns each, as written. There is time to add a third pass if the second finishes early — take thirty seconds from the closing silence. |
| **14–17** | One circle. Three seeds. Add the rule that nobody speaks twice, so six of the group return and the rest hold the images. Say aloud that watching is part of the exercise. |
| **18–20** | One circle, three seeds, but say up front that only six people will speak and that is by design. Cut the stillness to ten seconds and keep the pass timings tight so you finish on five. |

## Variations

**Seeds on the board** — Write the three images where everyone can see them instead of asking people to hold them in their head.  
*easier — removes the memory load so the group can concentrate on moving time forward*

**Four seeds, three returns** — Take four seeds and run three passes, each landing later than the last. Needs eight minutes, not five.  
*harder — more material to track and a genuine third beat, closer to a full Harold*

**Physical returns** — Each return is a single silent gesture or shape rather than a sentence, still placed later in time.  
*different emphasis — moves the skill from language into the body and stops anyone writing lines in their head*

## Debrief

1. **Which second return told you time had actually passed?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* They name a specific sentence and say what it implied had happened in between — 'the kettle was in a skip, so somebody cleared the house.'

2. **What was different about how you listened in the second pass?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something about tracking consequence instead of the object itself — waiting to hear what the first return had done before choosing what to say.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact. Nobody is touched and nobody is pulled forward. Stepping forward is the only way to volunteer, so standing still is a complete and silent opt-out — say that at the start. Speaking is not required; six voices out of twenty is a normal result. If someone's seed lands close to something personal, take a different image without comment. Keep the circle wide enough that people with mobility aids can take their pace forward, or let them mark the step with a raised hand instead.

## Why it works

Format literacy is knowing what a beat two owes to a beat one. The exercise strips a Harold return down to its only two obligations: it must be later, and it must know what already happened. With no character, no scene and no need to be funny, there is nothing left to hide behind — the group either moves the image forward or plainly does not. The one-voice rule adds the second half of format literacy: a return needs a gap to land in, so someone has to wait. Doing this in five silent minutes gives you a shared body memory to point back at every time a beat two restarts from zero later in the session.
