# 🧊 Ice-breaker — Open Tabs

![Open Tabs](../img/advanced_w04__b1_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone reads out the browser tabs they actually have open right now, one lap round the circle, no explaining.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 04 run-sheet](../week-04.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 4 opens the Harold. Before anyone builds a three-beat structure, they need to feel a return in their body: same order, same material, second pass, more truth. This ice-breaker is a two-beat form with no content demands, and it feeds directly into the first group game and the beat-two work later in the session.

## What students actually learn

- Returning to something is not repeating it — the second pass carries information the first could not.
- Their actual life is already specific enough to play with; they do not need to invent material.
- A lap of a circle has a rhythm, and keeping that rhythm is a group job, not a solo one.

## Setup

Circle, standing, no chairs. Phones and laptops allowed and encouraged — unusual for the room, so say it out loud. No props. Know your own three tabs in advance so you can model the pace. Anyone without a device pairs their eyes with a neighbour's screen or names a mental tab: something genuinely unfinished in their week.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Circle up. Explain: real tabs, skip anything private. Then everyone turns left and both talk at once, listing three tabs. Overlapping noise is the point. Call time hard. |
| 2 | Lap one. One tab each, title only, about seven seconds. Point at who starts and which direction. Cut context and apology on the spot. Keep the ball moving round. |
| 1 | Finish lap one if it is still running, then start lap two immediately. Same order, same tab, two words only: 'keeping it' or 'closing it'. |
| 1 | Finish lap two at speed. Then name two tabs from the room you would genuinely like opened. Say the cue. Move straight into the warm-up. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During the overlapping-talk minute | *"Both of you at once. Nobody has to be heard."* |
| First lap, someone starts explaining | *"Title only. Next."* |
| First lap, someone apologises for their tab | *"No apology. It's just a tab."* |
| First lap dragging | *"Seven seconds. Keep the lap alive."* |
| Starting lap two | *"Same order. Same tab. Two words."* |
| Lap two, someone adds a reason | *"Two words. Move."* |
| Just before the close | *"Beat two is the same game — later, and truer."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Lap one is fast and slightly boring in a good way — twenty ordinary titles, no performance. Lap two is faster and gets a laugh or two without anyone trying, because the verdicts are honest and clipped. The room is standing looser than it was ninety seconds ago and nobody has had to be clever.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lap one turns into short anecdotes and you are only halfway round at three minutes. | People assume a tab needs justifying, and the first two speakers set that norm. | Cut the third speaker mid-sentence with 'title only, next'. Nobody minds. The lap recovers within four people. |
| Two or three people say 'nothing interesting' or hide their screen. | They read the exercise as a judgement on their inner life. | Take one yourself out loud — something dull, a bank login, a recipe. Say 'boring is the correct answer' and move on quickly. |
| Lap two loses the original order and people name different tabs. | You did not fix the starting point and direction clearly enough at the top. | Stop, point at the first speaker, say 'same person, same tab, same way round', and restart the lap from them. |
| Someone reads out something genuinely private and the room goes quiet. | They over-committed to being open. | Take it lightly, say 'keeping it or closing it?', and move the lap on. Do not comment or thank them for sharing. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Both laps fit comfortably. Add a third lap if you finish early: same order, same tab, one word for how long it has been open ('today', 'weeks', 'years'). |
| **14–17** | One circle. Tighten lap one to five seconds and cut the second speaker who explains, not the third. Drop the closing 'two tabs I'd like opened' to one tab. |
| **18–20** | Split into two circles of nine or ten, side by side, running independently. Brief both at once, then start them together. You side-coach whichever circle is slowing. Skip the overlapping-talk minute; go straight to lap one. |

## Variations

**Fridge, not tabs** — Same two laps, but each person names one thing actually in their fridge, then 'eating it' or 'binning it'.  
*Easier — no device needed, and the material is even lower-stakes for anyone anxious about their screen.*

**Someone else's verdict** — On lap two, the person to your left delivers your 'keeping it' or 'closing it' for you.  
*Harder — you have to hold another person's tab in memory through a whole lap, and accept their call without defending.*

**Third beat** — Add a third lap: same order, same tab, one sentence starting 'and by Friday…'.  
*Shifts emphasis from return to escalation — the same material carried forward in time, which is closer to a Harold's third beat.*

## Debrief

1. **What was different about the second lap?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something like 'it was faster', 'I already knew what was coming', or 'it was funnier because we'd heard the first one'. Anything that names the second pass as carrying the first. Move on as soon as one person says it.

2. **Did anyone want to explain their tab, and what happened when you couldn't?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* 'It was a relief' or 'the title was enough'. You want them noticing that context was unnecessary, not that it was forbidden. One or two answers is plenty.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact. No touching phones other than your own. Say clearly at the top that anything private is skipped, no reason given, and that 'skip' is a complete answer — then honour it instantly the first time someone uses it. Nobody's screen is shown to anyone. If someone has no device or an empty browser, they name something unfinished in their week instead. Standing is optional; chairs work.

## Why it works

Format literacy means recognising a structure while you are inside it. This gives them a two-beat structure that costs nothing to perform: lap one establishes material and order, lap two returns to exactly that material and order and adds one new piece of information. The return is felt, not explained — they experience the second beat being funnier and truer than the first before anyone says the word 'Harold'. Because the content is real and unchosen, nobody spends attention on invention, so all the attention goes to the shape.
