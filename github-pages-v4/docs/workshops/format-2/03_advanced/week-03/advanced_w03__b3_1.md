# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Drop the Bucket

![Drop the Bucket](../img/advanced_w03__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room jogs on the spot, and on each call everyone freezes into a physical image thrown up by a single word.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 03 run-sheet](../week-03.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 3 is openings. Before they build a group opening that has to yield material, they need to feel material arriving from a single word without anyone deciding anything. This runs first, cold, and feeds the opening forms later in the session.

## What students actually learn

- A word produces an image in the body faster than in the head, and the body's version is usually more usable.
- Association beats representation — 'what it reminds me of' gives more to play than 'what it looks like'.
- Other people's images are raw stock, not competition. Copying and enlarging is a legitimate move.

## Setup

Clear floor, everyone standing, arm's length apart. No chairs, no props. Have twelve to eighteen words ready on a slip in your hand — concrete nouns for round one (kettle, harbour, ladder, wedding, snow, bruise), broader ones for rounds two and three (Sunday, debt, glass, mother, engine, gold). No music needed; your voice carries the rhythm.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Set the space. Everyone jogs on the spot. Explain the rule — word plus 'freeze', three seconds to shape, hold two, jog on. Demo one yourself, low and committed. |
| 1 | Round one. Six words, literal shapes allowed. Five seconds of jogging between each. Call for lower and higher shapes, not hands. |
| 1 | Round two. Six words, no literal object. The shape comes from what the word reminds them of. Same rhythm, no pause to explain. |
| 1 | Round three. Six words. After each freeze call 'look' — they break, copy the nearest shape, make it one size bigger, then jog. |
| 1 | One last word. Freeze and hold five seconds in silence, everyone looking around. Shake out. Say the cue and move straight on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During the first jog, before any word | *"Eyes up. Off the floor."* |
| First freeze looks like polite hand gestures | *"Lower. Get a knee down."* |
| Round one, third or fourth word | *"Whole body. Face too."* |
| Opening round two | *"Not the thing. What it reminds you of."* |
| Someone stalls, scanning for a good idea | *"First shape. Don't edit."* |
| Round three, immediately after each freeze | *"Look. Copy. Bigger."* |
| The final held freeze | *"Hold it. Look around."* |
| Straight after the shake-out | *"That was the well. Every one of those was full."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Breathing hard, floor being used, shapes at three different heights. Freezes land inside the three seconds without visible deliberation. By round two the shapes stop being illustrations and start being odd. In round three, the copies come out bigger and slightly wrong, and nobody minds. The final held image gets a laugh of recognition, not a joke.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone freezing at chest height with a small hand gesture. | They are miming the word rather than becoming a shape, and nobody wants to be the first on the floor. | Call 'lower' and drop to the ground yourself mid-round. Once one person is on the floor the height range opens. |
| A visible pause before each freeze while people think. | They are searching for a good answer instead of taking the first one. | Speed up. Cut the jog to three seconds and call the next word before they recover. Say 'first shape, don't edit'. |
| Round two collapses back into literal shapes. | The instruction was heard but not felt; association is harder under time pressure. | Use vaguer words — Sunday, debt, gold. A word with no obvious object forces the association. |
| Round three copies are shrinking rather than growing. | People are being careful with someone else's shape. | Call 'bigger' twice, hard, and name a size — 'twice that'. Enlarge one person's shape yourself as the example. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | Run as written, one group. Space is plentiful, so push for travelling shapes — allow one step out of the spot on the freeze. Keep six words per round. |
| **14–17** | Run as written, one group. Tighten spacing to arm's length and forbid travelling on the freeze so nobody collides. Five words per round if the room is small, to protect the timing. |
| **18–20** | Split into two half-rooms on opposite sides, both jogging, both freezing on your single call. Cut to five words per round. In round three, they copy within their own half only. Nobody sits out. |

## Variations

**Sound with it** — On each freeze, they add one sound — not a word, a noise the shape makes.  
*easier — the sound gives an escape route when the body stalls, and unsticks people who overthink shapes*

**Hold and name** — On the final freeze of each round, tap three people. Each says one word their own shape suggests. Those words become the next round's calls.  
*harder — closes the loop from image back to language, which is the actual move an opening has to make*

**Pairs on the freeze** — On 'freeze', they find the nearest person and make one shape together, connected but not touching unless both nod.  
*different emphasis — shifts from personal association to joint image-making, which is what group openings run on*

## Debrief

1. **Which round gave you shapes you'd actually want to use in a scene?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone names round two or three, and says the shape surprised them or came from nowhere — rather than praising a shape they planned.

2. **What happened when you had to copy somebody else's shape?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Being inside another person's image gave them an idea they wouldn't have had — or that making it bigger changed what it meant.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    Jogging on the spot is optional — offer marching, stepping side to side, or seated arm movement, and say so before you start, not after. Shapes can be made at any height; nobody has to go to the floor. No contact in the main version; the pairs variation requires a nod from both people and 'connected, not touching' as the default. Watch for knees and backs on low shapes — say 'you choose your own low'. Anyone who needs to step out can stand at the edge and still call shapes with their arms.

## Why it works

Format literacy starts with knowing what an opening is for. An opening produces raw material — images, associations, phrases — that the rest of the piece draws on. This activity strips that down to its smallest unit: one word in, one image out, no time to judge. The three-second limit blocks the editing brain, so what surfaces is genuine association rather than a chosen answer. Round two removes the literal option, which is the same constraint a good opening applies. Round three teaches that another person's offer is stock you can draw from, which is the mechanism every group opening depends on. By the time they build one, they have felt the well fill.
