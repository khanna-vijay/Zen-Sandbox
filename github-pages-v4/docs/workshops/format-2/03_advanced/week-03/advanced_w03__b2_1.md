# 😄 Laughter Blast — The Kettle

![The Kettle](../img/advanced_w03__b2_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room boils like a kettle, from a low hum to a shrieking whistle, then blows steam and does it again.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 03 run-sheet](../week-03.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 3 is openings. Before they build a well of material, the room has to be able to act as one body without negotiating. This is the physical version of that — a single unison object, no content, no cleverness. It comes first in the session and feeds straight into the opening forms that follow, where the same instinct (join the thing already happening, at full commitment) is the whole skill.

## What students actually learn

- Their voice can go louder than they thought without it being a performance choice.
- Joining a group sound is faster and simpler than deciding what to contribute.
- The body sets the pitch — crouching low and rising is doing the work, not the throat.

## Setup

Clear floor space so everyone can crouch and rise with arms free. Loose circle or scattered — no chairs. Check the room next door is not in a quiet session, and warn reception you are about to be loud. No props, no music.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Explain in three sentences: we are one kettle, low hum at the bottom, whistle at the top, steam out. Demonstrate the full arc yourself at full volume. Do not take questions. |
| 1 | Round one, slow. Everyone crouches, hums low. Count eight slow beats as the room rises. Whistle five seconds at the top. Flop forward, hiss the steam out. |
| 1 | Round two, double speed. Four beats up, whistle, hiss. Run it three times back to back with no gap between them. |
| 1 | Round three, boil-over. Rise, whistle, then shake the whole body while still whistling. Hold it. Call 'steam' after roughly fifteen seconds and let them collapse. |
| 1 | Split the room in half. Half boil the arc, half hiss underneath. Swap at fifteen seconds. Both halves whistle together to end. Then one question, three answers, stop. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| As they crouch for round one | *"Lower. Get under it."* |
| During the eight-beat rise | *"Let the legs pull the pitch up."* |
| At the top of any round | *"Whistle, don't scream. Thin and high."* |
| On the flop forward | *"All the air out. Empty."* |
| Round two, between repeats | *"Straight back down. No thinking."* |
| During the boil-over shake | *"Shake the sound, not just the arms."* |
| When one half hisses under the other | *"Steam is steady. Don't chase them."* |
| Final unison whistle | *"Together. One kettle."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The rise is unmistakably collective — you hear one pitch climbing, not fifteen. Everyone is red-faced and slightly out of breath by round three. Someone laughs on the flop-forward. Nobody is watching to see what others are doing before committing.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Polite mid-volume humming, nobody near a whistle at the top. | They are self-monitoring. Nobody wants to be the loudest in the room. | Be visibly louder and uglier than all of them. Say 'I need you above me' and run the round again immediately. |
| The rise is ragged — some at the top while others are still low. | They are listening to the room instead of following your count. | Count out loud and clap the beats. Say 'my count, not each other'. Regularity fixes it inside one round. |
| A few people standing still and mouthing it. | Vocal self-consciousness, or a genuine throat or knee issue they haven't mentioned. | Don't single anyone out. Offer the seated version aloud to the whole room: hands rise instead of body, hiss instead of whistle. |
| Coughing or rasping voices after round three. | They are pushing the shriek from the throat rather than sending it high and thin. | Stop, say 'thin and high, not loud and low', demonstrate the difference in two seconds, and drop the boil-over hold to ten seconds. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | Run it as a single circle. Give round two four repeats instead of three — a small room recovers faster and needs the extra push to get loud. |
| **14–17** | Single circle, exactly as written. Three repeats in round two. Split into two halves of roughly equal size for the last round. |
| **18–20** | Single group, but stand them in two concentric rings so nobody is more than a couple of metres from you. Cut round two to two repeats; a room this size takes longer to settle between arcs. |

## Variations

**Seated kettle** — Everyone stays in a chair. The rise is hands from the floor to overhead. Hum to a high hum instead of a whistle. Same counts, same steam.  
*Easier — removes the crouch and the vocal ceiling, keeps the unison.*

**No count** — Drop the beats entirely. The room has to find the rise together, whistle together and steam together with no one leading. Run it three times.  
*Harder — forces genuine ensemble listening rather than following an external pulse.*

**Two kettles** — Split the room permanently into two kettles on opposite walls. They boil alternately, each starting the moment the other steams out, six swaps without a break.  
*Different emphasis — shifts from unison to handover timing, which is what openings actually need next.*

## Debrief

1. **What happened to your attention at the top of the whistle?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something like 'it went away' or 'I stopped checking on myself'. They noticed that full commitment removed the self-monitoring, rather than describing whether it was fun.

2. **Who was setting the pitch?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* They don't name a person. They say it came from the group, or from their own legs, or they genuinely don't know — which is the answer you want before the openings work.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity. Say before you start: shrieking is optional, hissing is a full substitute, and anyone who prefers to stay seated should do so with no explanation needed. Warn about breath — tell them to breathe normally between rounds and sit down immediately if they feel light-headed. Anyone with a voice problem, a cold or recent vocal strain hums only. Watch knees on the crouch: hands can rise from a standing position instead. Keep the boil-over hold to fifteen seconds maximum; longer and throats start to go.

## Why it works

Format literacy starts with the body knowing that an opening is a collective object, not a queue of individual offers. The kettle gives the room one shape with no content in it — nothing to invent, nothing to be clever about — so the only variable left is commitment. Because pitch is tied to physical height, the group synchronises through the body rather than through negotiation, which is exactly how a good opening synchronises. By the time they sit down for the openings work, they have already had the experience of a room becoming one thing, and the cue — the opening is the well — has a physical referent rather than being an idea.
