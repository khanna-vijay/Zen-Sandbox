# 😄 Laughter Blast — Bubble Wrap Floor

![Bubble Wrap Floor](../img/advanced_w08__b2_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room becomes a sheet of bubble wrap and pops itself for five minutes.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 08 run-sheet](../week-08.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 8 is the public set. They arrive carrying nerves about being watched. This blast puts everyone in the same loud, undignified state at once, so no one is the exposed one. It feeds directly into the presence and clarity work: the frenzy-to-FLAT drops rehearse committing fully and then stopping clean, which is exactly what a set opening and a scene edit ask of them.

## What students actually learn

- Their body warms before their head does, and the nerves quieten as a side effect rather than a goal.
- Full volume is available to them without anyone judging it, because everyone is at full volume simultaneously.
- Stopping dead is as much a performance choice as going big — and the stop only reads if the going-big was real.

## Setup

Clear floor space so everyone can crouch and spring without touching. No chairs in the middle. You stand inside the group, not in front of it. Nothing to hand out. Check the room below or next door if the venue is shared — warn them, or move the frenzy sections down in volume.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Tell them the floor is bubble wrap and so are they. Demonstrate six pops yourself — crouch small, spring, loud wet "POP!". Badly and loudly. Then wave everyone in. |
| 1 | Everyone pops at their own speed, continuously. No pattern, no watching. Keep your own popping going the whole minute so no one person is the loudest in the room. |
| 1 | Stamp one foot, call a direction. Pops travel as a wave, each person popping as it reaches them. Send five or six waves, each one faster than the last. |
| 1 | One giant bubble. Everyone crouches low, inhales together for five slow seconds, then rises and releases one enormous drawn-out "POOOOP". Three rounds, sillier each time. |
| 1 | Fifteen seconds of all-out frenzy, then call "FLAT" — total stillness and silence, held three seconds. Repeat once. Then one question, thirty seconds of answers, and straight on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During your own demo, before anyone joins | *"Watch how bad this looks. Copy the badness."* |
| Ten seconds into the free popping, if it's thin | *"Wetter. From the belly, not the throat."* |
| Setting up the first wave | *"It reaches you, you pop. Don't wait to be sure."* |
| Between waves three and four | *"Faster. Let it arrive before you're ready."* |
| On the inhale of the giant bubble | *"Fill up. Five seconds. Don't leak."* |
| Starting the frenzy | *"Everything you've got. Now."* |
| The instant you call FLAT | *"Freeze. Nothing moves. Hold it."* |
| Coming out of the second FLAT | *"That's the set. Big, then clean. Keep it."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The room is loud enough that you have to shout the calls. People are red-faced and grinning at nobody in particular. Waves arrive as a ragged line, not a tidy one. On "FLAT" the noise stops inside half a second and the silence is genuinely still — that contrast is the thing you're after. Nobody is watching to see whether they're doing it right.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| A polite "pop" from the mouth only, arms folded, feet barely leaving the floor. | You demonstrated too competently, or you stopped popping to observe them. | Get back in and be visibly worse than them. Keep your own pops going without a break for the full free-popping minute. |
| The wave becomes a neat sequential ripple, people carefully waiting their turn. | They've turned it into a task with a right answer. | Call "overlap it" and send two waves from opposite sides at once. Ragged is correct. |
| On "FLAT", the noise trails off over three or four seconds with giggles. | The frenzy wasn't full, so there's nothing to cut. | Say "the drop only works if the noise was real" and run one more pair. Cut it hard yourself with a raised hand. |
| Two or three people at the edge of the room, half-participating, looking at the floor. | Volume feels exposing to them, or their knees don't want the springing. | Call out that hand-pops and seated pops count fully. Don't single anyone out — offer it to the whole room mid-round. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | Run as written. Waves cross the room quickly, so send six or seven rather than five. Keep the group loose in the middle of the floor rather than spread to the walls, so the noise banks up. |
| **14–17** | Run as written. Five or six waves is enough. Stand inside the group rather than at the edge so your calls reach the far side; raise a hand as well as your voice for "FLAT". |
| **18–20** | Split for the waves only: two halves facing each other, send the wave across the gap and back so it travels twice. Everything else stays whole-group. Use a raised hand plus a shouted "FLAT" — voice alone won't carry. |

## Variations

**Hand pops** — Everyone pops with hands only — a clap-and-vocal at chest height, no crouching or jumping.  
*Easier. Use it for tired rooms, low ceilings, shared buildings, or when several people have knees that object.*

**Named waves** — Instead of you calling directions, each person who is hit by a wave can redirect it by stamping and calling a new direction.  
*Harder. Adds a live decision under noise, which is much closer to what a set demands.*

**Pop and word** — On each pop, attach a single word — anything, no theme, no cleverness. During the frenzy, words become babble.  
*Different emphasis. Shifts from body warm-up towards voice and the willingness to speak before choosing.*

## Debrief

1. **What's different in your body now compared to five minutes ago?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Concrete and physical — warmer, out of breath, shoulders lower, less aware of being watched. Not analysis. If someone says "less nervous", take it and move on.

2. **What did the FLAT feel like?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone names the contrast — that stopping felt good, or landed harder because the noise before it was total. Thirty seconds. Don't chase it further.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity. Tell people to keep an arm's length so nobody springs into anybody. State up front that hand-pops or seated pops count fully — say it as a normal option, not a concession. Warn that volume gets high; anyone sensitive to noise can pop quietly or step to the edge and stay in the room. Check for a hard floor and low ceilings before asking for jumps. If the building is shared, run the hand-pops variation for the frenzy sections.

## Why it works

Stage presence fails when attention turns inward — the performer monitors themselves and shrinks. This blast makes self-monitoring impossible: everyone is loud at the same instant, so there is no audience inside the room and nothing to compare yourself against. Physical exertion also drops the shoulders and opens the breath, which is the mechanical basis of being clear on stage. The frenzy-to-FLAT pairs then train the specific thing a set needs — full commitment followed by a clean stop — in a context with no content to get wrong. They serve the piece by arriving warm rather than careful.
