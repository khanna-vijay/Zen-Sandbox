# Week 01 — Re-contract & Group Mind

> *The team's collective attention is a single instrument — play it, don't compete with it.*

| Course | Week | Domain | Focus | Stage | Length | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Piece — Long-Form & Audience | 1/8 | D4 | `D4.S1` — Peripheral Awareness | Competent → Proficient | 180 min | 10–20 |

!!! warning "Layer 0 — before anything else"
    Anyone may say **"Cut"** at any moment, for any reason, with no explanation owed.
    Everything here is opt-out-able: you can step out, sit down, or watch. The rule of
    consent overrides the rule of agreement — *Yes, And* never means *yes, you must*.

!!! note "Builds on"
    The Engine ended with ensemble support inside short scenes. Now the unit of work is the whole stage.

## 🎬 The arc of this session

They arrive as a group of competent scene players who have spent a whole course learning to make good two-person scenes, and who still think of the stage as a place they take turns in. Across the day the unit of work moves from the scene to the stage picture: they learn to feel where everyone is without checking, to move as a shared rhythm, and to withhold an offer when the piece does not need one. They leave knowing that the ensemble's attention is one instrument, and knowing which long-form they are building towards.

!!! tip "Watch for"
    Competent players protect their competence. Watch for the ones who fill every silence with a good offer — they are not being greedy, they are avoiding the discomfort of not knowing what the group will do. Name that early and kindly, or the whole day turns into a series of well-made two-handers.

## 🎒 Before you start

- Clear the whole floor. You need a playing space wide enough for 20 people to move at once without contact, plus a seated audience edge on one side only.
- Chairs in a shallow arc on one side, not a full circle — everything today is oriented to a front.
- Whiteboard or flipchart. Write the cue up before anyone arrives: "Know where everyone is without looking."
- Have the Layer 0 contract points ready to say aloud in under three minutes: consent, opt-out, the tap-out word, no touch without asking, injuries and access needs told to you privately before we start.
- Skim last course's final notes. Have two names in mind of players who dominate stage time and two who disappear — you will need them for the Drill B pairings.
- Decide before class which showcase form you are steering towards (10-minute Montage) and be able to name the second option in one sentence.

## 1. 🧊 Ice-breaker (5 min)

*"Stand in a rough circle. Phone out, last photo you took, no scrolling to find a better one. Thirty seconds each, and we are not judging your camera roll."*

**Why here:** Low-stakes talking that gets them looking at each other's actual lives rather than performing readiness, and it re-forms the group after a course break.

### Last Photo

> Everyone describes the last photo on their phone, first in pairs, then in five words to the room.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Pair everyone up fast. With an odd number, make one three. Announce the subject: the last photo you actually took on your phone.
2. Set two rules: no screens shown, and no explaining why the photo is interesting. Describe what is in it, out loud.
3. Give A thirty seconds to describe their photo. B listens and says nothing back. Call the swap out loud.
4. Give B thirty seconds. A listens in silence. In a three, the two partners split the thirty seconds between them.
5. Bring everyone back to one circle, standing. Each person names their own photo in five words or fewer, about six seconds each.
6. Run it round the ring with no commentary, no laughter break, no follow-up questions between people. Keep the pulse steady.
7. Close with a show of hands: taken today; has a person in it; a photo of a screen or a receipt. Move straight on.

## 2. 😄 Laughter Blast (5 min)

*"On your feet. Someone does a tiny action, we all replay it. Bigger each time. No commentary."*

**Why here:** Gets bodies moving and permission levels up fast, and starts the day with copying rather than inventing — which is exactly the muscle for peripheral work.

### Action Replay

> The whole room celebrates a goal that never happened, then replays the celebration in slow motion.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Spread the room. Everyone needs arms-wide clearance without touching a neighbour. Check the corners.
2. Demonstrate alone: a goal has just gone in. Arms up, full roar, mimed knee slide on the spot. Commit fully so nobody has to out-do you.
3. Count down from three. Everyone celebrates at once, full volume, five seconds. Call 'FREEZE'. Run it three times total. Tell them not to watch each other.
4. Call 'REPLAY'. Same celebration in slow motion, roar dropped to a low groan. Groan loudest yourself. Freeze at the end. Run it twice.
5. Call 'FAST FORWARD'. Same celebration at double speed, voices high and squeaky, five seconds. Freeze. Run it three times, tighter each go.
6. Alternate on your call — 'SLOW', 'FAST', 'SLOW', 'FAST' — about every eight seconds. Keep switching until the room is wheezing.
7. Call 'FINAL WHISTLE'. Shake out arms and legs. Ask the one debrief question, take three answers, move straight on.

## 3. 🔥 Warm-up cluster (15 min)

*"Four warm-ups, back to back, about four minutes each. They are all the same warm-up wearing different coats. Notice what they have in common."*

**Why here:** Four short pieces that install the physical vocabulary of the whole day: soft gaze, audible group rhythm, triangulation, and holding a front.

### Soft Eyes, Loud Feet

> A bouncing circle passes impulses it can only catch out of the corner of the eye, so the room wakes up wide instead of narrow.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Stand everyone in one circle, arm's length apart. Start a gentle bounce on the spot — heels light, knees soft. Keep bouncing until you call stop.
2. Tell them to soften the gaze onto a point above the heads opposite. Heads stay still. They take in the whole circle with peripheral vision, not by scanning.
3. Teach the impulse: arms shoot up, then drop with a loud 'HUP'. Send one round the circle. Receivers copy it the instant they catch it sideways.
4. Open the direction. The impulse can now jump to anyone — across, back, adjacent. One live at a time. Heads still, feet still bouncing.
5. Add a second impulse from the far side. Two live at once. If both land on one person, they send both straight back out in different directions.
6. Stop calling. Anyone may launch a fresh impulse whenever the room feels thin. Aim for three live at once with nobody turning their head.
7. Call stop. Feet still, gaze still soft. Three slow breaths. Ask them to notice how much of the circle they can still feel.

### Third Point

> Each person silently keeps an equal distance from two others, so the whole room becomes one slow-moving system nobody is steering.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact incidental` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Clear the floor. Spread everyone out, no clumps, arms' length minimum. Tell them this runs in silence from now until you call the debrief.
2. Tell each person to silently pick two others. No pointing, no telling, no checking. Everyone keeps their pair secret.
3. Give the job: keep yourself an equal distance from both of your people, so the three of you form an even triangle. Move slowly. Stay quiet.
4. Call go. Let the room churn. When it stops moving and settles, call freeze.
5. Frozen, have everyone point one hand at each of their two. Let them look at the web for a few seconds. Drop arms.
6. Reset. New two people. This round nobody may stop moving. Soft eyes, no staring, no head-swivelling — track them at the edge of your vision.
7. While it runs, name three people as anchors. They root to the spot. Everyone else keeps solving. Tell them the system will not settle now.
8. Call freeze. Hold ten seconds of stillness. Ask one question where they stand, then move on.

### Eyes Front

> A scattered room holds a fixed number of raised hands with heads locked forward, so people track each other by edge of vision only.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Scatter the room. Everyone stands facing the same wall, feet planted, at least an arm's length from the nearest person.
2. Give the rule: eyes stay on a fixed mark on that wall, head still, feet still. You read the room from the edge of your vision only.
3. Say a target number out loud — start at three. At every moment exactly that many hands must be up. No talking, no signals, no head turns.
4. Run it: raise, hold, lower. When the count is wrong, say the wrong number out loud and let the room correct without stopping.
5. Round two, same target. Add a cap — no hand stays up longer than about five seconds. Traffic increases; keep calling wrong counts as they happen.
6. Round three: raise the target by one and swap the hand for a slow half-crouch. Lower-body signals are harder to catch sideways, so attention has to widen.
7. Call stop. Hands down, eyes still front. Ask one question, take two answers, move straight into the next block.

## 4. 🧠 Theory — Peripheral Awareness (20 min)

![Peripheral Awareness](../../../assets/infographics/D4.S1.jpg){ .infographic }

- **The big idea:** A group's attention is a single instrument. When everyone points their attention at their own idea, the piece sounds like twelve people tuning. When attention is shared, one person can move and the whole stage changes shape.
- **Where you are on the path:** Competent looks like: good scenes, clean initiations, players who support when asked. Proficient looks like: a player who steps out and finds they have arrived at exactly the moment the stage needed someone, without having planned it. Today you are building the second thing on top of the first.
- **The one cue to coach:** *“Know where everyone is without looking.”*

**The three beats**

1. **Say it.** Say: "Until now the unit of work was the scene. From today the unit of work is the stage. That means two new jobs. One — you always know roughly where everyone is, without turning to look. Two — you can tell whether the piece needs another thing added to it, and most of the time it does not." Then say the cue and leave it on the board all day.
2. **Show it.** Take four volunteers. Stand them in a loose square, all facing front, and ask them to close their eyes. Move one of them three steps sideways, silently. Eyes open, no talking — ask the other three to rebalance the picture. They will. Point at it: "Nobody discussed that. That is the instrument. It already works. We are just going to make it louder."
3. **Try it.** Everyone up, walking the space. On your clap they freeze. Without looking around, each player points at the person they believe is nearest them. Look. Two rounds. Third round: freeze and, without looking, everyone moves one step to make the room more evenly spread. Look. Sit down.

!!! warning "The misunderstanding that always comes up"
    They hear "awareness" and think it means monitoring — constant checking, heads swivelling, a nervous scan. It is the opposite. Say it plainly: soft eyes, front-facing, and a general felt sense of the room. If they are looking, they have already stopped playing. The other version of the misunderstanding: "don't add" heard as "do nothing". Correct it as withholding, which is an active choice with a reason behind it.

## 5. 🎲 Drill A — isolation (20 min)

*"No scenes for the next twenty minutes. No words either. We are just going to be a place together and see if the place has a mood."*

**Why here:** Isolates peripheral awareness with no scene, no dialogue and no character, so failure costs nothing and the sensation is unmistakable.

### The Living Atmosphere

> Transform the physical space into a responsive, non-verbal mirror of the scene's emotional heartbeat.

`Players 5–99` · `~20 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Split the room: two Scene Actors, everyone else the Atmosphere. Take one suggestion from the floor — a location or a relationship. Nothing more.
2. Send the Atmosphere in first. Give them thirty seconds to build the place silently with bodies alone: walls, furniture, doors, weather. No talking.
3. Cue the Atmosphere to add sound — hum, drip, traffic, wind. Keep it low. It is a bed, not a solo.
4. Bring the two Scene Actors in. They play a grounded scene and use what the Atmosphere built. Open the door that exists. Sit on the chair that exists.
5. Tell the Atmosphere to track feeling, not words. When tension rises, close in, lower, tighten the sound. When it eases, open out and soften.
6. Allow the Atmosphere to add small obstacles — a jammed window, a rising draught — never anything that blocks or touches an actor.
7. Tell the Scene Actors to let the room land on them. A shrinking wall changes their posture, their volume, their next line.
8. End the scene when it resolves, or let the Atmosphere call it: one unified shift — all drop, all rise, all go silent — and you clap the edit.

## 6. 🎲 Drill B — under load (20 min)

*"Same instrument, more weight on it. Now someone is shaping the group in real time and the rest of you have to follow without staring at them."*

**Why here:** Same skill, now under load — a leader must be tracked and followed while the group also tracks itself, which is where the dominators and the disappearers reveal themselves.

### The Collective Conductor

> Shape an evolving, non-verbal sound and movement piece through fluid, unspoken leadership and subtle transformation.

`Players 5–99` · `~20 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Stand the whole group in a loose circle, arms' length apart. Tell them to soften focus — look at the middle distance, hold everyone else in peripheral vision.
2. Ask one person to start a simple repeating sound or movement: a hum, a sway, a foot tap. Keep it plain and loopable.
3. Have everyone join it exactly — same pitch, same tempo, same physical quality — until the room is one sound and one shape.
4. Tell them any player may change the state at any time, without asking, by altering one variable only: pitch, tempo, volume, size, or tension.
5. Forbid brand-new actions. Every change is a modification of what is already running, so the group can follow it.
6. Ban speech and direct eye contact. The group reads the change peripherally and adopts it until everyone matches again.
7. Tell the changer to settle back into the steady state once the group has caught up, leaving the next change open to anyone.
8. End when the group senses resolution together: fade sound and movement out into a shared still, silent picture.

## 7. 🎯 Scene Lab (35 min)

*"Now it means something. Each picture is a page. The page has to be readable from the back row before anyone speaks."*

**Why here:** First time today the awareness carries actual content; the form gives them a hard frame so the whole stage stays visible and nobody hides in a two-hander.

### Pop-Up Storybook

> Bring a physical storybook to life by popping up as characters, scenery, and moving parts.

`Players 3–99` · `~35 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Agree the contact rule first: bodies may lean, brace or touch shoulders and backs only, always after eye contact. Anyone can decline by staying a hand's width apart. No lifting.
2. Pick one Narrator. Everyone else spreads out low on the floor, flat or crouched, neutral, ready to rise.
3. Have the Narrator announce the book's title to the audience and mime opening the front cover.
4. Narrator describes page one — the place, the objects in it, the people. Keep it concrete and name three or four elements.
5. As each element is named, players pop up fast and safely and become it with their bodies: the clock, the bench, the clockmaker.
6. Tell players to scan before rising. One body per element unless the element is big. Fill gaps rather than doubling up.
7. Narrator mimes a paper mechanism — pulls a tab, turns a wheel — and says what moves. Those players animate immediately and repeat the motion until told otherwise.
8. Once the page is built, the Narrator may let characters speak for a few lines. Keep it short; the picture is the point.
9. Narrator says "And we turn the page…" and mimes the turn. Everyone drops instantly back to flat neutral. Build the next page. Repeat to a climax, then close the book.

## 8. 🏟️ Showcase round (40 min)

*"Half of you sit and watch, half of you play. This is the first thing today with an audience, which means the picture now has a reader."*

**Why here:** Ends the day on the highest-stakes application — reading and shaping meaning across the whole group under an audience eye — and gives them a first taste of the showcase's demands.

### Subtext Sculptors

> Make the unspoken visible as physical shadows that can step forward and transform the narrative.

`Players 5–12` · `~40 min` · `Complexity 4/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Mark two areas with tape or chairs: a main stage and a shadow zone running alongside it, visible to the audience and to the edge of a player's vision.
2. Send two or three players onto the main stage. Put three to five in the shadow zone. Everyone else watches from the front.
3. Give the stage players a relationship or a location. Tell them to play it straight — real people, clear want, small conflict, no jokes needed.
4. Tell the shadow players to watch for what is not being said. When one feels an unspoken thing, they step forward and hold it as shape, weight and movement.
5. Insist the shapes stay abstract. Archetypes, not extra characters. No dialogue, no mimicry of the players, no literal props.
6. Cap the shadow zone at two active bodies. Sculptors watch each other and melt back when the scene's emotional centre moves elsewhere.
7. Tell stage players to take the shapes in peripherally only. No looking across, no naming them. Let the shape change posture, tempo and tone.
8. Allow one activation per scene: a sculptor walks slowly onto the stage and holds eye contact. The stage players then make that truth explicit — a confession, a new figure, a turn.
9. Call the scene at the end of the round, rotate everyone, and run it again with a new suggestion.

**Side-coach:** *“"Withhold. If two people are already saying it, you saying it a third time makes it quieter, not louder."”*

## 9. 💭 Reflection & homework (10 min)

**Deepen your improv**
1. Describe one moment today when you were about to add something and did not. What told you to hold back, and what happened in the space instead?
2. When you were on stage and could not see the whole picture, what did you use instead of your eyes — sound, floor vibration, breathing, a sense of weight on one side? Which one is most reliable for you?

**Beyond the stage**
3. Think of a conversation this week where you spoke because the pause was uncomfortable rather than because you had something to say. What would the room have done with that silence if you had left it there?

??? note "🎓 Facilitator notes"

    **Timing risks**
    - The Layer 0 re-contract will eat the ice-breaker if you let it become a discussion. Say it, take access needs privately, move on — three minutes hard stop.
    - The warm-up cluster is four activities in fifteen minutes. That is under four minutes each including setup. Cut the fourth to two minutes rather than let the first two sprawl.
    - Showcase at 40 minutes with 18-20 people needs strict half-and-half swapping at the 20-minute mark, or the second group gets ten minutes and resents it.

    **Failure modes this week**
    - Head-swivelling. They will monitor rather than sense. Freeze the room and ask them to point at the nearest person with eyes shut — it resets the setting in twenty seconds.
    - Two strong players quietly turning every group piece into a two-hander with nine witnesses. Sidecoach the nine, not the two: "if you are watching, you are not in it."
    - Silence read as failure. The Living Atmosphere often looks dead for the first five minutes and players start adding out of panic. Tell them in advance it will feel dead and to hold anyway.
    - Over-conducting in Drill B, where the leader gives instructions instead of impulses. Swap the conductor early and often rather than correcting one person for ten minutes.

    **What good looks like by the end:** By the end of the showcase you should see at least three moments where the group changes shape or rhythm together with no visible cue and no one looking at anyone. You should also see someone start to step out, feel that the stage does not need them, and sit back down. Name that one out loud when it happens — it is the whole day in one move.

    **Carry into next week:** Note who withheld well and who could not stop adding; you will pair them deliberately next week. Keep the cue on the board every session of this course. Next week the group mind gets a shape — the same peripheral awareness applied to structure rather than picture — so tell them the Montage is the destination and give them one thing to notice in any long-form they watch before then: how often nobody is talking.
