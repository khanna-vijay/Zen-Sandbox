# 🎒 Homework — Week 06 · Pacing, Edits & the Shape of a Set

> Last session you edited scenes live and sat in silences longer than felt comfortable. This week you keep training both clocks: when to cut, and how long to let a pause hold.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Dance](../../../solo-practice/20_dance.md) | 8-10 minutes | Put on one track and move to it without choreographing. Notice where the music makes you want to stop and where it makes you want to push. Count how long you hold a still moment before you break it — most people break at about two seconds. Try holding to four. Notice the difference between a pause that has weight and one that has gone slack. Then notice the opposite: the point in a phrase where energy is still climbing. That climb is the edit point in a scene. Cut there, not at the top and not on the way down. By day four you should be able to name the moment you wanted to move a beat before you moved. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — The Back Row

Pick one conversation this week that is going well — a good one, with someone you like. Somewhere in the middle, while you are still enjoying it and still have things to say, stop. Do not announce it. Do not summarise. Ask one question, listen to the answer, and let the conversation end there or hand it to them for good. You will feel the pull to add one more story. Let that story go unsaid. Afterwards, write down two things: the thing you did not say, and how the other person seemed when you stopped. Once is enough for the week. You have failed this if you got to Sunday and every conversation ran until it flattened out or someone had to leave.  Do it early in the week so you are not hunting for it on Friday.  Keep the unsaid thing. You may not need it.  ,

## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a time you carried on past the point where you should have stopped — a story, a meeting, an argument, a night out. What told you to stop, and why did you override it?
2. Where in your ordinary week do you tolerate silence well, and where do you rush to fill it? Be specific about who you are with in each case.
3. Which is harder for you: ending something good early, or sitting through a pause that someone else needs? What does that suggest about how you'll edit scenes?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch fifteen minutes of anything with real conversation in it — a chat show, a podcast with video, a documentary interview. Time the longest silence you can find. Note what happens immediately before it and immediately after.

**Bring back:** One silence, its length in seconds, and one sentence on whether the person who broke it needed to.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The timed silence from the watch task, with its length in seconds.
- The sentence you did not say in your Back Row conversation.
- One note from Dance on where you felt the rise and whether you moved before or after it.
- Any journal line you are willing to read out — one sentence, no explanation needed.
