# 🎒 Homework — Week 01 · The Safety Container & the Circle

> Last session we built the container: the word "Cut", and the rule that nothing happens without a yes. This week's reps are about noticing your own edges before someone else has to find them.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Counting to One Hundred](../../../solo-practice/29_counting-to-one-hundred.md) | 5 minutes | Notice the moment you want to stop and the moment you make yourself carry on. That gap is the same one that shows up on stage when a scene goes somewhere you did not agree to. Log two things each day: the number you reached, and whether stopping was a decision or a drift. Notice whether you treat losing count as a failure. It is not part of the drill. If you find yourself pushing past discomfort to hit a target, stop early on purpose once this week and see how that feels. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — One Unplanned Thing a Day

Once a day, do something small you did not plan thirty seconds earlier. Take the other route home. Order the thing you never order. Say the sentence you would normally swallow. Answer a question honestly instead of politely. It must be small enough to cost you nothing and real enough that you notice doing it.

Set no theme in advance. If you find yourself planning tomorrow's unplanned thing, that one does not count.

Afterwards, write one line: what you did, and what you felt in the two seconds before. Two seconds before is the interesting part.

Seven days, seven lines. If you miss a day, write "missed" and carry on. Bring the list, including the misses.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Write about a time you wanted to say "stop" and did not. What made the word hard to reach? What would have made it easier?
2. Where in your week do you agree to things automatically? List three. Beside each, write what a real yes would have sounded like.
3. When someone else sets a boundary with you, what is your first internal reaction? Be honest, not generous.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Over the week, watch two conversations you are not part of — a café, a shop queue, a meeting, a family room. Watch for the moment someone declines something: an offer, a topic, a favour. Note how they did it, and what the other person did next.

**Bring back:** One sentence describing a refusal you saw, and one sentence on how it was received.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The seven-line spontaneity list, misses included
- Counting to One Hundred logs: number reached and how each session ended
- One observed refusal and how it landed
- One journal response you are willing to read aloud
