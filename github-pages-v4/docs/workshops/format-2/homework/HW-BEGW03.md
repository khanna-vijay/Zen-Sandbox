# 🎒 Homework — Week 03 · Yes, And — Receiving the Offer

> Last session the room practised taking what it was handed as true; this week's work is about the second half — adding exactly one thing, not five.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Character Interview](../../../solo-practice/06_character-interview.md) | 8-10 minutes | Notice the moment you're asked something you hadn't decided yet. Watch whether you stall, deny it, or accept it as already true and answer. Then count your additions: an accepted fact plus one new detail is enough. If you hear yourself giving three details, stop mid-sentence and let the next question do the work. Note which questions were easy to receive and which made you want to reach for a joke. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Their Version First

Once a day this week, someone will change a plan on you. A different route, a different restaurant, a rearranged meeting, a child who wants the other jumper. When it happens, go with their version. Do it fully, not grudgingly — no commentary, no scoreboard, no telling them afterwards what you gave up. Choose the smallest ones: things that cost you inconvenience rather than money or safety. You are allowed to decline anything genuinely unworkable, but say so plainly and pick a different moment that day. At bedtime, write down one line: what changed, and what you actually did. If you can't name one change you followed, you missed that day. Notice, by Friday, whether their version was worse, better, or simply different from the one you were holding onto.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe a conversation this week where you accepted what the other person said as true, and one where you quietly corrected them instead. What was the difference in how each ended?
2. When you add to something someone else started — a plan, a story, a piece of work — do you tend to add one thing or take it over? Where did you learn that?
3. Write about a time somebody accepted an idea of yours without amending it. How did it feel, and does that change how you want to receive other people's ideas?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch one real conversation you are not part of — a café, a bus, a family kitchen. Track a single exchange where one person offers something and the other either takes it or blocks it.

**Bring back:** Bring one sentence you actually heard: the offer, and the exact reply that either accepted or refused it. Be ready to say it aloud in class.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The overheard offer-and-reply sentence from the watch task
- One line from the Their Version First log — the change you followed that surprised you most
- A note on which Character Interview question was hardest to receive
- Any question about accepting an offer you found genuinely uncomfortable
