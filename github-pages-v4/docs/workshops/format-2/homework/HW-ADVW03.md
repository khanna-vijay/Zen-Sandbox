# 🎒 Homework — Week 03 · Openings — Filling the Well

> Last session we ran openings cold and then tried to build scenes off them; most groups reached for plot and left the material on the floor. This week's reps are about generating more than you can use, and noticing what you left behind.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Continuous Typing Exercise](../../../solo-practice/04_continuous-typing-exercise.md) | 10 minutes | Set a single word at the top of the page and type without stopping. Notice the moment you start steering — where you stop associating and begin composing a little story. Mark it with a slash and keep going. At the end, read back and count how many separate images, phrases and details you produced, not how good they were. That count is the well. Also notice what you circled back to twice without meaning to; in an opening, that repetition is the group's actual subject. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Reading a Room You Are Not Performing In

Three times this week, sit somewhere public for fifteen minutes with no task — a café, a bus station, a waiting room, a launderette. Do not read, scroll or talk. Do not try to be interesting to anyone there. Work out the mood of the room the way you would work out the weather: from evidence. Who sets the pace? Is anyone being watched? What is the volume, and who changed it last? Where would a stranger be least welcome to sit? Before you leave, name the room's temperature in one plain sentence — "tired but not unhappy", "everyone waiting for the same thing". Write it in your phone. You have failed the week if you only managed one sitting, or if you spent them on your phone.<br>Do not photograph anyone. Do not eavesdrop on purpose or record what you overhear.</br>

## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a group you belong to outside class — family, team, a WhatsApp thread. What is the shared material everyone draws on without explaining it? List five things. How long did it take that well to fill?
2. When you got stuck this week — in the typing drill or in a scene — what did you reach for first: a new idea, or something already said? What does that tell you about how you trust other people's offers?
3. Describe a time someone repeated something you said and made more of it than you had. How did that feel, and did you let them?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch the first eight minutes of any recorded long-form set, or the first eight minutes of a live show if you can get to one. Ignore the laughs. Track what the openers put into the room: names, places, phrases, physical images, relationships. Write each one down as it appears.

**Bring back:** Your list, plus one item from it that you can point to being used later in the set, and one item that was never touched again.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- Your typing pages from at least three days, with the steering-points marked.
- Your three one-sentence room temperatures from the off-stage practice.
- The eight-minute opening list, with the used item and the abandoned item circled.
- One suggestion word you would like the group to open on, written on paper and folded.
