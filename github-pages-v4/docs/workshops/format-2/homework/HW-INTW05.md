# 🎒 Homework — Week 05 · Character From the Outside In

> Last session the room built people out of posture and pace; this week keeps the body leading and lets the biography turn up late.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Body Parts](../../../solo-practice/16_body-parts.md) | 8-10 minutes | Notice how fast the feeling follows the shift. Lead with the chin for thirty seconds and check what mood arrives uninvited. Notice which leads you avoid — most people skip the pelvis and the belly. Notice whether you are inventing a backstory to justify the walk; if you are, drop it and keep walking. Notice the moment the body change stops being a costume and starts being a preference: what this person would order, refuse, sit on. |
| [Character Morning Routine](../../../solo-practice/18_character-morning-routine.md) | 10 minutes | Notice how the body handles small obstacles — a stiff tap, a lost sock, cold tea. Emotional fluidity shows up in recovery time. Notice how long this person stays annoyed before moving on, and try running the same routine with a faster and a slower recovery. Notice whether your voice changed without you deciding to. Notice the one physical detail you keep repeating; that is the character's spine, and you can bring it into a scene in one second. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Finished Badly

Pick one small job you have been putting off because you cannot do it properly. The reply you owe. The shelf. The photos. The call to your aunt. Do it this week at about sixty per cent, in one sitting, and then stop. Send the short unpolished email. Hang the shelf slightly off level. Sort the photos into two rough piles instead of twelve tidy ones. Set a timer if it helps, and let the timer end the job rather than your standards. Do not go back and improve it later in the week. Notice what happens in your chest when you leave it as it is, and notice how much of the dread was about the doing versus the judging. You can tell you have failed this practice if the job is still undone on Saturday, or if you fixed it.


## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe a person you know by how they move — the walk, the hands, the speed of turning their head. Write four lines without naming a single fact about their life. What do you now assume about them?
2. Think of a time your mood changed because your body changed first: after a long walk, after being cold, after carrying something heavy. What did you tell yourself the mood was about?
3. Where in ordinary life do you hold one emotional setting for far too long? What does it cost, and who notices before you do?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Spend fifteen minutes somewhere with people passing through — a station, a queue, a park bench. Pick one stranger and copy their posture in your own body, discreetly, while seated. Hold it for a minute.

**Bring back:** Bring the physical lead you copied — one specific thing, like 'weight on the heels, shoulders behind the hips' — and be ready to walk the room in it for thirty seconds.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The copied posture from the watch task, ready to demonstrate in thirty seconds
- One sentence on the job you finished badly and what it felt like to leave it
- The repeated physical detail your morning-routine character kept doing
- Any body lead from the drill that you kept avoiding
