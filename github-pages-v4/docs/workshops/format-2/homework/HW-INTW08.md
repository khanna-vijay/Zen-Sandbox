# 🎒 Homework — Week 08 · The Public Showcase — Match or Genre Night

> Last session you ran the showcase format on its feet; this week's reps are about reading the room and giving it what it needs.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Sound to Character](../../../solo-practice/12_sound-to-character.md) | 8 minutes | Notice how fast the character arrives once the sound leads. Then notice the size of it. Ask yourself, each time, whether that size would land in a room of eighty people or only for the three feet around you. Mark which characters you reach for out of habit — those are your bits. Keep going past them until you find two you have never used. Also notice your volume: a sound made at rehearsal level often dies on a stage, and a sound pushed too hard reads as panic rather than character. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Notice the Group Mind

Pick five rooms this week — a kitchen at breakfast, a meeting, a bus, a pub, a waiting area. Before you speak, stop at the threshold for a slow count of ten. Read the room. Is it tired, wired, wary, bored, mid-something? Then decide one thing the room seems to need: quiet, a question, someone to start, someone to stop. Do that one thing, or deliberately do nothing. Write the room, your read and your choice in your phone within the hour — one line each. At week's end you should have five entries. You have failed the practice if you have fewer than five, or if any entry records only what you did and not what you read first.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a time you kept doing something that was working for you while it stopped working for everyone else. What told you, and how long did you ignore it?
2. Where in your ordinary life do you serve the room well — and what makes that easier there than on stage?
3. Name one thing you do on stage purely because you enjoy it. What would the show lose if you cut it, and what would it gain?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch a live audience, not a performance. Comedy night, gig, church, school assembly, a talk at work — anywhere a group is being addressed. Spend the time watching the crowd rather than the stage. Note the exact moment the room's attention lifts and the exact moment it drops, and what the performer was doing at each.

**Bring back:** Two sentences on paper: one moment the room came with the performer, one moment it went away, and your guess at the cause of each.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- Your five threshold entries from the week's off-stage practice
- The two-sentence audience note from the watch task
- Two new characters found through Sound to Character, ready to play at showcase volume
- One bit of your own you are willing to cut from the showcase
