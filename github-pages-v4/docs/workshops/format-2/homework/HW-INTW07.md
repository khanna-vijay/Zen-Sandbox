# 🎒 Homework — Week 07 · Match Play — Short-Form Under Pressure

> Last session you played rounds against a clock; this week the reps are about hearing what the room is already giving you and adjusting before the round ends.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Scene with Emotional Shift](../../../solo-practice/21_scene-with-emotional-shift.md) | 8-10 minutes | Notice where you place the shift. Most people shift when they get bored, not when the scene has earned it. Say the beat out loud where the change lands and ask whether anything before it made it necessary. Then notice what you do in the two lines after the shift: do you commit to the new emotional temperature, or do you scramble back to the old one because it was safer? Under a clock you will feel the pull to push the shift early. Log which days you pushed. |
| [Scene with Emotional Shift](../../../solo-practice/21_scene-with-emotional-shift.md) | 5 minutes | Run it once more, but imagine an audience that is quiet rather than hostile. Notice the moment you start decorating lines to get a response. That decorating is begging. Cut it and see whether the scene survives without it — usually it does, and it plays cleaner. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Back the Person With the Momentum

Pick three group decisions this week where you would normally have an opinion. A meeting, dinner plans, what the family does on Saturday. In each one, spend the first ten minutes finding whoever already has momentum — the person the group keeps returning to, whose idea keeps getting picked up. Back them. Add to their idea out loud at least once. Ask a question that helps their idea get more specific. Do not offer your alternative, do not hedge, do not say "yes, but". After ten minutes you may steer if you still want to. Write down, straight after, whether you still wanted to. You have failed the practice if you steered in the first ten minutes, or if you could not name who had the momentum.


## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a time you kept pushing a joke, an idea or a story after the room had moved on. What told you to stop, and why did you ignore it?
2. When you walk into a room you did not choose — a waiting room, a family gathering, a work meeting — what do you read first: the loudest person, the quietest, or nothing at all?
3. Where in your life do you beg for approval rather than earn it? Be specific about the behaviour, not the feeling.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch any live audience event this week — comedy, a gig, a talk, a pub quiz. Watch the audience, not the performer, for ten minutes. Track one moment where the performer clearly adjusted to the room, and one where they did not.

**Bring back:** A one-sentence description of each moment, and what the audience did immediately before the adjustment (or the missed one).

## 📌 Bring to next session

- Notes on where the emotional shift landed each day, and which days you pushed it early
- Your written record of the three group decisions and whether you steered inside the ten minutes
- Answers to at least two journal prompts
- The two audience moments from the watch task, one sentence each
