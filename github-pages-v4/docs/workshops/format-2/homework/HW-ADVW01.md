# 🎒 Homework — Week 01 · Re-contract & Group Mind

> Last session we re-contracted and started sharing one attention between us; this week's reps take that off the stage and into the rest of your life.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Notes on Good Acting](../../../solo-practice/30_notes-on-good-acting.md) | 10 minutes, five days | Notice how much of the drill you spend in the centre of your own attention. Each time you catch yourself steering the material, widen out: what else is in the room, on the page, at the edge of the frame? Note in one sentence when the wide view changed a choice you were about to make. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Notice the Group Mind

Pick one group you are already in this week — a household, a team at work, a regular class, a group chat. Sit with it three separate times. Each time, spend five minutes doing less than you normally would: talk less, offer less, steer less. Watch what the group moves towards on its own. Where does the energy go when you stop pushing? Who is being followed, and who is being talked over? Afterwards, write two lines in your phone: what the group seemed to want, and what you wanted. Keep them separate. Do not correct the group, do not announce the experiment, and do not use it as an excuse to withdraw from something that needs you. If you finish the week with fewer than six lines, you missed it.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Name a group where you consistently know what everyone is doing without checking, and one where you have no idea. What is different about how you sit in each?
2. When you feel unnoticed in a group, what do you do? Write the specific behaviour, not the feeling.
3. Recall a time the group wanted something you did not want. What did you do with the gap?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch any group of three or more in public — a café table, a queue, a work meeting — for ten minutes. Track only handovers: who gives the floor to whom, and how. Words, glances, a shift of weight, going quiet.

**Bring back:** One handover you can describe in two sentences: what the person did, and how the others knew it was their turn.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- Six or more lines from the group-mind week, group's want and own want kept separate
- One described handover from the watch task
- One sentence from the drill where widening out changed a choice
