# 🎒 Homework — Week 06 · The Ensemble Mind — Support & Edits

> Last session the back line finally started cutting scenes at the top of a laugh instead of three lines after it — this week's reps are about keeping that instinct warm between classes.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Solo Character Switches](../../../solo-practice/05_solo-character-switches.md) | 8-10 minutes | You are not practising the characters. You are practising the join. Notice the half-second before each switch: do you pause to plan the next one, or do you let the body change first and find out who it is afterwards? Notice which character you keep returning to when you get tired — that one is your default, and on stage it will crowd out whatever your partner needs. Notice how it feels to drop a character you were enjoying because the timer said so. That discomfort is the same discomfort as calling an edit on a scene that is still fun. Get used to it alone so it costs less in front of people. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Make Someone a Genius

Pick one meeting or conversation this week where you would normally push your own idea. A work meeting, a discussion about the weekend, a family decision. Go in having decided in advance that you will not put your idea forward. Instead, find someone else's half-formed suggestion and spend the whole conversation making it work. Ask what they meant. Say the strong part of it back to them out loud so the room hears it. Add the practical detail they missed. Name them when the idea gets credit. Afterwards, write down one sentence: whose idea left the room, and whether you added to it or steered it back toward your own. If you find you had quietly rerouted things to your version, you have not done the practice. Do it again with someone else before the week ends.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a time someone made you look good in front of others without making a show of helping. What did they actually do — the specific words or moves? Could you do that on stage?
2. Where in your life do you hold on to something past its ending — a job, a conversation, a story you tell at parties? What would a clean edit look like there, and what stops you calling it?
3. When you support someone, are you following them or managing them? Write about one recent case where you were honestly not sure which it was.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch any group activity where nobody is officially in charge — a kitchen at service, a band, a five-a-side team, three colleagues assembling flat-pack furniture. Spend fifteen minutes watching only the people who are not the current focus. Track what they do while someone else has the task.

**Bring back:** One specific, describable action you saw a supporter make: the gesture, the step sideways, the object handed over before it was asked for. Be ready to demonstrate it in ten seconds without explaining the context.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The one action from your watch-and-notice, ready to demonstrate physically in under ten seconds
- The single sentence from your Make Someone a Genius practice — whose idea left the room
- One character from your solo switches that you now recognise as your default
- One moment from the week, on or off stage, where you should have edited something and didn't
