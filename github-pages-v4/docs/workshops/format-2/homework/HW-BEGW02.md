# 🎒 Homework — Week 02 · The First Thought Is a Gift

> Last session we watched what happens in the half-second before you speak; this week's reps are about shortening that gap.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Word Association](../../../solo-practice/02_word-association.md) | 5 minutes | Notice the words you swallow. Most people discard their first association because it is rude, boring, or too personal, then offer a second-rate substitute. Count how many times that happens in five minutes. Notice also whether your pace drops when you start trying to be interesting — the slowing is the filter switching on. |
| [Write an Improvised Scene](../../../solo-practice/03_write-an-improvised-scene.md) | 10 minutes | Notice the urge to reread and fix. Keep the pen moving and see whether the obvious line was in fact fine. Afterwards, mark any place you paused. Ask what you were protecting. The scenes will often be plain. Plain is the point this week. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Three Sounds

Once a day, wherever you are, stop what you are doing for thirty seconds and name three things you can hear. Say them silently to yourself, plainly: fridge, traffic, someone typing. Take the first three sounds that arrive rather than hunting for interesting ones. Do not describe how they make you feel. Do not decide whether the moment was peaceful. Just name three and carry on. Pick a fixed hook so you remember — kettle boiling, seatbelt clicked, before you open your laptop. If you miss a day, start again the next day; do not double up. At the end of the week you should be able to say how many days you managed out of seven. That number is the whole test.

If you find yourself listening for something worth reporting, you have drifted. Come back to the ordinary noise.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Write about a moment this week when you had an answer ready and said something else instead. What was the second version protecting?
2. What kind of first thought do you trust — practical, funny, kind? What kind do you never let out, and who taught you that?
3. Describe something ordinary you did this week in six sentences, without making it a good story.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch a conversation you are not part of — a queue, a café, a work meeting where you are quiet. Listen for one moment where someone answered instantly, without hedging.

**Bring back:** The exact words they used, as close as you can recall, and what happened in the conversation immediately after.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- Count of days completed on Three Sounds, out of seven
- One overheard instant answer, quoted, with what followed
- One written scene from the drill, unedited, willing to read a few lines aloud
- One journal entry you are happy to summarise in a sentence
