# 🎒 Homework — Week 08 · The Public Showcase — Two Chairs or Games Night

> Last session they ran the showcase format in the room with the lights up; this week is about holding that same plainness when strangers are watching.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Read a Character from a Play Out Loud](../../../solo-practice/28_read-a-character-from-a-play-out-loud.md) | 10 minutes | Notice where you speed up. Most people rush the line they think is weak, then apologise with their body. Track your feet and your breath: are you standing still and breathing low, or shifting weight on every full stop? Read one page twice — once at your normal pace, once slower than feels comfortable — and note which version you could still hear clearly from the back of a room. Notice what happens after a line that falls flat on your own ears. Do you drop the character, or stay in it and keep going? That recovery is the thing being tested on showcase night, not the line itself. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Get Creative

Make one small thing this week that nobody asked you for. A drawing on the back of a receipt. A four-line poem in your notes app. A meal you invented instead of followed. A playlist for a mood nobody else has. Ten minutes is enough. Rules: it must be finished, it must be for no purpose, and you must not show it to anyone until class. Nobody grades it, nobody buys it, nobody thanks you. That is the point — you find out what you make when there is no audience to please and no brief to satisfy. If you reach Friday and there is nothing you could hold up and say "I made this, and it was pointless", you have missed the week. Do it Saturday morning instead. Twelve minutes.


## ✍️ Journal

1. Where in your ordinary week do you take care of yourself first — and where do you hand everything to other people and hope it comes back? Give one example of each from the last seven days.
2. Write about a time you said something plain and it landed badly. What did you do in the next thirty seconds? What would you do now?
3. What do you believe an audience wants from you? Write it down, then write what you actually want from a performer when you are the one watching.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch someone speak to a group this week — a colleague presenting, a barista taking orders at a rush, a teacher, someone on a stage. Watch their feet and their breath, not their words. Track what they do in the moment something does not land: a joke ignored, a question misheard, a slide that fails.

**Bring back:** One sentence describing exactly what their body did in that moment of not-landing, and whether it made you trust them more or less.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The finished pointless thing you made, in your pocket or on your phone
- One sentence on the person you watched and what their body did when something failed
- The play or scene you read aloud, with the page you read marked
- Any question about showcase night logistics — running order, arrival time, guests
