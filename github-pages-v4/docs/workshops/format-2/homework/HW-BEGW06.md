# 🎒 Homework — Week 06 · Status — The Seesaw

> Last session you moved status up and down mid-scene without changing who the character was. This week's reps keep that gap visible and adjustable.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Scenes of Status Shift](../../../solo-practice/23_scenes-of-status-shift.md) | 10 minutes | Notice the exact moment the gap changes hands, and what caused it — a look held too long, a sentence finished for someone, a chair taken. Notice whether you shifted status by raising yourself or by lowering the other person; they feel different to play. Notice how often you reach for volume when a pause would do the same work. Keep the character constant while the status moves, and see whether you can still recognise the same person at both ends. |
| [Scenes of Status Shift](../../../solo-practice/23_scenes-of-status-shift.md) | 5 minutes (second pass, no words) | Run the same shift with gesture and eyeline only. Notice where your weight sits, where your hands go, how much space you take. Notice that low status is not smallness and high status is not stillness — both can be either. Notice which end you default to when the words are gone. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — One Thing You Let Go

Pick one thing this week you would normally push for. Make it real but survivable: the restaurant choice, the last word in a work thread, being the one who is proved right about the route. Write it down on Monday. Then, for seven days, when that thing comes up, take your hands off it. State your view once, plainly, if asked. Then let it land wherever it lands. Do not sulk it back into the room later. Do not re-open it by text. You have failed the week if you find yourself relitigating it, and that is useful information, not a verdict. Notice what you feel in your chest in the ten seconds after you stop pushing. Notice whether anything you feared actually happened. Note the outcome on Sunday in one line.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe one relationship in your life where the status gap is fixed. Who set it, and when? What would it cost either of you to move it a notch?
2. Write about a time you raised your own status in a room and it worked, and a time you lowered it and that worked better. What told you which was needed?
3. Where do you confuse status with worth? What happens in your body when someone takes the higher position with you?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch one real interaction between two people who are not you — a counter and a customer, two colleagues, a parent and a teenager. Track the gap between them for two minutes. Mark the moment it changes and what changed it.

**Bring back:** One sentence describing the trigger of the shift, plus the physical detail that gave it away.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The written status-shift moment from the watch task, ready to read aloud in ten seconds
- The one thing you chose to let go of, and one line on how it went
- One journal entry you are willing to paraphrase in a pair
- A note of which end of the seesaw you default to when you stop thinking
