# 🎒 Homework — Week 05 · Weaving — Callbacks, Mapping, Theme

> Last session the group found the second half of a set by digging back into the first; this week's reps are about spotting the material that is already on the floor before you reach for anything new.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Styles and Genres in a Hat](../../../solo-practice/08_styles-and-genres-in-a-hat.md) | 10 minutes | Notice what survives the change of style. Run the same short scene idea through two or three genres and track which detail keeps coming back unprompted — a name, an object, a grievance. That sticky detail is what an audience would call the theme. Also notice the moment you feel the urge to invent a fresh premise instead of re-using the one in front of you. Mark it. That urge is the thing that kills weaving in a long set. Finally, notice whether the genre changes the meaning of a repeated element or just its costume. Both are useful, but only the first one lands as a callback. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — What the Piece Needed

Pick one thing each day that you did with other people: a meeting, a meal, a handover, bedtime with a child. Afterwards, take two minutes and write one sentence answering a single question — what did that actually need from me? Not what you contributed. Not how it went. What it needed. Sometimes the answer will be a thing you did. Often it will be something duller than what you offered: fewer words, an earlier finish, someone to agree quickly, someone to ask the obvious question. Write it plainly, even when the answer is that it needed nothing much from you. Seven sentences by the end of the week. If you find yourself writing about your own performance instead of the thing's needs, cross it out and try the sentence again.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a story you tell about your own life. What detail turns up every time you tell it, and what does its return suggest the story is really about?
2. Write about a time two unrelated parts of your life turned out to be connected. When did you notice, and did the noticing change anything?
3. Where in your week do you start something new because finishing the old thing feels harder? Give one concrete example.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch or rewatch anything with a shape — a film, an episode, a stand-up special, a long podcast interview. Track one element that appears early and returns late. Note the timestamp of both appearances and what has changed about it in between.

**Bring back:** The two timestamps and one sentence on what the return made you feel that the first appearance did not.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The two timestamps and the sentence from the watch task.
- One sticky detail from the Styles and Genres drill that kept reappearing across genres.
- The seven sentences from What the Piece Needed, or however many you managed.
- One journal answer you are willing to read aloud.
