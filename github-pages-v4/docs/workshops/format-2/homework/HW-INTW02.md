# 🎒 Homework — Week 02 · Base Reality & the One Odd Thing

> Last session the room kept naming the strange thing and then leaving it behind. This week's reps are about building the ordinary first, and staying with the exception long enough to learn what it is.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Heightening](../../../solo-practice/25_heightening.md) | 10 minutes, five days | Notice whether you set a normal before you break it. If you can't say what ordinary looked like in that scene, the odd thing had nothing to push against. Then watch what you do on the second and third beat: most people swap in a new odd thing rather than going further into the first. Track how many beats you get out of one deviation before you reach for a fresh one. Three is good. Also notice your volume — heightening is not getting louder or faster. It is the same thing, more of it, taken more seriously by the people in the room. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Three Things You Actually Want

On one evening this week, sit down with paper and write three things you want right now. Not things you should want. Not five-year plans. Things like a weekend with nothing in it, a different job title, an hour alone in the car, your brother to call first for once. Write until each one is specific enough that someone else could tell whether you got it. Cross out anything you wrote to look reasonable. Then, before the week ends, say one of the three out loud to another person — partner, friend, colleague on a walk. Say it plainly. You do not have to ask them for anything, explain it, or justify it. You have failed this only if the list stays in your head, or if nobody hears one of them.

Note where in your body you felt it when you said it.



## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe the base reality of an ordinary hour in your week — the routine so familiar you stopped seeing it. Now name the one thing in it that isn't normal, that you've quietly accepted. How long has it been there?
2. When you told someone one of your three wants, what happened in the pause before they answered? Write what you expected them to say and what they actually said.
3. Think of a time you noticed something odd and let it pass without asking. What would it have cost to stay with it for one more question?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Pick one conversation between other people this week — a shop, a train, a work meeting — and watch for the moment someone says something slightly off and nobody follows it. A strange detail, an unexplained fact, a small confession. Note what was said, and note what the group did next: changed subject, laughed, carried on.

**Bring back:** One sentence you overheard that nobody picked up on, and the question you'd have asked if you were in that scene.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The overheard unexplored line, plus the question you'd have asked.
- Your three wants list, or at least the one you said out loud — you can keep the other two private.
- A note on how many beats you sustained one deviation in the heightening drill.
- One scene idea built from a base reality you actually live in.
