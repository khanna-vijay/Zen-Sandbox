# 🎒 Homework — Week 04 · Platform, Tilt & the Story Spine

> Last session the room built platforms and then broke them; this week the reps are about noticing where the break happened and what it cost.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Non-Fiction Summary](../../../solo-practice/09_non-fiction-summary.md) | 10 minutes, five days | Tell them to summarise something real — a news item, a work meeting, a row with a neighbour — out loud in under ninety seconds. Then notice three things. One: where the routine was. What was normal before anything happened? Most students skip this and start at the drama, so make them name the ordinary state first. Two: the exact moment it broke, and who or what broke it. Three: whether the ending made the break make sense, or just stopped. If the summary ends with 'and then it was over', the spine is missing. Have them redo that one the next day and find a consequence instead of a full stop. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Look for the Potential in Others

Pick one person you see most weeks — a colleague, a neighbour, your brother-in-law, the person who runs the desk at your gym. Spend the first three days watching for what they are quietly good at. Not their job title. The thing they do easily that others find hard: settling an argument, remembering names, fixing things, knowing when to stop talking. Write it down in one sentence by Wednesday. Then, before the week ends, hand them one real chance to use it. Ask them to take a decision, sort a problem, teach you the thing. Say why you asked them. Then stay out of the way while they do it. You have failed the week if you never asked, or if you asked and then did it yourself.


## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe a week of your life where the routine broke. What was the platform — the boring, stable thing — before it went? Write the platform in more detail than the break.
2. Think of a change in your life that made sense only afterwards. What did you have to learn or admit before it made sense? How long did that take?
3. Where in your life are you protecting a routine that nobody actually needs? What would break if you broke it on purpose?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch one episode of any scripted show, or read one short story. Time how long it takes before the ordinary world is disturbed. Note what the ordinary world consisted of — who does what, who wants what, what a normal day looks like.

**Bring back:** A single index card with three lines: the platform in one sentence, the tilt in one sentence, and the consequence in one sentence. Bring the card, not the plot.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The index card with platform, tilt and consequence in three sentences.
- One Non-Fiction Summary they had to redo because the ending stopped rather than resolved — be ready to say it aloud in ninety seconds.
- The one-sentence description of what the person they chose is quietly good at, and what happened when they were given the chance.
- One journal answer they are willing to read out, or the reason they are not.
