# 🎒 Homework — Week 04 · Listening With the Whole Body

> Last session you kept catching yourself drafting a reply mid-scene; this week's reps are about noticing that moment sooner and dropping the draft.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Sound to Dialogue](../../../solo-practice/11_sound-to-dialogue.md) | 8-10 minutes, five days | Notice the gap between hearing the sound and speaking. If you already know your line before the sound finishes, you planned. Notice where the listening happens in your body — most people report the chest or the jaw locking when they start rehearsing. Notice whether your first words repeat something from the sound or ignore it entirely. Repeating is fine. Ignoring is the signal to slow down. |
| [Sound to Dialogue](../../../solo-practice/11_sound-to-dialogue.md) | 5 minutes, two days (eyes closed variation) | With eyes shut, notice how much more of the sound you take in — length, volume, where it stops. Notice if the urge to plan gets stronger or weaker without visual input. Note one day where you did not plan at all, and what was different about that day. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Say Yes To One Thing

Once this week, accept something you would normally turn down. Not a large thing. The colleague's lunch invitation you usually skip. The neighbour's offer of help. The extra song at the party. The conversation with the person at the bus stop who starts talking to you. Pick something that costs you under an hour and does not put you or anyone else at risk. You will know the moment when it comes, because your first instinct will be a polite excuse already half-formed in your mouth. Let it go unsaid and accept instead. Do it once. Afterwards, write down what actually happened, not what you feared would happen. If you reached Sunday and declined everything, you did not do the practice. Say so honestly in class.


## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a recent conversation where you were clearly waiting to speak rather than listening. What were you protecting by having your line ready?
2. Who in your life listens to you well? Describe what they physically do — where they look, how they sit, when they speak. Which of those could you borrow?
3. When you accepted the thing you would normally decline, what did your body do in the seconds before you said yes?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch two people having a real conversation — a cafe, a train, a work meeting, a family dinner. Do not join in. Watch only for the moment one of them stops listening and starts loading their reply. It shows up before they speak: a breath taken and held, a shift forward, eyes flicking away, a small nod that comes too fast.

**Bring back:** One sentence describing the physical tell you saw, and roughly how long before they spoke it appeared.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The physical tell from watch and notice, in one sentence each — collect five or six aloud before the warm-up.
- Whether they completed the Say Yes To One Thing practice, and what happened. Take honest failures without comment.
- One line from their journal on what they were protecting by having a reply ready — volunteers only.
- Number of days they managed Sound to Dialogue, for pacing the week's scene work.
