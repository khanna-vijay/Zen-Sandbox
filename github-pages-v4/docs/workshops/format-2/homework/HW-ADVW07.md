# 🎒 Homework — Week 07 · The Audience Contract

> Last session the room read the audience mid-scene and then chased the laugh; this week's reps separate reading from reacting.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Film Dialogue](../../../solo-practice/27_film-dialogue.md) | 10-15 minutes, four days | Watch the scene twice. First pass, note where the room in the film reacts — where a laugh, gasp or silence is clearly built in. Second pass, note what the characters do next. Nine times in ten they carry on with their own business rather than serving the reaction. Mark the one moment where a character does play to the reaction and write down what it costs the scene. Then notice your own body while you copy the dialogue: if your voice lifts at the end of a line to invite approval, you are asking for something. Say the line again without the lift and see whether the meaning survives. It usually does. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Truth Over Pandering, Off-Stage

Once a day this week, catch yourself about to smooth something over. It will be small: a colleague asks how their draft was, a friend asks if you minded waiting, someone asks whether you liked the meal. You will feel the softer answer arriving before you choose it. Say the plainer one instead. Plain does not mean blunt or cold — you can still be kind about how you say it — but the fact has to survive intact. "I found the middle section hard to follow." "I did mind, a bit." Then stop talking and let them respond. Do not rescue the pause with a joke or a compliment. Write one line in your phone each evening: what you were asked, what you nearly said, what you said. If a day passes with nothing written, that day is a miss. Aim for five out of seven.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe a time you changed what you were saying because of how a room was reacting. What did the room actually want, and what did you assume it wanted?
2. Who in your life gets your plainest answers, and who gets the softened version? What makes the difference — them, or you?
3. When you sense an audience, a meeting or a dinner table going flat, what is your first instinct? Louder, funnier, faster, quieter? Where did that instinct come from?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Sit in on any live event with an audience this week — stand-up, a gig, a lecture, a school assembly, a comedy podcast recording. Watch the performer, not the crowd. Find one moment where the room's energy drops and the performer notices it. Note what they do in the next thirty seconds.

**Bring back:** One sentence describing that thirty seconds, plus your call on whether the performer used the drop as information or obeyed it as an instruction.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The seven-line phone log from the off-stage practice (or however many lines you managed).
- One sentence from the watch-and-notice task, ready to read aloud.
- Your Film Dialogue notes: the one moment a character played to the reaction, and what it cost.
- One question you now have about reading a room that you did not have last week.
