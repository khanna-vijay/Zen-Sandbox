# 🎒 Homework — Week 03 · The Want — Stakes & Objectives

> Last session we found scenes that mattered because someone could lose something; this week you keep asking the same question off-stage and in solo reps.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Improvising Your Half of the Scene](../../../solo-practice/26_improvising-your-half-of-the-scene.md) | 8-10 minutes | Notice the moment you decide what your character wants from the imagined partner. It usually arrives late, after two or three lines of pleasantries. Time it. Then notice what happens to your voice and pace once the want is clear: most people speed up and get more specific. Also notice what you would lose if the imagined partner said no. If the answer is nothing, the scene goes flat and you will feel it. Try running the same opening twice, once where a no costs you nothing and once where it costs you the job, the friendship, the evening. Note the difference in how hard you listen for the reply. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — The Honest Answer

Once this week, when someone asks you something ordinary — how was your weekend, are you happy with the plan, can you take this on — give the true answer instead of the easy one. Pick a low-stakes moment with someone you will see again. Not a confrontation. Just the version you would normally sand down: "Actually, I'd rather not", or "It was a hard week", or "I don't think that will work." Say it plainly, then stop talking. Do not soften it with three qualifiers. Notice the pause afterwards, and notice what you were afraid of losing before you spoke. You have failed this practice if you got to Sunday and cannot name the exact sentence you said and who heard it.

One time is enough. Do not make a project of it.

Write the sentence down the same day.

Bring it, or don't.

That's the whole thing.

Don't confess to anyone you cannot afford to unsettle.

Keep it small.

Keep it true.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Think of a conversation this month where you wanted something and did not say so. What were you protecting? What would it have cost you to name it out loud?
2. Name three things you would genuinely hate to lose. Which of them has ever shown up in a scene you improvised?
3. When you play a character who wants something badly, what stops you — embarrassment, fear of seeming aggressive, not knowing what they want yet? Be specific about which one it usually is.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch any scene from a film or drama you already like — ten minutes is plenty. Track one character and work out, minute by minute, what they stand to lose if the scene goes badly. Note the moment the audience is first told or shown the cost.

**Bring back:** Bring one sentence: the character, the thing at risk, and roughly how far into the scene we learn about it. Be ready to say it in under twenty seconds.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The one honest sentence you said this week, and who you said it to — share only if you want to.
- Your watch-and-notice sentence: character, what's at risk, when we find out.
- One journal line about a want you did not voice.
- Any solo drill opening where the want arrived quickly and the scene got sharper.
