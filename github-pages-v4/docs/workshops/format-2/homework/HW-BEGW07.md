# 🎒 Homework — Week 07 · Emotion & the Voice

> Last session you swapped emotions mid-scene without changing a word of the plot; this week's reps put that switch in the breath first.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Gibberish Scene](../../../solo-practice/10_gibberish-scene.md) | 6-8 minutes | Notice where the feeling actually lives once the words are useless. Track your breath: does it shorten before you sound angry, drop before you sound tender? Notice the moment you start decorating the gibberish to be clever, and drop back to pure sound. Notice whether you can tell what you are feeling from the tape of your own voice. |
| [Emotional Range Challenge](../../../solo-practice/22_emotional-range-challenge.md) | 8-10 minutes | Notice the lag between deciding on an emotion and your body agreeing. Time it roughly. Notice which two or three emotions you keep landing on by default, and which one you skip every time. Notice what happens if you change the breath first and let the volume, pace and pitch follow, rather than pushing the voice into a shape. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Inner Critic → Inner Coach

Once a day this week, catch yourself right after a small mistake. Missed turning, wrong name, burnt pan, sent email with the typo. Whatever your head says next, catch the actual sentence and write it down on your phone in the words you used. Then say the same information out loud, quietly, in the voice you would use for a friend who had done it. Same facts, different breath. Do not argue with the first sentence or correct it. Just say the second one. One a day, seven by Saturday. If you get to the end of a day with nothing written, you missed it rather than had a perfect day; note that too. Bring the list, or the count.


## ✍️ Journal

1. Which emotion do you perform easily in life and which one do you only ever describe? What does each one do to your breathing?
2. Write out one of your inner-critic sentences from this week exactly as it arrived. Then write what changed when you said it kindly — the words, the pace, or only the tone?
3. When did you last let someone see a feeling arrive on you rather than telling them about it afterwards? What did it cost, and what did it get you?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch one real conversation this week where someone's mood shifts mid-sentence — a phone call overheard, a colleague, someone at a checkout. Do not watch a film for this. Track the order: what moved first, breath, pitch, pace, posture or vocabulary?

**Bring back:** One sentence naming the person's situation and the order of the shift, plus a ten-second imitation of the voice before and after.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The seven inner-critic sentences, or however many you caught, and the day count
- One journal prompt answered in full
- The overheard mood-shift, ready to imitate for ten seconds
- Any emotion from the range drill that you kept avoiding
