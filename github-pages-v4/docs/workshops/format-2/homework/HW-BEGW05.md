# 🎒 Homework — Week 05 · Where, Who, What — The Body in Space

> Last session you built rooms with your hands instead of your mouths; this week's reps keep the objects the same size from one minute to the next.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Environment](../../../solo-practice/14_environment.md) | 8-10 minutes | Notice where the walls are the moment you stop thinking about them. Pick one fixed point — a door, a window, a sink — and check whether it has drifted by the end. Notice your feet: do you walk through the table, or around it? Notice whether your hands change the height of a shelf between the first reach and the fourth. Do not narrate. If you catch yourself saying the name of the room out loud, stop and use weight instead. |
| [Breakfast](../../../solo-practice/17_breakfast.md) | 5-8 minutes | Notice the weight of things. A full kettle and an empty one do not travel the same way. Notice how long a real action takes — pouring is slower than you think, opening a jar takes two hands and a moment of resistance. Notice where you put the lid down, and whether you can find it again. Notice your eyes: they should arrive at the object before your hand does. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Hand It Over

Pick one task this week that is genuinely yours and give it to someone else. Not a chore you hate — something you quietly believe only you do properly. The weekly shop. A report at work. Getting a child out of the door on time. Hand it over on Monday with the outcome stated once and no method attached. Then leave it alone. No checking in. No asking how it's going. No going behind them to look at the result before it's finished. You have failed this practice if you follow up, hover, or redo any part of it. You have completed it if the week ends and the task was done by someone who wasn't you, in a way you would not have chosen. Note what you felt on the second day, when the urge to check was strongest.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe a room you know so well you could walk it in the dark. What tells your body where the furniture is — sound, air, the give of the floor? Write it without naming the room.
2. Where in your life do you explain instead of showing? Think of a time you told someone what you were feeling because you did not trust them to read it. What would showing have cost you?
3. When you handed your task over this week, what did you assume would go wrong? Did it? Write what actually happened, not what you expected.

## 👀 Watch / notice

Spend fifteen minutes somewhere with a counter — a café, a chemist, a bakery. Watch one worker's hands. Track how they handle the objects they touch a hundred times a day: how far they reach without looking, where their hip meets the counter, which drawer they open with a knee.

**Bring back:** One repeated physical habit you saw, described in three sentences, and be ready to do it on your feet for thirty seconds.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- Journal responses — at least two of the three prompts written down.
- The observed physical habit, written in three sentences and rehearsed on your feet.
- A one-line report on the handed-over task: what you gave away, and whether you checked.
- One object from Breakfast whose weight you never got right, ready to name in the warm-up.
