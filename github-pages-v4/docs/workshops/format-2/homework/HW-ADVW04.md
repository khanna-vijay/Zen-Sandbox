# 🎒 Homework — Week 04 · The Harold — Beats, Groups, Returns

> Last session you ran full Harolds and found beat two either repeated the joke or abandoned it; this week's reps are about carrying a game forward so it returns later and truer.

## 🧘 Solo drills this week

| Drill | Time | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| [Object Monologue](../../../solo-practice/19_object-monologue.md) | 8-10 minutes | Pick one object and talk about it for three minutes, then stop, wait a minute, and talk about the same object again without repeating your first sentences. Notice what the second pass does on its own: it usually goes further back, or further in, and lands somewhere more specific. That is beat two. Notice also the moment you feel tempted to start a fresh object instead — that urge is the same urge that makes a Harold abandon its own material. |
| [Object Monologue](../../../solo-practice/19_object-monologue.md) | 5 minutes | On alternate days, record the first pass and play it back before the second. Notice which single detail you want to return to. Name it out loud before you start again. In class this is the skill of hearing the game inside a first beat rather than inventing a new one under pressure. |

## 🌍 Beyond the stage — Generosity of Energy

Choose one person this week — a colleague, a neighbour, someone at home. Once a day, for seven days, give them more energy than the exchange requires. Not a favour. Energy: the full greeting instead of the nod, the follow-up question instead of the polite noise, the two minutes of actual attention when you were going to give thirty seconds. Same person each time, so it accumulates rather than scatters. Afterwards, note in one line what it cost you — time, patience, the effort of switching out of your own head. Some days it will cost nothing. Some days you will resent it, and that is the useful data. You have failed the week if you spread it across many people, or if you cannot say on Sunday who your person was.

Do not tell them you are doing it.

Do not tell them.

Keep the daily note to one line.

You will know by Wednesday whether you are actually doing it or performing it.

Stop if it becomes a burden on them.

Seven days, one person, one line a day.

Bring the notes.

## ✍️ Journal

1. Describe a situation in your own life that came back later, changed. What stayed the same about it, and what had genuinely moved on? Write the two versions side by side.
2. When you feel a structure closing in on you — a routine, a meeting format, a family ritual — what do you do? Do you break it, endure it, or use it? Write about one specific instance from the last month.
3. Who in your life keeps returning to the same subject? Is it repetition or is it deepening? How can you tell the difference from the outside?

## 👀 Watch / notice

Watch or rewatch one episode of a returning television comedy or drama you already know. Track a single running element — a phrase, a relationship beat, a piece of business — across the episode and note each time it comes back.

**Bring back:** Write down the element and its three appearances in one sentence each, then one sentence on what changed between the first and last. Bring the four sentences on paper.

## 📌 Bring to next session

- The four sentences from the watch task, on paper
- Seven daily one-line notes from the Generosity of Energy week, plus the name of your chosen person
- One object you used for the Object Monologue drill, or a photo of it
- One question about beat structure you could not answer yourself this week
