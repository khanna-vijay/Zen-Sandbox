# Week 05 — Character From the Outside In

> *Change how the body moves and a specific person arrives; the biography can wait.*

| Course | Week | Domain | Focus | Stage | Length | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Engine — Scene & Ensemble | 5/8 | D1 | `D1.S2` — Emotional Fluidity | Advanced Beginner → Competent | 180 min | 10–20 |

!!! note "Builds on"
    They can shape a scene from outside it now, like writers; this week puts them back inside it as bodies, so the person doing the wanting is a particular person.

## 🎬 The arc of this session

They arrive as four weeks of good talkers — quick with words, all standing the same way, all playing themselves with a different job title. The session takes dialogue off the table first, gives them weight, tempo, tension and hands as the only starting material, and holds the line that nobody explains their character out loud. They leave able to walk into a scene as someone specific before they speak, and with a first taste of the same story told through four genres.

!!! tip "Watch for"
    Faces doing the work while bodies stay neutral — they will act the character from the neck up and think they have changed. Coach from the back row with the sound off in your head: if you cannot tell two players apart by silhouette, the body has not moved yet.

## 🎒 Before you start

- Clear the widest floor you can and push chairs to one wall — today needs walking room, not a seated circle. Mark a playing area about four metres wide with tape or two chairs as goalposts.
- Set a back-row chair at the far end of the room. You will sit in it during Drill A and the Scene Lab to check whether two players read as different people from there.
- Have a music source and three tracks ready for Somatic Weather Systems: something slow and heavy, something fast and jittery, something even and light. Check the volume before anyone arrives.
- Review last week's homework — they were shaping scenes from outside. Ask two people in the first five minutes what they noticed, then park it; today is inside the body.
- Write the cue on the board: 'Change the body, and the person arrives.' Under it write the rule for the day: 'No one describes their character in words.'
- Note who has an injury, a bad knee or a pregnancy before the warm-up cluster. Every physical instruction today has a smaller version; tell the room that out loud rather than privately.

## 1. 🧊 Ice-breaker (5 min)

*Stand in a circle, phones down. Quick one — you either regret it or you repeat it, and you tell us which in under five seconds.*

**Why here:** Gets voices in the room and low-stakes decision-making going before anything physical is asked of them; run one circle at 10-13, two circles at 14-17, three at 18-20 so nobody waits more than a minute for their turn.

### Regret or Repeat

> A fast lap of the circle where everyone names the last thing they spent money on and delivers a one-word verdict on it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Stand everyone in one circle. Set the task: the last non-essential thing they paid for this week. No rent, no bills, no groceries.
2. Model it yourself first. Name your item in five words or fewer, then give a one-word verdict. Keep the whole demo under ten seconds.
3. Send them to the person on their left. Thirty seconds each: what it was, what happened after. Call the swap at halfway out loud.
4. Run the circle. Each person: the item in five words maximum, then 'again' or 'never again'. No stories, no justifying. Keep the lap moving.
5. Run a second, faster lap. One word only — how they felt at the moment they paid. Roughly three seconds each.
6. Ask for hands up from anyone who would buy the item named by the person on their right. Count the hands aloud, thank them, go straight to the warm-up.
7. Tell anyone who would rather not disclose a purchase to name a borrowed one, or say 'pass' and give a verdict on the room's last item.

## 2. 😄 Laughter Blast (5 min)

*Same circle, on your feet. Everything at half the speed you'd normally do it, and twice the volume. Go.*

**Why here:** Breaks the neutral default body straight away by decoupling speed from volume, which is the first hint of the day's dials.

### Half Speed, Full Volume

> The whole room celebrates an invisible goal in extreme slow motion, roaring at quarter speed, then does the same lap at double speed.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Clear the floor. Tell the room something wonderful has just happened and we are watching the replay in slow motion.
2. Demonstrate first. Arms up slowly, jaw slack, one long low dragged-out roar. Go bigger than feels sensible so nobody has to be first.
3. Run it on the spot at quarter speed, all together. Both arms up, one long low cheer, faces stretched. Three times, no travelling.
4. Add the lap. Everyone moves round the room in slow motion — knees high, sound still dragging, eyes up, no contact.
5. Call "slower". Halve it again. One step takes five seconds, the sound is barely a note. Hold it past comfortable.
6. Call "double speed". Same lap, same cheer, now high and fast and squeaky. Jogging only, two laps, nobody sprints.
7. Call "freeze". Hold pose and face for five seconds. Then one shared slow-motion roar, arms up, and let the laugh land.
8. Ask one question, take thirty seconds of answers, move straight into the next block.

**Side-coach:** *“Keep the volume up while the body slows — do not let them travel together.”*

## 3. 🔥 Warm-up cluster (15 min)

*Three short things back to back. Nobody speaks in any of them unless I say so.*

**Why here:** Three escalating physical inputs — a whole shape, a spine, a two-millimetre adjustment — so they meet the full range of scale before theory names it; at 10-13 run all three as one group, at 14-17 split into two groups for Trade You a Spine, at 18-20 split into three and shorten each round by a minute.

### Snap Into Someone

> On each clap the room throws itself into a random body shape, then lets whatever feeling that shape suggests take over for eight seconds.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Spread the room so everyone has an arm's span clear. Feet stay under the body throughout — no leaping, no floor work, no touching anyone.
2. Demonstrate once: on a clap, throw yourself into a shape you did not plan. Hold it. Drop back to neutral. Say that planning is the only wrong answer.
3. Run four shape-only rounds. Clap, everyone snaps into a shape, holds three counts, shakes out. No movement inside the shape, no sound yet. Just land.
4. Run six rounds with the person added. Clap, snap, hold two counts, then let the shape move for eight seconds as whoever it is. Clap resets to neutral.
5. Run four more rounds the same way, but the moving body now breathes out loud — sighs, huffs, grunts, groans. No words, no character voices.
6. Run two final rounds. Move as the person for eight seconds, then on the clap freeze, and drop straight to neutral: feet flat, shoulders down, face empty.
7. Cut talking between rounds. Keep the claps coming. Say nothing that invites anyone to explain who they were.

### Trade You a Spine

> Pairs hand each other one physical change at a time and let the feeling that arrives ride back across.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Put everyone in pairs facing each other, an arm's length apart. Label one A, one B. Anyone left over joins a pair as a trio.
2. Set the rule: one change to one body part only — jaw, shoulder, hip, hand, spine. No deliberate face-pulling, no whole-body poses.
3. Have A make one change and hold it. B copies it exactly, then says "I'll be straight with you" in whatever feeling the borrowed body hands them.
4. Swap roles and repeat, so B offers and A copies. Keep the offers small and specific.
5. Tell pairs to keep one shape and trade it back and forth. Each time you receive it, add one notch more of the same thing. Same line every pass.
6. Change partners. Run the same trade in silence: pass, copy, add a notch, and let the feeling out through breath and one sound only.
7. Call the last pass. Everyone drops the shape, shakes out the arms, faces front and takes two normal breaths before you move on.

### Two Millimetres

> Standing still, the room stacks three tiny postural changes and names the mood each one delivers.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Spread the room so nobody can touch a neighbour. Everyone faces the same wall. Set neutral: feet parallel under hips, knees soft, arms hanging, eyes on one spot. Silence from here.
2. Call the first micro-change: weight fully back into the heels. Say two millimetres, not a pose. Everyone holds it, silent and still.
3. Cue the murmur. The whole room says one word for the mood that arrived, all at once, low volume. No solos, no discussion.
4. Layer the second change on top of the first: chin down two millimetres, breath high in the chest. Hold in silence, then cue another one-word murmur together.
5. Layer the third: one shoulder drifts forward, jaw slackens. All three changes stay on. Hold in silence, then cue a final one-word murmur.
6. Have everyone walk four slow steps forward carrying the whole stack. Stop. Then shake it out and return to neutral standing before you speak again.

## 4. 🧠 Theory — Emotional Fluidity (20 min)

![Emotional Fluidity](../../../assets/infographics/D1.S2.jpg){ .infographic }

- **The big idea:** A specific person is a specific body. Change weight, tempo, where the tension sits and what the hands do, and behaviour follows without anyone deciding a backstory.
- **Where you are on the path:** They are competent scene-thinkers who default to themselves in costume. In the room this looks like fluent, likeable dialogue where every character has the same posture, the same pace and the same amount of eye contact. They will resist this block slightly because talking is where they feel safe.
- **The one cue to coach:** *“Change the body, and the person arrives.”*

**The three beats**

1. **Say it.** Say: four weeks you have been good with words, and the scenes are starting to sound like each other. Say: today you get four dials — weight, tempo, tension, hands — and you set them before you speak. Say: the biography can wait; it will turn up on its own, and if it does not, the scene does not need it. Say the day's rule plainly: nobody tells us who they are. We watch it.
2. **Show it.** Stand in the playing area neutral. Say a plain line — 'I've been waiting.' Then change one dial only: drop your weight into your heels and slow your tempo by half. Say the same line. Then reset, put the tension in your jaw and let your hands go still at your sides. Same line again. Ask the room what changed about the person. Take two answers, no more. Point out you invented no facts.
3. **Try it.** Everyone on their feet, spread out, no partners. Call one dial at a time — 'weight low', 'weight high', 'tempo fast', 'tension in the shoulders', 'hands never stop'. Thirty seconds each, walking the room. On your last call, freeze them and have them say one line of nothing dialogue — 'it's not here' — from wherever the body has put them. Then sit them down. No discussion.

!!! warning "The misunderstanding that always comes up"
    They hear 'physical character' as 'big cartoon walk' and start doing comedy shapes. Correct it once, clearly: the dials can move two millimetres and still change the person. Small and consistent beats large and abandoned after ten seconds.

## 5. 🎲 Drill A — isolation (25 min)

*Spread out, find your own metre of floor. Music will change three times. Your body answers the music; your mouth stays shut.*

**Why here:** Isolates the four dials with no scene, no partner obligation and no dialogue, so the body has nowhere to hide behind talking; keep it as one whole-room drill at every size and use the back-row chair to spot the neutral bodies.

### Somatic Weather Systems

> Navigate and blend complex, conflicting emotions using physical precision and non-verbal vocalization.

`Players 2–99` · `~25 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Spread the room so nobody can touch a neighbour. Have everyone stand, eyes soft or closed, and spend thirty seconds noticing breath, weight and posture without changing anything.
2. Name one emotion at low intensity — Anticipation at 3. Tell them to build it in posture, breath rate and small non-verbal sound only. No words, no mime, no story.
3. Call numbers on a dial of 1 to 10. Players scale the same emotion up and down as you call, changing shape, breath and sound to match each level.
4. Give the recovery tool now: anyone lost or overloaded plays being lost — physicalise the confusion, sound it out, and let that carry them back. Stepping to the wall is always allowed.
5. Add a second, conflicting emotion at a different number. Grief at 4 underneath Joy at 6. Both live in the body at once. Neither cancels the other.
6. Ask for abstract vocalisation only — hums, sighs, clicks, breath. Ban words, laughter as a joke, and character voices. The sound reports the blend, nothing more.
7. Layer imagined sensation on top: cold rain, hot sand underfoot, thick air. Players keep the blend and let the sensation shift it.
8. Bring everyone back to neutral stance and unhurried breath. Finish with one deep in-breath and a loud shared exhale on your count.

## 6. 🎲 Drill B — under load (25 min)

*Partners. One of you builds, one of you gets built. You will swap. Builders — you set the body, you do not say who they are.*

**Why here:** Puts the dials under pressure by making someone else set them, which stops them retreating to the one physicality they find comfortable; pairs at 10-13 and 14-17, and at 18-20 run trios with one observer per trio rotating every round.

### The Impulse Architect

> Externalize your deepest, wordless physical impulses and engage in a profound, non-verbal dialogue with them.

`Players 2–99` · `~25 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Put the class in pairs. Name one A, the Embodier, and one B, the Architect. Give each pair a clear patch of floor, out of arm's reach of the next pair.
2. Tell A to stand still, close their eyes, breathe once, and locate a real physical sensation in the body right now — tightness, heaviness, buzz, pull. No story, no naming.
3. Have A open their eyes and hold that sensation as a shape: weight, resistance, posture, face. Stillness for ten seconds. Count it out loud for the room.
4. Tell B to watch and take the first impression that arrives. No analysis. Ask what this energy seems to want.
5. Send B into the space as that impression made into a separate creature — specific walk, specific physical centre, non-verbal sound only: hums, breath, gibberish, rhythm.
6. Give B one clear physical intention towards A: crowd them, soothe them, pull away, disrupt. Commit fully. No words at any point.
7. Let A answer with the body — resist it, take it in, dodge it, absorb it. Wordless throughout. Run thirty to sixty seconds and let the relationship shift.
8. Call freeze at a resolution or a strong physical beat. Both breathe out and shake the body loose. Swap roles and repeat.
9. Agree contact rules before the first round: hands on shoulders, arms and upper back only if both nod; anyone may work no-contact all session, no explanation.

## 7. 🎯 Scene Lab (35 min)

*Scenes now. You set your body before you cross into the space, and once you're in, I can turn any of the three dials on you mid-scene.*

**Why here:** First scenes of the day, with all three channels live at once so emotional shifts come from physical shifts rather than announcements; at 10-13 everyone plays twice, at 14-17 everyone plays once with a second pass for four volunteers, at 18-20 split into two lab groups in opposite corners and rotate coaching between them.

### The Triple-Channel Dial

> Calibrate your voice, body, and emotion instantly to a single, dynamic intensity scale.

`Players 3–99` · `~35 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

**How to play**

1. Send two players into the space. Have them establish a relationship and a location inside their first three lines. Keep it ordinary — a kitchen, a queue, a car.
2. Give each player a secret emotion and a matching body centre — head, chest, gut or hips. Whisper it or hand a folded slip. They do not share it.
3. Call "Dial five" and start the scene. Both players pitch emotion, physical size and vocal volume at moderate.
4. Call a new number between one and ten every fifteen to thirty seconds. Say only the number. Do not explain it.
5. Tell players to move all three channels on every call: emotional intensity, physical scale and movement, vocal volume and articulation.
6. At one to three, demand real restraint — minimal movement, quiet or whispered speech, the emotion compressed but still fully present.
7. At eight to ten, demand expansion — large physical presence, high kinetic energy, projected voice.
8. Forbid any mention of numbers, dials or levels in the dialogue. Players justify each shift inside the story as it happens.
9. Run each scene three to five minutes, then swap in the next pair with fresh emotions and centres.

## 8. 🏟️ Showcase round (30 min)

*One suggestion from the room. We tell it plain once, then four more times through four genres, with a short reset between each — sit, breathe, choose the new body, go.*

**Why here:** First taste of the format they will be performing later in the course, and it forces the bodies to survive a change of genre rather than resetting to neutral; at 10-13 use one cast of four for all four genres, at 14-17 use two casts alternating, at 18-20 use three casts with the rest as audience and a hard two-minute cap per telling.

### Five Tellings

> One audience suggestion becomes a plain two-minute story, then the same story is retold four more times in genres the audience picks.

`Players 10–20` · `~30 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact incidental` · `Props: required`

**How to play**

1. Collect suggestion slips as people arrive: one ordinary object or one dull errand each. No jobs, no puns. Fold them into a hat.
2. Host explains the shape in one minute: one story, told five ways. Draw three slips, read them aloud, let applause pick one.
3. Send three players to run the Plain Telling. No genre, no jokes. They establish who they are to each other, where they are, and what one wants.
4. Appoint a Story Keeper. They write three anchors on the board during the Plain Telling: one spoken line, one object, one relationship.
5. Appoint a Genre Caller with a deck of genre cards. They draw a genre before each retelling and announce it clearly.
6. Recast fresh players for every retelling. Everyone who has not yet played goes on before anyone repeats. Reread the anchors aloud each time.
7. Run four genre retellings of the same story. Same anchors, same events, different body and world each time.
8. Finish with the Stack: the whole company replays all five tellings, sixty seconds each, in order, cut by a bell.
9. Bow in one line, tech and front of house included. Host names the three anchors once more and thanks the room.

## 9. 💭 Reflection & homework (10 min)

**Deepen your improv**
1. Which of the four dials did you set today without noticing, and which one did you have to force? Name the one you avoid.
2. In the Scene Lab, when did the body give you an emotional change you would not have thought of in words?

**Beyond the stage**
3. Think of someone you see every week — colleague, neighbour, family. What does their weight, tempo and hands tell you about them that they have never said out loud?

??? note "🎓 Facilitator notes"

    **Timing risks**
    - Theory overruns if you take more than two answers during show_it. Cap the discussion at 90 seconds and let the try_it do the arguing.
    - The Showcase is the tightest block — four genre tellings plus resets in 30 minutes means roughly five minutes each including the reset. Have a hard signal ready and use it mid-sentence if needed.
    - The warm-up cluster runs long at 18-20 because splitting and re-gathering three groups eats three minutes. Pre-assign groups before the block starts.

    **Failure modes this week**
    - Cartoon walks. They mistake specificity for size, and by minute three of Drill A half the room is doing hunchbacks. Stop it once, demonstrate a two-millimetre version, move on.
    - Talking the character. Someone will say 'I'm an anxious dentist' in the first Scene Lab scene. Restate the rule, restart the scene, do not lecture.
    - Faces only. The most common quiet failure — everything happens in the eyebrows. Watch from the back row and call the body, not the performance.
    - Body dropped on the first laugh. They set a good physicality, get a response, and revert to themselves to enjoy it. Side-coach 'keep the body' the moment you see it go.

    **What good looks like by the end:** By the end, two players in a scene are distinguishable from the back row with the sound off. Emotional shifts arrive as changes in weight or tempo before they arrive as lines. And the space around them starts getting used — doors, chairs, objects — because bodies that are somewhere specific tend to furnish the room.

    **Carry into next week:** They can now build a person from a body. Next week that person needs to want something in front of another person who wants something else, so open by asking them to set a body first and find the want second — and keep the no-describing rule in force so it becomes house style rather than this week's exercise.
