# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Not Mine Anymore

![Not Mine Anymore](../img/intermediate_w08__b3_2.jpg){ .infographic }

> In pairs facing the same wall, one player makes a tiny offer and the other sends it out to an imaginary crowd, so the credit lands on the room rather than the maker.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 08 run-sheet](../week-08.md)

## Why this activity, here

First thing in the door on showcase week. The room arrives nervous and inward. This turns everyone's body towards the audience wall before any scene work, and rehearses the handover that keeps a match moving. It feeds the whole night: every game, every scene, every tag-out depends on someone taking a small thing and making it the room's.

## What students actually learn

- That making an offer big is a job you can do for someone else, not a bid for attention.
- That an offer does not need to be good to be worth amplifying.
- That facing the audience is a physical choice, and it changes how you play.

## Setup

Clear floor. Name one wall the crowd wall — the one the real audience will be behind, if you know it. Everyone stands facing it. Pairs stand side by side, both facing the wall, not each other. No chairs, no props, no notes.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Set the crowd wall. Pair people side by side facing it. Demo once with a volunteer: your small sound and gesture, their big version to the back wall. State the rule — giver stays small. |
| 1 | Round one. A gives a tiny offer every few seconds. B receives and sends it out to the wall. Keep calling the pace. A never grows. |
| 1 | Swap without discussion. B gives small, A sends wide. Same tempo. Cut any pair that starts talking about how it went. |
| 1 | Round three. No fixed roles. Whoever gives goes small, whoever receives goes wide. Push for a clean handover roughly every three seconds. |
| 1 | Call freeze. Everyone faces the crowd wall. One shared breath out towards it. Say the cue: serve the show, not your bit. Move straight into the next block. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| Straight after the demo, as round one starts | *"Small hands. Small voice. Give it away."* |
| When givers start performing their own offer | *"Giver, shrink. It's not yours."* |
| When receivers stay polite and contained | *"Back wall. Make the back row see it."* |
| Mid round two, if pace sags | *"Every three seconds. Don't wait for a good one."* |
| Round three, when pairs freeze deciding who goes | *"Nobody's in charge. Just take it."* |
| Any time someone laughs and stops | *"Keep the handover. Laugh and carry on."* |
| Last ten seconds before freeze | *"Freeze. Face the wall."* |
| On the shared breath | *"Breathe it out to them. That's the show."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Twenty people in a row facing one wall, arms and voices getting bigger while the person beside them stays tiny. A steady tick of handovers you can hear across the room. Offers that are obviously nothing — a click, a shrug — landing large. Nobody apologising, nobody checking with their partner. At the freeze, the room is already pointed outwards.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Both players in a pair growing at once, turning it into a duet | They've slipped into mirroring, which is the more familiar exercise. | Stop the room for five seconds. Re-demo the size gap yourself: your offer barely visible, the volunteer's enormous. Restart. |
| Pairs turning to face each other instead of the wall | Default social geometry — people talk face to face. | Call "shoulder to shoulder, both eyes on the wall." Walk the line and physically re-angle a couple of pairs as an example. |
| Long pauses while a giver invents something worth giving | They think the offer has to be good. | Call the three-second tick out loud yourself for fifteen seconds. Praise the dullest offer you can see. |
| Receivers doing a comic version, playing for the laugh of their partner | Old habit of aiming at the nearest person. | Call "back row only." Tell them nobody in this room is the audience. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One line facing the wall, five or six pairs. Run exactly as written. You can watch every pair, so side-coach individuals by name. |
| **14–17** | Two staggered lines facing the same wall, front line half a step to one side so nobody's view is blocked. Coach the room as a whole, not individuals. Keep all timings as written. |
| **18–20** | Two lines, ten pairs. Shorten the demo to forty seconds and start round one early — the extra bodies eat time in pairing up. If numbers are odd, make one trio: one giver, two receivers sending it out together. |

## Variations

**Sound only** — No gesture. The giver makes a small noise, the receiver sends the same noise out big.  
*Easier — removes the choreography problem and gets nervous players moving faster.*

**Double amplify** — Trios in a line. A gives tiny, B takes it medium, C sends it to the wall at full size.  
*Harder — requires reading the size of what you were handed and choosing your own scale.*

**Name the crowd** — Before round three, tell them the crowd wall is a specific house: a packed late show, a half-empty matinee. They adjust size accordingly.  
*Different emphasis — shifts from handover mechanics to calibrating energy to a real room.*

## Debrief

1. **What was harder — staying small, or going big?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone admits staying small was harder because they wanted to finish their own idea. That's the showcase note, in their words.

2. **Did the quality of the offer matter?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* They notice the rubbish offers worked as well as the good ones, because the receiver's commitment did the work. Move on as soon as one person says it.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity. Nobody touches a partner — the handover is visual and vocal only. Voice work is optional: anyone who'd rather not make sound can do gesture alone, and the pair still works. Big gestures can be made seated or with hands only if standing or reaching is uncomfortable. If someone would rather not pair up on arrival, they can stand at the end of the line and send out offers you feed them.

## Why it works

Audience-energy management fails when players aim their work at each other and let the room overhear it. This activity makes the direction of the offer physical: everyone is angled at the wall, so the only place energy can go is out. Splitting the giver and receiver roles separates having an idea from selling one, which is the mechanism behind the cue. If you are the giver, the only way your offer reaches the crowd is through someone else — so you stop protecting it. Three rounds is enough to make the size gap a habit rather than a rule people remember.
