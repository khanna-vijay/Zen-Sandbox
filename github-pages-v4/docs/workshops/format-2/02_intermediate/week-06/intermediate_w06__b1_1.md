# 🧊 Ice-breaker — Borrowed News

![Borrowed News](../img/intermediate_w06__b1_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone tells a partner one small true thing from their week, then hands that news to the room on their partner's behalf.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 06 run-sheet](../week-06.md)

## Why this activity, here

Opens Week 6, the ensemble session. The class arrives from Week 5 still thinking of scenes as things they drive. This puts someone else's material in their mouth before any scene work starts, so 'support' has a physical memory attached by the time you name it. Feeds directly into the support and edit work later in the block — carrying a partner's offer accurately is the same muscle as carrying a scene partner's.

## What students actually learn

- That handling someone else's material carefully is a distinct skill from generating your own.
- That plain, unembellished detail is more interesting in another person's mouth than they expected.
- That listening changes when you know you will have to repeat it.
- Names. Everyone hears fifteen to twenty names spoken aloud with something attached.

## Setup

Room clear enough to cross. No chairs needed. You need a watch or phone with a second hand — the swap calls are tight and you make them by voice. Nothing to hand out, nothing to write.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Pair everyone off. Give the brief: one small true thing from your week, not impressive, actual. Give two examples of your own — what you ate, a parcel you collected. Keep it dull on purpose. |
| 1 | Round one. Call go, both talk at once. At thirty seconds call 'swap'. Remind them to ask one follow-up. Call time at sixty. |
| 1 | Break pairs, new partner across the room. Tell them plainly: you will be reporting this one. Run the same round — go, swap at thirty, one follow-up each, time at sixty. |
| 1 | Circle up fast. Start the round: partner's name, partner's news, one sentence. Keep it moving — five seconds each. Do not let anyone editorialise. |
| 1 | Finish the round. Ask for hands: whose news sounded better coming back. Take the hands. Do not discuss. Straight into the warm-up. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| As you set the brief | *"Boring is correct. Reach for the dullest true thing you have."* |
| Both partners talking at once, round one | *"Both at once. Don't wait your turn."* |
| At thirty seconds | *"Swap. Other one talks."* |
| Just before round two | *"Listen like you'll have to repeat it. Because you will."* |
| During round two follow-ups | *"One question. Ask for the detail you actually want."* |
| First few in the circle report | *"Name first. Then the news. One sentence."* |
| If someone adds a joke or a comment | *"Just the news. It's not yours to improve."* |
| Last three in the circle | *"Keep the pace. Five seconds each."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The circle round moves without stalling. People say a name they learned four minutes ago and get it right. The news items are small and specific — a wrong bus, a burnt lid, a text from a cousin. Reporters deliver them straight, and the room finds several of them quietly funny without anyone trying. Several hands go up at the end. The whole thing lands under five minutes and the energy is warm, not manic.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| People searching for something interesting to say, long pauses in the pairs. | They heard 'true thing' as 'good story'. The bar went up in their heads. | Cut in with your own example mid-round — 'I bought the wrong milk on Tuesday'. Say the bar out loud again: dull is right. |
| Reporters embellishing, adding a punchline, playing the news for a laugh. | Default instinct to entertain. They think a flat delivery will bore the room. | Stop the round briefly. Say: it's their news, deliver it as given. Restart. The next three set the norm. |
| The circle round overruns and you blow the five minutes. | Too many people, no pace pressure, and reporters hesitating on names. | With sixteen or more, split the circle in two and run them simultaneously. Stand between them and call pace. |
| Someone cannot recall their partner's name. | They listened to content, not identity. Common and not a crisis. | Let the partner say their own name from across the circle. Move on immediately. Do not make it a moment. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Reporting round comfortably fits ninety seconds with slack. You can let one or two items breathe. |
| **14–17** | One circle, but call pace from the second speaker onward. Cut anything over one sentence. Keep the closing hands question to five seconds. |
| **18–20** | Split into two circles for the reporting round only, standing at opposite ends of the room. Both run at once. You stand between and call time. Reconvene as one group for the hands question. |

## Variations

**News in your own words** — Reporters may paraphrase freely rather than repeat the sentence. Only the fact must survive.  
*Easier — removes the recall pressure, keeps the carrying.*

**Two hands** — Each reporter delivers their partner's news and one thing the partner asked them in the follow-up.  
*Harder — doubles the load and forces attention onto the question, not just the answer.*

**Silent claim** — After each report, the owner of the news raises a hand only if it was delivered accurately. No talking.  
*Different emphasis — shifts the room's attention from performance to fidelity.*

## Debrief

1. **What was different about listening in the second round?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone names the mechanism plainly — they held onto detail, or asked a better question, because they knew they would have to hand it over. Move on as soon as one person says it.

2. **Whose news sounded better in someone else's mouth?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Hands, and one person saying something like 'I thought mine was nothing'. That's the whole point landed. Don't fish for more.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact. Nobody has to disclose anything personal — restate that a purchase or a bus journey is a full answer. Anyone who would rather not share can report only, and take a pass on the pairs sharing; tell the room that's available before you start. People with hearing difficulty struggle when both partners talk at once — pair them and let that pair alternate rather than overlap. If someone's name is mispronounced in the circle, correct it once, gently, and keep going.

## Why it works

Support work fails when a player treats a partner's offer as raw material to improve. This activity removes the option. The news is factual, it belongs to a named person in the room, and any embellishment is visible to its owner — so the only available move is accurate carrying. 'Follow the follower' means the material leads and both players serve it; here the material is literally not yours, so the instinct to steer has nothing to grip. The two-round structure does the teaching: round one is ordinary chat, round two is chat under the knowledge of transmission, and the difference in quality of attention between them is felt rather than explained.
