# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Rung by Rung

![Rung by Rung](../img/intermediate_w02__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room mimes one ordinary chore, then climbs it five notches without ever changing the chore.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 02 run-sheet](../week-02.md)

## Why this activity, here

First thing on the floor in week two. The session is about base reality and the single deviation from it — which only reads if people can feel the difference between ordinary and heightened in their own bodies. This gives them a five-point scale they can refer to all day, then hands the language to the scene blocks that follow.

## What students actually learn

- They stop treating 'bigger' as 'different' — the chore stays the same while only the intensity moves.
- They get a shared vocabulary. 'Take it to a four' becomes a note anyone in the room understands.
- They feel how small real size actually is, which makes base reality something they can play rather than something they skip.

## Setup

Clear floor. Everyone standing, arms' length apart, facing you or facing outward in a loose circle. No chairs, no props, no partners. Know your third chore before you start.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Explain the five rungs in three sentences. Name the chore: wiping a table. Start everyone at level one and let them wipe at honest, real size for twenty seconds. |
| 1 | Call "two", "three", "four", "five" roughly every twelve seconds. Keep calling the same chore back — same table, same cloth — as the size grows. |
| 1 | Take a chore shout from the room. Everyone rebuilds at one for fifteen seconds, then climbs to five on your calls. |
| 1 | Name a third chore yourself. Climb roughly eight seconds a rung. At five, hold for ten seconds, breathing hard. |
| 1 | Call "back to one". Twenty seconds of silent, real-size work. Shake out arms. Say the cue and move straight on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| At level one, first chore | *"Real size. Nobody's watching you yet."* |
| Moving from two to three | *"Same chore. Just more of it."* |
| Anyone starts inventing new business | *"Don't change the job. Change the size."* |
| At four | *"Let your breath go with it."* |
| At five | *"As big as your body honestly goes. No further."* |
| Holding five | *"Stay in it. Ten more seconds."* |
| On the drop back | *"Back to one. Keep working."* |
| In the silent twenty seconds | *"Feel how small that is. That's your base."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    By the third chore the room climbs together without you having to push. Level five is loud, physical and still recognisably the same chore. When you call back to one, the drop is visible — the room goes quiet and the work gets small and specific. Nobody is trying to be funny.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Level five looks the same as level three — everyone plateaus early. | They spent their whole range in the first two rungs, or they're embarrassed to be the biggest thing in the room. | Stop the climb. Reset at one and tell them one is genuinely small and boring. Then climb again. Call "you've got two more rungs" at three. |
| People add jokes — the cloth catches fire, the shopping bag becomes a baby. | They read 'heighten' as 'escalate the story'. Very common, and it's the exact confusion the session is fixing. | Call "don't change the job". Name it out loud after the round: heightening intensity is not the same as adding events. |
| Half the room is watching the other half. | No sightline discipline — a circle facing in makes people spectate. | Turn everyone outward, or spread them facing you. Say "eyes on your own work". |
| The drop back to one collapses into laughing and chatting. | You released the frame too early. | Hold the silence yourself. Don't speak for the full twenty seconds. Start the next call only when the room settles. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One group, everyone facing outward in a loose ring. Run exactly as written. You can take two chore shouts instead of one in round three if the room is quick. |
| **14–17** | One group, spread in two lines facing you rather than a ring — it stops people watching each other. Keep the rung timings as written; don't add a fourth chore. |
| **18–20** | Two lines facing opposite walls, roughly a metre apart. Cut the third chore's climb to six seconds a rung so the whole thing still lands in five minutes. Take only one chore shout, and take it from someone who hasn't spoken. |

## Variations

**Three rungs only** — Run one, three, five and drop the intermediate levels. Fifteen seconds each.  
*Easier — the jumps are obvious, so nobody has to judge fine gradations while also miming.*

**Silent ladder** — Stop calling numbers. Hold up fingers instead, and change them without warning, including going back down.  
*Harder — they have to watch you and stay in the work, and descending is much harder than climbing.*

**Odd one out** — On the last chore, tap two people as you pass. They stay at one while everyone else climbs to five.  
*Different emphasis — it puts the session's actual cue in the body: one normal thing inside a heightened room, or the reverse.*

## Debrief

1. **What changed between one and five?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Speed, weight, breath, how much space they took. Someone should notice the chore itself never changed. If they only say "it got bigger", push once: bigger how?

2. **What did level one feel like after level five?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Tiny. Boring. Exposed. Some say it felt like doing nothing — that's the useful answer, because base reality feels like doing nothing and still reads.

3. **Who was tempted to add something new at four or five?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Hands go up and someone names the invention they nearly made. Good — you can hand them the distinction between more of the same and something else entirely, then move on.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact, no partners. Say up front that level five means as big as your own body honestly goes — not as big as the person next to you. Anyone with a shoulder, back or knee issue can run the whole ladder seated or scale their five to a comfortable three; tell them this before you start, not after. Check nobody is within swinging distance. Chore suggestions stay ordinary and domestic — redirect anything that heads somewhere loaded.

## Why it works

Heightening & exploration fails when people confuse intensity with novelty: asked to raise the stakes, they add a new event instead of deepening the existing one. This activity removes the option. The chore is fixed, so the only available variable is degree. Doing that in the body, before any scene work, builds a felt scale rather than an idea about one — and because the whole room climbs at once, nobody is performing, so nobody defaults to the joke. The drop back to one is the part that teaches base reality: after five, real size is unmistakable, and that contrast is what makes the one odd thing visible later.
