# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — The Usual

![The Usual](../img/intermediate_w02__b3_2.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs build a shared ordinary routine one detail at a time, then hold on a single exception and question it instead of adding to it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 02 run-sheet](../week-02.md)

## Why this activity, here

First thing in the room on a week about base reality. It comes before any scene work and feeds every scene that follows: the class needs the muscle of building ordinary before they get to build a world. It also plants the week's second half — that one unusual thing is explored, not stacked on.

## What students actually learn

- That saying something boring out loud is allowed, and that agreement on the boring makes the odd thing visible.
- That a single exception is more interesting than five, because it can be asked about.
- That questioning a detail is a form of heightening — the thing grows because you look at it, not because you add to it.

## Setup

No chairs, no props. Standing pairs scattered around the room. Enough space that pairs aren't shouting over each other — if the room is tight, send half the pairs to the walls. Have a way to be heard over twenty people talking: a clap, a bell, or your voice raised once.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Pair the room. Give the brief: alternate one ordinary detail each about a routine you both share. No strange, no funny. Take no questions — start them instead. |
| 1 | Pairs build the routine, one sentence each, swapping. Move between pairs. Side-coach for smaller and more specific. Let it get properly dull. |
| 1 | Call "except". Partner A names one thing that's different today. Both then only ask and answer questions about that thing. Hold them there — no new oddities. |
| 1 | Reset with the same partner. New routine, roughly forty seconds of ordinary, then call "except" for Partner B. Questions only from there. |
| 1 | Let the second exception run about fifteen seconds more, then stop the room. Ask one debrief question, take two answers, move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| First fifteen seconds of the ordinary build | *"Smaller. Duller. Something nobody would mention."* |
| When a pair starts inventing rather than agreeing | *"Agree with what they said, then add one thing."* |
| Just before you call the exception | *"Ten more seconds of nothing happening."* |
| The moment you call it | *"Except. One sentence. One thing."* |
| When a pair adds a second oddity | *"Nothing else is weird. Only that."* |
| When questioning goes flat | *"Ask about it again. Different angle."* |
| Mid-second round | *"Stay curious, not clever."* |
| Ten seconds before stopping | *"Last question. Then hands off."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The room gets quieter and more specific in the first minute, not louder. When you call "except", you hear a small drop — a pause, then one plain sentence. After that the volume comes back as questions, and pairs are leaning in rather than performing outward. Nobody is laughing much. That's fine.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pairs producing surreal details in the first thirty seconds — talking dogs, secret agents. | They think ordinary is boring and boring is failure. Habit from every warm-up they've done. | Stop the room for five seconds. Say: "Nothing has happened yet in your routine. Nothing happens for another minute." Restart. |
| After "except", the pair adds a second and third strange thing and the scene runs away. | They're heightening by accumulation, which is the default. The rule didn't land. | Walk to them and say the rule again, short: "Only that. Ask about it." Don't explain why mid-round. |
| Questions turn into interrogation — rapid-fire, no answers, no build. | One partner has taken the questioner role and the other is just receiving. | Call "answer, then ask back" to the whole room. Both sides ask. |
| A pair stalls in silence after the exception. | They picked an exception that's too abstract to ask about — "something feels off". | Drop in and say: "Name it as an object or an action." Give one if they need it. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | Straight pairs, everyone plays, all timings as written. With an odd number, make one three: A names the exception, both others question it, then rotate for round two. |
| **14–17** | Same structure. Split the room physically — half to one end, half to the other — so pairs can hear each other. Cut the ordinary build in round two to thirty seconds; the room is louder and attention drops faster. |
| **18–20** | Pairs still, nobody watches, but call your cues twice — once to each half of the room — or half the pairs will miss "except". Shorten the first build to fifty seconds to protect the second round. |

## Variations

**Named routine** — Give every pair the same setting before they start — the bus stop, the office kitchen, the Sunday phone call. They only invent details, not the frame.  
*easier — removes the negotiation about what the routine is, which is where slow pairs lose their first thirty seconds*

**No questions, only noticing** — After "except", ban question forms. They may only state what they observe about the odd thing: "It's warm." "You've moved it." "That wasn't there Tuesday."  
*harder — takes away the easy engine and forces genuine attention to detail*

**Third eye** — Run in threes. Two build and question; the third stays silent, then afterwards names in one sentence what the odd thing turned out to be about.  
*different emphasis — shifts from generating to reading, and shows players what an outside eye actually picks up*

**Delayed except** — Don't call "except" at a fixed time. Wait until you hear a pair say something genuinely specific, then call it for the whole room.  
*different emphasis — rewards specificity and makes the ordinary build feel like it matters*

## Debrief

1. **Which was harder — the ordinary bit or the one odd thing?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone says the ordinary was harder, and can say why: they kept wanting to make it interesting. That's the point landed. Move on.

2. **What happened to the odd thing when you kept asking about it?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* "It got bigger without us adding anything." Or "we found out what it meant to us." Anything that separates growth from accumulation. Take two, stop.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact. Pairs stand and talk. Anyone who'd rather not stand can sit and pair with someone sitting beside them. If someone doesn't want to invent a personal routine, tell them to use a stranger's — two people who work the same shift. Volume can be hard for anyone with hearing difficulty: put those pairs nearest you and give your cues with a hand signal as well as a voice.

## Why it works

D3.S2 asks players to heighten by exploring rather than escalating. The exercise separates the two mechanically. The first minute forces agreement on a shared normal, which costs nothing and gives the odd thing something to be odd against. Then the rule — one exception, questions only — removes escalation as an option. The only move left is attention. Players discover that a detail deepens when examined, and that they didn't need a bigger idea, they needed a longer look at the one they had.
