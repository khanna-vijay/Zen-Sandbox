# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Beat, Beat, Break

![Beat, Beat, Break](../img/intermediate_w04__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room drills a three-move body rhythm in unison, then absorbs a deliberate break into the pattern and keeps going.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 04 run-sheet](../week-04.md)

## Why this activity, here

Opens Week 4. The session is about platform, tilt and what follows. This puts both halves in the body before anyone has to think about them: a routine the room builds together, a break dropped into it, and the pattern carrying on with the break absorbed. Everything later in the session — scene platforms, the first unusual thing, the spine that runs from it — refers back to this.

## What students actually learn

- That a break in the pattern is only interesting because the pattern was solid first.
- That the group absorbs a change faster by copying than by discussing it.
- That wobble after a tilt is normal and recoverable — you keep going, you don't restart.

## Setup

Clear floor, everyone standing in a circle, arms' length apart. No chairs, no props. Check the floor takes a stomp — carpet is fine, hollow staging is loud but workable. You stand in the circle, not outside it, and do every move with them.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Circle up. Teach stomp–clap–HUP. Run it eight times slow, counting out loud, until every body in the room is on the same beat. Fix stragglers by slowing down, not by stopping. |
| 1 | Drive the tempo. Four rounds, each faster than the last, counting over the top. Push volume as well as speed. End this minute with the room loud and locked. |
| 1 | Call 'TILT' and point at one person. On the next cycle they add a move on beat four. Everyone copies it that same cycle. Run the four-beat version six times at speed. |
| 1 | Tilt a second person onto beat five. Copy immediately. Six cycles. Tilt a third onto beat six. Six cycles. Do not stop to teach the moves — the pattern teaches them. |
| 1 | One or two more tilts until the pattern is seven or eight beats. Then run the whole thing twice at full volume and cut the room dead. Hold three seconds of silence. Move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During the first slow eight | *"Same foot. Same beat. Listen more than you look."* |
| As you push tempo | *"Louder than you think. The volume holds the timing."* |
| The instant after a tilt | *"Copy now — don't watch, copy."* |
| When someone hesitates before adding their move | *"Any move. First one. Go."* |
| When the pattern gets ragged around beat six | *"Through it. Nobody restarts."* |
| When the group starts talking over the rhythm | *"No words. Body only."* |
| On the last two full runs | *"Full size. All of it."* |
| On the cut | *"Stop. Hold. Hold."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The room is loud, slightly out of breath, and unified without anyone leading. New moves get absorbed within one cycle. When the pattern wobbles, people push through and re-sync on the next stomp rather than looking around for permission. The final silence lands hard.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The tilted person freezes and adds nothing, so the cycle collapses. | They're hunting for a clever move. | Call 'any move, first one, go' before they have time to think. If they still stall, add a move for them and point at the next person immediately. Never wait. |
| The pattern degrades into everyone doing slightly different things by beat six. | Tempo pushed too high too early, so nobody can see the new moves land. | Drop the tempo by a third for one cycle, let it re-sync, then push again. Speed is a tool here, not the goal. |
| People stop to ask what the move was. | They think accuracy matters more than continuity. | Call 'no words, body only'. Tell them afterwards that an approximate copy on time beats an exact copy late. |
| Half the room is doing it small and quiet. | Self-consciousness in the first ten minutes of a session. | Do it bigger yourself and name volume, not confidence: 'louder than you think'. Do not single anyone out. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Tilt four people, ending at a seven-beat pattern. You have room to run seven or eight cycles between tilts. |
| **14–17** | One circle, wider. Tilt four people but cut to five cycles between tilts to protect the timing. Point clearly — at this size people mistake who you meant. |
| **18–20** | Split into two circles of nine or ten, each with its own pattern; you run one and hand the count of the other to a confident participant. Three tilts each, six-beat pattern. Finish by cutting both circles together. |

## Variations

**Fixed menu** — Before you start, offer three legal tilt moves — a spin, a knee slap, a shout of 'HA'. The tilted person picks one.  
*Easier. Removes invention entirely for groups that freeze on the first tilt.*

**Silent tilt** — Stop calling 'TILT'. Anyone may add a move on the next open beat, any time. The room absorbs whatever appears.  
*Harder. Forces peripheral attention and shared responsibility for when the break comes.*

**Break and justify** — After each tilt, the tilting person says four words naming what the pattern now is — 'now we are marching'. The room keeps the rhythm underneath.  
*Different emphasis. Shifts from copying the break to making sense of it, which is where the session is heading.*

## Debrief

1. **What made the tilts easy to absorb, and what made them hard?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone says the clear, locked platform made the new move obvious — and that when the base was already ragged, the new move just added noise. That's the whole session in one sentence.

2. **What did you do when you lost the pattern?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* 'I picked it up on the next stomp' or 'I copied whoever I could see.' Not 'I stopped and waited.' If you hear a lot of stopping, name it and move on.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact. Stomping is optional — offer a hand slap on the thigh as an equal-value substitute and say so before you start, not after. Anyone with knee, hip or back issues should scale the stomp down or sit in a chair inside the circle and do the pattern with arms and voice only. Anyone who prefers not to be pointed at for a tilt can say so at the start; you'll skip them without comment. Loud shouting is part of it — flag it for anyone sensitive to noise, and let them step to the edge for the fast rounds.

## Why it works

Narrative architecture depends on the audience recognising a norm before it can register a break. This puts the norm in the muscles: eight slow repetitions build a pattern nobody has to think about, so when the tilt arrives it registers as an event rather than as confusion. The rule that the room copies immediately, without discussion, is what makes the break become the new normal — which is exactly the move a scene makes when the unusual thing gets accepted and built on rather than questioned. Running the tilts repeatedly, without restarts, teaches that a spine survives disruption by absorbing it, not by returning to the start.
