# 😄 Laughter Blast — Down We Go

![Down We Go](../img/intermediate_w04__b2_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room shuffles in a circle chanting nonsense, then drops to a crouch together and laughs on the floor.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 04 run-sheet](../week-04.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 4 is about platform, tilt and return. The warm-up before this settled the room; the scene work after it asks for a stable ordinary world that gets broken on purpose. This puts that shape in the body — repeat, break, absorb, resume — with no story to manage yet.

## What students actually learn

- A break lands harder when the thing it breaks was boring and steady.
- They can take a disruption together without anyone having to lead it.
- Volume, tempo and pitch are dials they can turn on command, not personality traits.

## Setup

Clear floor space for a standing circle with a metre between people. No chairs in the ring. Check nobody is holding a drink or a phone. Note anyone who tells you crouching is a problem before you start.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Demonstrate loudly yourself — shuffle, chant "wibble wobble", call "AND DOWN", crouch, laugh. Then form the circle and get them shuffling and chanting with you. |
| 1 | Round one at normal speed. Call "AND DOWN" after ten seconds. Crouch, laugh five seconds, stand, reverse direction. Run it twice. |
| 1 | Round two, slow motion. Low drone chant, drop over a count of five, land and laugh slow and deep. Two drops. |
| 1 | Round three, fast. Double-speed chant, high voices, drops every five seconds. Four or five drops, no gap between landing, laughing and standing. |
| 1 | Final round in a whisper — whispered chant, whispered laugh. Last call: full-volume drop and laugh for ten seconds. Stand. Ask one question, take shouted answers, move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| As the first chant starts and people are still mumbling | *"Louder than feels sensible."* |
| Mid-chant, before any drop | *"Keep it boring. Same speed, same volume."* |
| The call itself, every time | *"AND DOWN!"* |
| In the crouch, if the laughing dies after two seconds | *"Keep laughing. Don't wait for funny."* |
| Coming out of a crouch | *"Up and straight back in. Other way now."* |
| Slow-motion round | *"Lower the pitch. Take the whole five counts."* |
| Fast round, when it starts to scatter | *"Together. Same breath."* |
| Whisper round, just before the final call | *"Quieter. Quieter. AND DOWN — full volume!"* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The chant is dull and unified, the drop is sharp and simultaneous, and the laugh starts before anyone decides to laugh. People are red-faced and slightly out of breath by the whisper round. The final full-volume drop is loud enough that you notice the neighbouring room.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| A thin, half-hearted chant with people watching each other's mouths. | You demonstrated too quietly or explained instead of doing it. | Stop. Do the chant alone at full volume for five seconds, then restart. Your commitment sets the ceiling. |
| The drop is ragged — people go down over two or three seconds. | Your call is buried under the chant. | Call it on a rising pitch and cut through. Land "DOWN" hard on the beat. |
| Laughter in the crouch stops instantly and people look up at you. | They are treating the laugh as something to be earned. | Say "fake it, it counts" and hold the crouch two seconds longer than they want. |
| One or two people standing outside the ring watching. | Knees, self-consciousness, or an unclear opt-out. | Bring them in on the chant only — shuffling and chanting is full participation. Give them a bend-at-the-waist version of the drop. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Run every round as written, including two drops in slow motion. You may fit an extra fast drop in round three. |
| **14–17** | One circle, wider. Shorten the chant before each call to about eight seconds so the round count holds. Stand in the ring, not the centre, so the call reaches both sides. |
| **18–20** | Split into two concentric circles shuffling in opposite directions — outer ring left, inner ring right. Same chant, same call for both. Cut round two to a single slow drop to protect the clock. |

## Variations

**Chant only** — Drop the crouch entirely. On "AND DOWN" everyone stops dead and laughs standing still, then resumes.  
*Easier — no floor work, no knees, and still teaches the break-and-resume shape.*

**Their call** — After round two, hand the call to the circle. Anyone may shout "AND DOWN" at any point. Nobody may call twice.  
*Harder — they must risk the initiation and the group must follow an unfamiliar voice instantly.*

**Name the break** — After each crouch, the first person to speak shouts one word explaining why everyone fell over. Everyone repeats it once and carries on chanting.  
*Shifts emphasis from physical unison towards justification — the second half of the cue.*

## Debrief

1. **Which drop landed hardest — and what was the chant doing just before it?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone says the whisper one, because everything before it was tiny and level. That connects the size of the break to the flatness of the platform. Move on.

2. **Did anyone decide to laugh?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Shouts of "no" and "it just happened". Enough. You are not looking for insight, you are marking that commitment came before feeling.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact at any point — keep a metre between people and say so before the first shuffle. Announce the knee option up front: anyone who prefers can bend at the waist, sit, or simply stop and laugh standing. That is full participation, not a lesser version. Watch for shuffling into furniture. Loud sustained laughing can trigger coughing or light-headedness — tell people to step out of the ring and breathe if that happens, and carry on without commenting on it.

## Why it works

Narrative architecture runs on contrast: a tilt only reads as a tilt against an established routine. Here the chant is a platform stripped of content — repetitive, unvarying, deliberately dull — so the drop registers purely as structure. Because the break is called from outside, nobody has to invent it, and the whole group experiences the same rupture at the same instant. Repeating it across four tempos means they feel that the size of the break depends on the flatness of what preceded it. That bodily knowledge transfers directly into scene work, where the temptation is to break the routine before the routine exists.
