# 🏟️ Showcase round — Five Tellings

![Five Tellings](../img/intermediate_w07__b9_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> One audience suggestion becomes a plain two-minute story, then the same story is retold four more times in genres the audience picks.

`Players 10–20` · `~30 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact incidental` · `Props: required`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 07 run-sheet](../week-07.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 7 is the match-play week: the room stops being a class and starts being an audience. Earlier blocks drilled genre and callback in private. This is the showcase, where the anchors give nervous players something solid to hold while the audience's laughter teaches them what actually landed. It feeds the final weeks, where students run scenes without a safety line.

## What students actually learn

- That the same three facts can carry five completely different tempers, so genre is a costume and not a new story.
- That they can hear an audience — where it leans in, where it goes quiet — while still playing the scene.
- That the laugh comes from the anchor arriving on time, not from working harder at being funny.
- That a plain, unfunny opening telling is not a wasted two minutes; it is the thing everything else pushes against.

## Setup

Chairs in a rough arc facing a clear playing space. Whiteboard or flipchart visible to players and audience, marker to hand. A hat with slips and pencils by the door. Genre cards face down on a stool — write fifteen or so beforehand (horror, nature documentary, courtroom, silent film, telenovela, heist, children's bedtime, disaster movie, period romance, sports commentary). A bell, chime or hand drum for the Stack. Appoint three roles before doors open: Story Keeper (writes and guards the anchors), Genre Caller (draws and announces), tech (bell and lights). If the class is the audience, put non-playing students in the arc and rotate them in.

## How to run it — 30 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 3 | Set the room. Slips already collected in the hat. Host welcomes, states the shape — one story, five tellings — draws three slips, reads them, takes applause. Loudest suggestion wins. |
| 4 | Plain Telling. Three players. No genre, no gags. They fix who they are to each other, where they are, and what one of them wants. Cut it clean at four minutes. |
| 2 | Story Keeper writes the three anchors on the board and reads them aloud: one spoken line, one object, one relationship. Audience should be able to see them all night. |
| 5 | Genre Caller draws card one. Fresh cast of three retells the same story in that genre, holding all three anchors. Two and a half minutes, then bell. |
| 5 | Draw card two. New cast of three. Same story, same anchors, new temper. Bell at two and a half minutes. Reread anchors between casts if they are drifting. |
| 4 | Draw cards three and four back to back, two minutes each. Cast everyone who has not yet played. Do not stop for notes. |
| 5 | The Stack. Whole company replays all five tellings, sixty seconds each, in order, cut by the bell. Land on the Plain Telling's last line. |
| 2 | One line of bows, tech and front of house included. Host names the three anchors once more, thanks the room, lights up. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During the Plain Telling, when someone reaches for a joke | *"Plain. Just tell it."* |
| When the first genre cast starts inventing new plot | *"Same story. New weather."* |
| When an anchor is about to be dropped | *"Where's the object?"* |
| When a player pushes for a laugh that isn't coming | *"Earn it. Don't beg."* |
| When the room laughs and the cast plays over it | *"Let it land."* |
| When a genre cast is playing the label rather than the people | *"Genre in the body. Truth in the face."* |
| Entering the Stack | *"Sixty seconds. Anchors only."* |
| Final beat of the Stack | *"Take us home on the line."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The audience recognises the anchor line before it arrives and laughs on the recognition, not on the joke. Each genre cast plays the same three facts with a completely different tempo. Players cut cleanly on the bell without protest. By the Stack, the whole room is ahead of the story, and the last plain line lands quiet and clean rather than as a punchline.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| The Plain Telling gets big laughs and the genre versions feel flat afterwards. | The first cast played for comedy, so there was nowhere left to escalate. | Cut them at two minutes and say the plain telling is scaffolding. If it happens anyway, name it to the room and instruct the next cast to play the anchors dead straight. |
| Each genre cast invents a new plot; the audience loses the thread. | Players treat the anchors as suggestions rather than fixed points. | Reread the three anchors aloud immediately before every cast enters. Side-coach 'Same story' the moment a new character appears. |
| Genre versions are all the same speed and volume — loud, fast, shouty. | Adrenaline. Players equate performing with energy. | Deliberately call a slow genre — nature documentary, silent film, bedtime story — and hold the bell an extra thirty seconds so they have to fill quiet. |
| The Stack collapses; nobody remembers which telling is which. | Too many players on stage, no one leading each sixty seconds. | Before the Stack, name one player per telling as its lead. Everyone else supports. The bell is absolute. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | Casts of three, four genres, everyone plays at least once and most play twice. Non-playing students sit in the arc as audience and must applaud the suggestion choice. Keep the Stack at sixty seconds each. |
| **14–17** | Casts of three for the plain telling and first two genres, casts of four for the last two so everyone gets on. Track who has played on the back of the board. Stack stays five tellings, sixty seconds each. |
| **18–20** | Casts of four throughout, and run genres three and four simultaneously as split-stage — two casts, stage left and stage right, ninety seconds each, bell between. Everyone plays once. For the Stack, name one lead per telling and cap the stage at six bodies. |

## Variations

**Two Tellings** — Plain telling plus two genres only. Give each five minutes instead of two and a half, and skip the Stack. Bows at the end.  
*easier — fewer transitions, more time to find the story, better for a group that is still nervous about being watched*

**Audience Calls the Genre** — Drop the cards. The host takes genre shouts from the room between each telling and picks the first clear one heard.  
*harder — players get no warning and no vetted list, and must build the temper from a word they may not know well*

**Anchor Swap** — Before the fourth telling, the Story Keeper rubs out one anchor and the audience supplies a replacement on the spot. All later tellings use the new set.  
*different emphasis — shifts the work from genre transformation to holding a moving target, and shows the room how much a single fixed detail was carrying*

## Debrief

1. **Which telling got the biggest laugh, and what was in the room before it that made that laugh possible?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone points at a specific earlier moment — 'we'd heard that line four times' — rather than praising the performer. They describe the laugh as owed rather than earned by wit.

2. **When did you notice the audience and what did you do about it?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* A concrete moment: 'they went quiet so I slowed down', or 'they laughed and I waited'. Not 'I was too nervous to notice' — if that's the answer, ask who did notice and what they heard.

3. **What did the plain telling give you that a funny opening wouldn't have?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something about having a floor to push off — room to escalate, a shared reference the audience trusted, three facts they could hold when their mind went blank.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No physical contact is required. If a genre suggests it — romance, wrestling, disaster — say before the round that contact is optional and any player may play it at arm's length. Anyone can stand out of a round without giving a reason; keep two chairs at the side for that. Vet the suggestion slips before the draw and quietly bin anything targeting a person in the room or naming a real individual. Genre cards are pre-written by you, so screen out anything that leans on accents or national caricature. Tell the group at the top that no one has to be funny to take part — the anchors do most of the work.

## Why it works

Room Reading needs a stable signal to read against. Because the story stays fixed and only the genre changes, every audience reaction is attributable: the players know the laugh came from the tempo, the timing or the anchor, not from a new joke. Repetition builds audience recognition, so by the third telling the room is anticipating rather than following, and the laugh arrives on the anchor's return. That is what earning it feels like from the inside — and once a player has felt the difference between a laugh they set up and a laugh they chased, begging stops being tempting.
