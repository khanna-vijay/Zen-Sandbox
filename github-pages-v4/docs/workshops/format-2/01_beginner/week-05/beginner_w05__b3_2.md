# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Heavy Little Gift

![Heavy Little Gift](../img/beginner_w05__b3_2.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs hand invisible objects back and forth, and the receiver's body has to prove the weight before they take it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 05 run-sheet](../week-05.md)

## Why this activity, here

First thing in a week about place and body. Before anyone builds a room, they have to prove one object exists. It feeds the larger environment work later in the session, where the same discipline — consistent size, weight and height — has to hold across a whole space rather than a single handover.

## What students actually learn

- They stop miming with their hands only and start using shoulders, knees and breath to carry weight.
- They begin watching a partner's body for information instead of waiting for words.
- They feel the difference between showing an object and describing one, which is the week's cue in miniature.

## Setup

Clear floor with pairs spaced so nobody swings into anyone else — about two metres between partners and a metre between pairs. No props, no chairs in the working area. You need a clear voice call for the freeze. Have a slip of floor at the front for your demo so all can see.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Pair everyone off at two metres. Demo the handover once — lift, carry, offer, wait for their hands, release. Name the rule: nobody lets go until the other pair of hands has arrived. |
| 1 | Round one. A gives, B receives and holds the weight for two seconds, then gives back something different. Silent. Keep the swap going continuously. |
| 1 | Round two. Same swap, but the receiver's hands must meet the object at the exact size and height offered. If it slips, both freeze and re-take it before continuing. |
| 1 | Round three. Givers go extreme — feather-light or almost unliftable. Receivers show the strain or the lack of it in the legs, back and breath, not just the wrists. |
| 1 | Call for one final object each way, then call freeze. Everyone holds their object still. Ten seconds looking round the room. Shake it out and move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| Thirty seconds into round one | *"Wait for their hands. Don't drop it."* |
| When handovers get fast and sloppy | *"Two seconds. Feel the weight before you pass."* |
| Start of round two | *"Same size in, same size out."* |
| When an object shrinks mid-pass | *"Freeze. Re-take it."* |
| Round three, when only wrists are working | *"Legs. Back. Breath."* |
| Mid round three | *"Show me the weight, don't tell me."* |
| Just before the final call | *"One more each way."* |
| On the freeze | *"Hold it. Look around."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Knees bend. Shoulders drop when something heavy lands. Hands arrive at roughly the right height and stay there. Objects come back the same size they went out. The room is quiet apart from breath and the odd laugh, and pairs are watching each other rather than watching their own hands.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Objects handed over instantly, no hold, hands flapping at chest height every time. | They are performing the idea of a mime rather than tracking a physical thing. | Stop the room for five seconds. Say: two seconds of weight, every time. Restart and count the first two aloud with them. |
| People naming the objects or mouthing what they are. | Discomfort with silence; wanting the partner to get it right. | Call out that nobody has to guess correctly. The rule is silence — the body does the naming. Let the mismatch happen. |
| Weight shown only in the face — grimacing, no body change. | Habit from cartoon mime and self-consciousness about looking odd. | Side-coach the legs specifically: bend the knees, shift your feet. Demo one heavy lift yourself, exaggerated, so permission is visible. |
| A pair chatting and laughing, not passing. | They have decided the exercise is silly and opted out socially. | Walk over, stand near them, say nothing, wait. If it continues, hand them an object yourself and take one back. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One large circle of pairs, everyone working at once. You can watch every pair. Add fifteen seconds of coaching inside round three by shortening the setup demo. |
| **14–17** | Two loose clusters of pairs on opposite sides of the room so you can sweep between them. Keep all five rounds at one minute. Demo once, centrally, before splitting. |
| **18–20** | Three clusters. Demo from the middle of the room so all can see. Give your side-coaching calls loudly to the whole room rather than to individual pairs; you will not reach everyone up close in five minutes. |

## Variations

**Named object** — The giver says one word — 'brick', 'egg' — as they offer it. Everything else stays the same.  
*Easier. Removes the guessing load so attention goes entirely to weight and size.*

**Third object** — Work in threes. The object must travel A to B to C and back, arriving the same size it left.  
*Harder. The object has to survive three sets of hands, so consistency is public and errors are obvious.*

**Awkward shapes** — Drop the weight instruction. Objects must be an awkward shape instead — long, wide, floppy, sharp.  
*Different emphasis. Shifts from weight to dimension and how the whole body negotiates space around an object.*

## Debrief

1. **What told you what you'd been handed?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something concrete about the partner's body — 'his knees went', 'she held it out flat so I knew it was wide' — rather than 'I just guessed'.

2. **Where in your body did the heavy things live?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Naming a part below the neck. Legs, back, shoulders. If everyone says 'my hands', that is your note for the rest of the session.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact between people — hands come close but never touch. Say that out loud before you start. Anyone with shoulder, back or wrist trouble can keep every object light and small; make that offer to the room, not to an individual. Anyone who wants to sit out can hold the count for a pair instead. Nobody has to lift, crouch or kneel; weight can be shown in the upper body alone.

## Why it works

Physicality and space work fails when the body is treated as a delivery system for description. This exercise removes speech, so the only channel left is the body, and it puts a second person in the loop who has to read what the body produced. The two-second hold forces a decision about weight rather than a gesture. The re-take rule in round two makes inconsistency visible and cheap to correct. By round three the extremes recruit the legs and breath, which is where believable weight actually lives. Five minutes is enough because the loop is short and repeats twenty times.
