# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Under Your Breath

![Under Your Breath](../img/beginner_w02__b3_3.jpg){ .infographic }

> Standing still with eyes closed, the room whispers its first association to each word on a shared exhale, then holds the silence.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 02 run-sheet](../week-02.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 2 opens the door on unfiltered response. This is the first thing they do — before anyone has spoken alone, before anyone can be watched. It lowers the cost of a first thought to almost nothing, then hands that lowered cost to the louder, more exposed word games that follow in the cluster.

## What students actually learn

- That a first association arrives whether they invite it or not — the work is releasing it, not producing it.
- That speaking without vetting feels survivable, because the first time they try it nobody can pick their voice out.
- That silence after an offer is a place to stand, not a hole to fill.

## Setup

Clear floor space for a standing circle with arm's length between people. No chairs in the ring. Have twelve to fifteen plain concrete words ready on a card in your hand — you will not think of them under pressure. Use: bread, river, mother, iron, Tuesday, glove; then window, salt, dog, engine, blue, hallway; then hand, rain, door. Know where the clock is. No props.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Form the circle standing, feet planted. Eyes closed or gaze down. Lead three slow breaths together. Then demonstrate once: say "bread", and on the next out-breath everyone whispers their association at the same time. |
| 2 | Round one, eyes closed. Call six words — bread, river, mother, iron, Tuesday, glove — one every fifteen seconds. Whisper on the exhale. Hold three seconds of full stillness before each new word. |
| 1 | Eyes open, soft gaze across the circle rather than at any one face. Six more words — window, salt, dog, engine, blue, hallway — same rhythm, same whisper, feet still. |
| 1 | Three final words with no breathing gap: hand, rain, door. Call, whisper, call, whisper, call, whisper. Then hold one long silence, ten seconds. Give the cue and move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During the three opening breaths | *"Feet still. Shoulders down."* |
| After the demo whisper | *"Whisper only. Nobody is heard alone."* |
| Two or three words into round one | *"Take the first one. Don't shop."* |
| In the three-second hold | *"Hold the silence. Let it sit."* |
| As eyes open for round two | *"Soft gaze. Nobody in particular."* |
| If whispers start climbing in volume | *"Back to breath. Under your breath."* |
| During the fast final three | *"No thinking room. Straight out."* |
| On the last long silence | *"Stay here. Don't fill it."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    A low, overlapping hiss on each exhale, arriving all at once and dying together. Bodies stay put. The three-second holds feel long to you and are not broken by shuffling or laughter. By round two, people are looking softly across the circle and still whispering at the same moment without you counting them in. The room is quieter at the end than at the start.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Whispers arriving in a ragged trickle over four or five seconds instead of together. | People are editing — waiting for a better word than the one that came. | Call "Straight out — on the breath, not after it." Then re-lead one shared out-breath so they have a clear beat to land on. |
| Giggling, especially in the silences, and volume creeping up to normal speech. | Eyes-closed standing silence is uncomfortable, and laughing is the exit. | unused. |
| Somebody standing with lips shut, not whispering at all. | Self-consciousness, or they genuinely didn't hear the word. | Don't single them out. Say to the room "Any word counts — repeat the word back if nothing comes." Give them a floor they can stand on. |
| The five minutes run to seven because you're waiting for perfect stillness. | Chasing a quality of quiet this group can't reach in week two. | Take what you get and keep the fifteen-second beat. The rhythm does the work, not the quality of the hush. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Run as written. With ten, the whisper is thin and individual voices are faintly identifiable — say so up front and drop your own word list to a whisper too, so you're inside the sound rather than above it. |
| **14–17** | One circle, as written. Ideal size. The overlap is thick enough that nobody is exposed. Keep the fifteen-second beat exactly; don't rush because the room is bigger. |
| **18–20** | One circle still — do not split, the mass of sound is the point. Widen the ring so nobody is shoulder to shoulder. Project your words slightly harder and cut round two to five words so you finish inside five minutes. |

## Variations

**Word given back** — Instead of an association, everyone whispers the called word itself back on the exhale.  
*Easier — removes production entirely, keeps only the shared breath and the stillness. Use with a very nervous group in the first minute, then switch to associations.*

**Second thought banned aloud** — Add a rule: if a second word arrives before you whisper, say the first one anyway. Call "first one" between words.  
*Harder — makes the editing visible to each person and forces them to notice the swap they were about to make.*

**Whisper to a neighbour** — Round two, turn to the person on your left and whisper your association to them rather than to the room. No response, no discussion.  
*Different emphasis — shifts from anonymity to being heard by exactly one person, bridging towards the exposed work later in the session.*

## Debrief

1. **What happened between the word landing and your whisper?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone naming the swap out loud — that a word came instantly, they judged it boring or too revealing, and reached for another. Once one person says it, others recognise it. Move on.

2. **Which was harder, eyes closed or eyes open?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Split answers, with reasons. Some found closed eyes exposing because there was nowhere to look; others found open eyes harder because of being seen. Either is fine — you want them noticing where the self-consciousness actually lives.

3. **What did the three seconds of silence feel like?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* "Longer than three seconds." "I wanted to fill it." That admission is the whole point — flag it as something they'll meet again in scenes today.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact. Nobody touches anyone. Standing is not required — offer chairs at the edge of the ring for anyone who wants one, and say so before you start, not as an afterthought. Eyes closed is optional throughout; a dropped gaze works identically. If someone finds shared breathing exercises uncomfortable, they can skip the breaths and simply stand. Words are deliberately plain and domestic; avoid anything that might pull up loss or trauma. Anyone may stay silent for any word with no explanation required — say this once at the top.

## Why it works

D1.S1 is about closing the gap between the thought arriving and the thought leaving the mouth. That gap is held open by the fear of being judged for what comes out. This exercise removes the judge: twelve people whispering at once produce a sound with no author. Nobody can be attributed, so there is nothing to defend, so there is no reason to edit. What remains is the physical experience of a first association leaving the body unvetted — repeated fifteen times in five minutes. The three-second holds do the second job: they make silence a normal part of the rhythm rather than a failure, so later in the session a pause in a scene doesn't read as panic. The final unbroken three remove even the breath as thinking room, which is the closest thing to the speed of an actual scene.
