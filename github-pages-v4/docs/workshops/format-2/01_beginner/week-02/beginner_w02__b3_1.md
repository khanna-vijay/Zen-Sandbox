# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Three, Two, Shape

![Three, Two, Shape](../img/beginner_w02__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> Count the room down, call a word, and everyone throws their whole body into the first shape it suggests.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 02 run-sheet](../week-02.md)

## Why this activity, here

Opens Week 2. The room has arrived cold and self-conscious, and the week's whole argument is that the first thought is usable. This puts bodies ahead of judgement before anyone has to speak in a scene. It feeds directly into the word-association and first-offer work later in the session, where students will need a physical memory of having answered fast and survived it.

## What students actually learn

- That their body produces an answer before their head has vetted it, and the answer is fine.
- That being the same as someone else costs nothing — copying a neighbour is a legitimate move, not a failure.
- That sound and shape can arrive together instead of one waiting politely for the other.

## Setup

Clear floor, everyone standing, arm's length apart in a scatter — no circle, so nobody is performing to a ring of faces. Have twenty words ready on a slip in your hand: concrete, obvious nouns and verbs (kettle, wolf, avalanche, spaghetti, statue, sprint, jellyfish, hinge). No music. No chairs to retreat to.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Scatter the room. Everyone jogs on the spot. Demonstrate once yourself: jog, "three, two", call "kettle", freeze whole-body, hold a beat, jog on. Say nothing about quality. |
| 1 | Run six words at a steady pace. Everyone jogs between calls. Call out for whole-body shapes if you see hands only — legs, spine, face. |
| 1 | Run six more words. On each freeze, add one sound the shape would make. Coach for simultaneity: sound and shape land on the same beat. |
| 1 | Speed the count. Call eight words with barely a jog between. Announce that repeating a shape is fine and copying a neighbour is fine. |
| 1 | Stop the jog. Shake out arms, legs, jaw. Land the cue in one sentence: be obvious, be average, the first thought is the gift. Move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| First jog, before the first word | *"Feet moving. Don't plan ahead."* |
| After the first freeze | *"Whole body. Legs in it, face in it."* |
| Second round, if shapes look designed | *"Take the first one. It's already right."* |
| When sound is added | *"Sound and shape together. Same beat."* |
| Fast round | *"Faster than thinking. Go."* |
| If someone hesitates and looks around | *"Copy your neighbour. That's allowed."* |
| Last two words | *"Average is the target. Don't improve it."* |
| On the shake-out | *"Shake it out. Jaw too."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Bodies commit fully within half a second of the word, and the shapes are unremarkable — three people doing roughly the same kettle. Sounds are blunt and loud enough to hear across the room. Nobody is watching anybody else for permission. The fast round is slightly chaotic and people are breathing hard. Laughter is incidental, not performed.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Shapes made only with hands and forearms, feet still planted. | People are protecting themselves by keeping the shape small and deniable. | Call "legs in it" between words and demonstrate one enormous shape yourself mid-round. Don't stop the exercise to explain. |
| A visible pause after the word while people decide, then a shape. | They are treating it as a test with a right answer. | Speed the count immediately. Cut the gap between "two" and the word. Tell them a repeated shape scores the same as a clever one. |
| Sounds are thin, half-swallowed, or arrive after the shape. | Volume feels more exposing than movement, so it gets postponed. | Call "same beat" and let one word be sound-only from a frozen shape, so they hear the room together. |
| Two or three people jogging but not freezing at all. | Physical self-consciousness, or an injury they haven't mentioned. | Offer a seated or small-scale version out loud, to the whole room, without singling anyone out. Keep running. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One scatter. Keep the word count as written: six, six, eight. You can call words slightly slower because the room reads quieter — but do not add extra rounds. |
| **14–17** | One scatter, but spread them wider so nobody is shoulder to shoulder. Same word counts. Move around the outside of the group as you call, so your voice reaches the back. |
| **18–20** | One scatter in the largest floor space available. Drop to five, five, eight words so the block still lands in five minutes — the freeze takes longer to settle with twenty bodies. Project the count. |

## Variations

**Shapes only, no jog** — Everyone stands still instead of jogging. Count "three, two", call the word, freeze.  
*easier — removes the cardio and the coordination load for a room with mixed mobility or low energy*

**Partner echo** — After each freeze, everyone finds the nearest person and copies their shape exactly for one beat before jogging on.  
*harder — adds a fast observation and reproduction task on top of the first-thought demand*

**Two-word collision** — Call two unrelated words at once — "wolf, kettle" — and let the body resolve them into one shape.  
*different emphasis — shifts from pure first-thought speed towards accepting an awkward offer without smoothing it*

**Sound leads** — Call the word; everyone makes the sound first and lets the shape follow out of it within a beat.  
*different emphasis — puts voice ahead of body for students who hide in physicality*

## Debrief

1. **Which round felt easiest?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone names the fast round, and says there was no time to check the shape first. That is the point made without you making it — move on.

2. **Did anyone copy a neighbour? How was it?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* A hand goes up and the person sounds mildly surprised it was fine. If nobody admits it, say you saw it happen and that it worked. Don't press.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in the base version. If you run Partner Echo, ask for consent to stand close and offer copying from a metre away as the default. Say at the start that shapes can be made seated, small, or with the upper body only, and that jogging on the spot can be marching or stillness — offer this to the room, never to an individual. Watch for anyone freezing hard onto a locked knee or a twisted spine and call "safe shapes, no torque" if you see it. Keep the floor clear of bags. Nobody has to make a sound.

## Why it works

Unfiltered spontaneity fails because the filter is faster than the offer. This closes the gap mechanically: the count fixes the moment of response, so there is no time to generate a second, better idea. The body answers because the body is what is available in half a second. Permitting repetition and copying removes the last exit — if originality is not required, hesitation has no purpose. By the fast round, students have produced twenty offers without vetting any of them, which is the evidence the cue rests on.
