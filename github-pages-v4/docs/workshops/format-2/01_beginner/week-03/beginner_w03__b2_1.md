# 😄 Laughter Blast — Boil Over

![Boil Over](../img/beginner_w03__b2_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room becomes a kettle: low hum, rising whistle, then a full-body laugh that spills everywhere at once.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 03 run-sheet](../week-03.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 3 is about receiving what you are given. This sits early in the session, after the welcome and before the first paired Yes-And work. It puts the room into a shared physical event that nobody can do alone, so when partner exercises start, people are already synchronised, warm and past the first embarrassment of making noise in front of strangers.

## What students actually learn

- That they can make a loud, uncontrolled sound in a room of adults and nothing bad happens.
- That committing to a group action at the same moment feels better than hedging — the ones who go full volume enjoy it more.
- That laughter is available on demand, not something you wait to earn.

## Setup

Clear floor space for a circle with arms' length between people. No chairs in the way. No props. Check the room next door — this is loud, so warn neighbours or shift to the far end of the space.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Circle up. Demonstrate one full kettle yourself at full volume — crouch, hum, rise, whistle, laugh. Then run round one together: ten slow counts up, call 'BOIL', laugh ten seconds. |
| 1 | Round two at double speed. Five counts up, boil, ten seconds of laughing. Keep your own laugh going loudest until the ten seconds are up. |
| 1 | Round three faster. Three counts, boil, laugh, straight back down into the crouch. Repeat it twice more without stopping to explain. |
| 1 | Chaos round. Everyone boils at their own speed, repeatedly, whenever they like. No counting from you. Let it run until it starts to thin out. |
| 1 | Call a final crouch and silence. Ten slow counts up, one synchronised boil. Hands down, three breaths, one debrief question, two answers, move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| During your demonstration | *"Watch me be ridiculous first."* |
| As the hum starts in round one | *"Low. Lower. From the belly."* |
| On the rise | *"Climb. Keep climbing."* |
| Right after 'BOIL' | *"Whole body. Arms up."* |
| Around six seconds into a laugh, when it sags | *"Don't stop. Ride it out."* |
| Start of the chaos round | *"Your kettle, your timing. Go."* |
| When the chaos round thins | *"One more each. Nobody's finished."* |
| Final crouch | *"Silence. Then everything."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    By round three the sound is loud enough that you can't hear yourself calling counts. Faces are flushed. In the chaos round people are laughing at each other's kettles rather than performing their own, and several of the laughs stop being manufactured somewhere in the middle. The room is noticeably louder when you finish than when you started.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Polite half-volume humming and laughs that stop after three seconds. | You demonstrated at seventy per cent. The room reads your ceiling as their ceiling. | Stop, redo your own kettle at genuinely embarrassing volume, then restart. Your commitment is the only permission they have. |
| People glancing sideways to check what everyone else is doing. | The circle is too small or too spread; they feel watched rather than inside the thing. | Tighten the circle and tell them to look at the ceiling on the rise. Eyes off each other removes the audience. |
| The chaos round dies within fifteen seconds. | No external timing, so nobody wants to be the last one going. | Keep your own kettle running continuously through the chaos round. Model that it doesn't stop until you call it. |
| Someone standing still, arms folded, not making sound. | Vocal exposure is genuinely hard for some adults on week three. | Say nothing in the moment. Offer the silent version — full body movement, no voice — before the next round, to the whole group not to them. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Run round three twice. Give the chaos round the full sixty seconds — with a small group it needs longer to build density. |
| **14–17** | One circle, slightly wider. Run round three twice. Chaos round at fifty seconds; the noise builds faster with more bodies. |
| **18–20** | One circle, or two concentric rings if the room is narrow — inner ring faces out. Run round three once only. Chaos round at forty-five seconds; it saturates quickly. Cut nothing else. |

## Variations

**Silent kettle** — Same shape, no sound at all. Crouch, rise, boil, laugh — but everything mimed with the body and face only.  
*easier — removes vocal exposure for a room that is still guarded, while keeping the physical commitment*

**Pass the boil** — Only one person boils at a time. They point at someone as they laugh; that person crouches and starts their own rise immediately.  
*harder — each person is briefly solo and must pick up the moment without hesitating*

**Two kettles, one room** — Split into two halves. One half hums low and holds; the other half rises and boils over the top of them, then swap.  
*different emphasis — trains listening and holding your own line while someone else takes the foreground*

**Boil on a partner's count** — In pairs, one person counts the rise out loud at whatever speed they choose; the other must match it exactly and boil on their word.  
*different emphasis — puts the session skill directly in the body: take the timing you're given, don't negotiate it*

## Debrief

1. **What was different between the laugh you started and the laugh you ended on?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone says the first was fake and somewhere in the middle it turned real, or that they stopped deciding to laugh. Two answers like that and you move on. Don't chase a third.

2. **What made it easier to go loud?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Answers point outward — everyone else was doing it, nobody could hear me individually, the person next to me went first. That's the connection to receiving offers. Name it in one sentence and start the next block.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity. Crouching is optional — offer a standing version with a bend at the knees, or a seated version with hands dropping to the lap, and say so before round one, not after someone struggles. Warn that this is loud; anyone with sound sensitivity can stand at the circle's edge or step to the doorway and rejoin. Vocal strain is the real risk — tell them the whistle is a light head voice, not a scream, and stop the exercise if you hear anyone shredding their throat. Anyone who does not want to make sound does the movement only; state that as a normal option, not a concession.

## Why it works

Offer reception fails when people spend the first half-second evaluating what they've been handed. This block removes the evaluation window. The count gives an unarguable timing, the boil is a single unmissable cue, and everyone acts on it simultaneously, so there is no gap in which to decide whether to commit. Doing it three times at increasing speed shortens the decision window until it disappears. The chaos round then hands the timing back to them, and they discover they can generate the same commitment without being counted in. Physiologically, forced laughter and raised arms shift breathing and posture out of the guarded default, which lowers the cost of the paired work that follows. The cue — accept the reality, then add one thing — is rehearsed here in its crudest form: the reality is the count, and the one thing you add is your whole body.
