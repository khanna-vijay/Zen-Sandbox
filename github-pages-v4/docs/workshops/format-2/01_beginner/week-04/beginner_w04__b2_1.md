# 😄 Laughter Blast — Popcorn Feet

![Popcorn Feet](../img/beginner_w04__b2_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room bounces on the spot making popping sounds until the popping turns into real laughter.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 04 run-sheet](../week-04.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 4 sits on listening with the whole body. The blast comes early, before any partner work, to shift the room out of morning politeness and into physical noise. It breaks the habit of monitoring yourself before you act — which is the same habit that makes people plan lines instead of hearing their partner. It feeds directly into the listening exercises that follow, where students need to be already loud, already breathing, already unembarrassed.

## What students actually learn

- Making a noise in front of strangers stops feeling like a decision and starts feeling like a reflex.
- Their impulse to pop arrives before any thought about whether it is a good time to pop — the felt difference between reacting and planning.
- Being out of breath and slightly ridiculous is a workable state to start a class in, not a failure of composure.

## Setup

Clear the floor. Push chairs and bags to the walls. You need roughly one arm-span per person in every direction. No props, no music. Check for slippery floor and tell anyone in socks to add shoes or keep the bounce very small. Ask, before you start, whether anyone would rather not jump — offer them the low-impact version without singling them out further.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Scatter the room. Say: we are kernels in a pan. Demonstrate the bounce — balls of the feet, loose arms, soft knees. Get everyone bouncing small and silent. No sound yet. |
| 1 | Hold the quiet bounce. Call it warming up. Keep it tiny and constant. You keep bouncing the whole time — never stop to watch the room. |
| 1 | Call POP. Anyone may jump higher and bark POP whenever they feel it. Random, overlapping, no order. You pop loudest and most often. |
| 1 | Speed up. Call faster, louder. Invite squeaks, honks, any noise. Room becomes continuous popping, arms flying. Push it past comfortable. |
| 1 | Call BURNT. Long sagging groan, sink towards the floor, flop. Let the laughter land. Shake out arms and legs. One question, thirty seconds, move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| Setting the quiet bounce | *"Tiny. Constant. Nothing above the knee."* |
| Just before releasing sound | *"Pop when it hits you, not when it's your turn."* |
| First pops are sparse and polite | *"Louder than feels sensible. Now."* |
| Room is popping but tidy | *"Overlap. Don't wait for a gap."* |
| Pushing into the fast stretch | *"Any noise. It doesn't have to be a word."* |
| Calling the collapse | *"BURNT — everything sags, all the way down."* |
| On the floor, laughter arriving | *"Let it out. Don't tidy it up."* |
| Shaking out | *"Shake the arms. Long breath. Stay standing."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Noise that stops being organised. Pops landing on top of each other, nobody watching for permission, several people making sounds they clearly did not choose. The groan on BURNT collapses into real laughter without you asking for it, and people are slightly out of breath when they stand up. You should not be able to pick out individual voices in the fast stretch.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pops arriving one at a time, evenly spaced, with small pauses between them. | The group has read it as a turn-taking game and is waiting for gaps out of politeness. | Pop three or four times in a row yourself, right over someone else. Call "overlap — don't wait for a gap." It corrects in about ten seconds. |
| Two or three people bouncing minimally, arms folded or half-folded, silent through the loud stretch. | They are watching to see how far the room will go before committing. Very common in week 4 with adults. | Do not name them. Raise your own volume and move physically nearer them while popping. Most join within twenty seconds. If they don't, let it go — this is five minutes, not a hill to die on. |
| The laughter never arrives at BURNT; the room just stops and looks at you. | The fast stretch was cut short or stayed too quiet, so there was no pressure to release. | Accept it and move on cheerfully. Next time, spend the full minute on the fast stretch and push volume harder before calling BURNT. The collapse only works if there was something built up. |
| Someone stops, holds their side or their knee, and steps out. | Impact or breathlessness — this is a jumping exercise done at speed. | Catch their eye, nod, keep the room running. Check in during the shake-out. Remind the group aloud that the bounce can be a heel lift with no jump at all. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One scatter, whole room. Give slightly more space per person and push volume harder — a small group sounds thin, so you carry more of the noise yourself. Keep the timings exactly as written. |
| **14–17** | One scatter, whole room, as written. This is the size the exercise sits best at. Move through the space during the fast stretch so no corner stays quiet. |
| **18–20** | Keep one group but tighten the spacing to arm's length only, and check the floor is genuinely clear at the edges. Bounces stay small the whole way through — at this density, higher jumps risk collisions. Call BURNT ten seconds early if the room is already at full volume. |

## Variations

**Kernels on chairs** — Everyone stays seated. The bounce becomes a bounce of the shoulders and knees. POP is a sharp lift of the arms plus the sound. BURNT is a slump over the knees.  
*Easier. Use when the floor is bad, the group is tired, or several people can't jump — running it seated for everyone is better than a visible opt-out group.*

**Named popping** — When you pop, you shout someone else's name instead of POP. That person must pop back within two seconds, then anyone may pop again.  
*Harder. Adds a listening load on top of the physical release — you must hear your own name through the noise.*

**Pan lid** — You hold a hand up as the lid. Hand down, popping is unlimited. Hand up, everyone must hold the bounce silently, however much they want to pop. Alternate every fifteen seconds.  
*Different emphasis. Shifts it from release towards impulse control and watching a shared signal while fully engaged physically.*

## Debrief

1. **When did you decide to pop?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* "I didn't" — or someone describing the sound arriving before they thought about it. If they describe choosing a good moment, name that as the planning habit and connect it to the cue, then move on.

2. **What changed in the room between the quiet bounce and BURNT?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Anything concrete about volume, breath, or having stopped caring how they looked. You want one honest sentence, not analysis. Thirty seconds, then next block.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity. State the low-impact option before you start, for everyone, not as an accommodation: the bounce can be a heel lift with feet staying on the floor, or a seated shoulder bounce in a chair at the edge. Say that anyone can drop to that version at any moment without asking. Watch the floor for bags and cables. Warn that it gets loud — if anyone is noise-sensitive, they can stand nearer the wall or step outside the door for the fast minute and return for BURNT. Anyone with knee, hip, back or pelvic-floor concerns should default to the heel lift. Nobody is asked to explain why.

## Why it works

Active listening fails when attention is spent on self-monitoring — rehearsing, checking, deciding. This block loads the body hard enough that self-monitoring becomes expensive to maintain. Fast bouncing plus forced vocal noise raises breath rate and occupies the planning attention; the pops then have to come from impulse because there is no spare capacity to choose them. The collapse on BURNT gives the raised arousal somewhere to go, and it goes into laughter, which is shared and involuntary. Students arrive at the next exercise already in the state the cue describes: they have just spent four minutes unable to plan, and it worked.
