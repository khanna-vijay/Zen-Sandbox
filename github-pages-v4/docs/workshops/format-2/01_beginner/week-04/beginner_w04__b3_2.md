# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Closing the Gap

![Closing the Gap](../img/beginner_w04__b3_2.jpg){ .infographic }

> Two facing lines: you only step towards your partner when you can hand their exact words back.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 04 run-sheet](../week-04.md)

## Why this activity, here

Opens Week 4. The room arrives full of plans for the day. This puts a physical cost on inattention before any scene work — you literally do not move until you have taken in what was said. It feeds the longer listening blocks later in the session, where echoing becomes internal rather than spoken.

## What students actually learn

- That they were listening to reply, not to receive — the missed echo makes it visible in three seconds.
- That the body follows attention: copying a partner's lean makes the words easier to hold.
- That closing distance with someone is earned by accuracy, not effort or charm.

## Setup

Clear a strip of floor long enough for two lines four metres apart. No chairs in the lane. Nothing to hold. Note the arm's-length stopping point before you begin so nobody drifts into contact.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Set the two lines, pair everyone across the gap, and state the rule: one short true sentence about your week, listener returns the last four words exactly. Correct echo, both step in. Wrong, both hold. |
| 1 | Line A speaks, Line B echoes the tail. Keep sentences short and rolling. Side-coach shorter sentences if anyone stalls. |
| 1 | Swap. Line B speaks, Line A echoes. Same stepping rule. Do not reset the distance already earned. |
| 1 | Add the body. Listener copies the speaker's posture and lean while listening, then echoes. Alternate speaker each sentence, keep stepping. |
| 1 | Stop everyone at arm's length. One sentence each, no echo, just receive it. Thank the partner, turn out to the room. Ask one question and move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| First ten seconds of round one | *"Short sentences. Four words is all they need to carry."* |
| Pairs debating whether an echo was close enough | *"Speaker decides. Hold and go again."* |
| Listeners visibly rehearsing their next line | *"You cannot plan and listen at the same time."* |
| Echoes coming back approximate | *"Exact words. Not the meaning — the words."* |
| Start of the posture round | *"Take their shape first. Then take their words."* |
| Pairs racing to close the gap | *"Nobody wins the floor. Slow down and get it right."* |
| Any pair nearing arm's length | *"Stop there. That's the edge."* |
| Final exchange | *"No echo now. Just let it land."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The room gets quieter as it goes. Sentences shorten without being told twice. You see listeners lean in and hold still before speaking. Most pairs arrive at arm's length; a few are still halfway and that is fine. The final unechoed sentence lands with a pause instead of a laugh.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Long, rambling sentences and echoes that fail every time. | Speakers are performing an anecdote instead of giving one line. | Stop the room for five seconds. Model one: 'I slept badly on Thursday.' Restart. Cap it at eight words. |
| Pairs stepping in on echoes that were clearly wrong. | Politeness. Nobody wants to hold their partner back. | Say the holding is the point — you learn nothing from a free step. Remind them the speaker rules on it. |
| During the posture round, listeners copy the body and lose the words. | Attention has gone to imitation as a task rather than a way in. | Call 'shape first, then words' and let them take an extra beat before echoing. Slow the round rather than dropping the body. |
| Two or three pairs standing chest to chest. | Momentum, and no one heard the stopping rule. | Call arm's length again for the whole room and step in to reset those pairs yourself. Do it without singling anyone out. |

## Variations

**Two words** — Listener echoes only the last two words instead of four.  
*easier — lowers the memory load so the room gets moving and building distance early*

**Whole sentence** — Listener returns the entire sentence, word for word, before either steps.  
*harder — forces total attention from the first syllable, not just the tail*

**Echo the tone** — Listener returns the last four words in the speaker's exact pitch, pace and volume.  
*different emphasis — shifts from information to delivery, and previews matching energy in later scene work*

## Debrief

1. **What happened in your head just before you got one wrong?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* They name a specific distraction — planning their own sentence, thinking about how they sounded, deciding what was interesting. Not 'I've got a bad memory.'

2. **Did copying the posture make the words easier or harder to hold?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Either answer is fine as long as it is specific. 'Easier — I stopped fidgeting' or 'harder at first, then it settled.' Move on once two people have answered honestly.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact at any point. Arm's length is a hard floor and you enforce it, not the pairs. Anyone uncomfortable standing close can stop early where they are and finish from there — say this before you start, so it is a choice, not a withdrawal. 'True sentence about your week' means true, not personal; model something dull so nobody feels pushed to disclose. Anyone who prefers not to stand can sit and echo from a chair with their partner seated opposite. Hearing difficulty: pair them with a shorter starting gap.

## Why it works

D2.S1 treats listening as a physical act rather than a courtesy. The stepping rule attaches an immediate, visible consequence to attention: you can only cross the floor with words you actually took in. Because the echo must be exact, paraphrase and the internal rehearsal that produces it both fail out loud. Adding posture recruits the body, which is where the cue lands — planning your reply requires you to leave your partner, and the copied lean makes leaving obvious to both of you.
