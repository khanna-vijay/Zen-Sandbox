# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Tall Chair, Small Chair

![Tall Chair, Small Chair](../img/beginner_w06__b3_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs pump their bodies bigger and smaller against each other on a caller's count, waking the legs and spine while the status gap swings.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 06 run-sheet](../week-06.md)

## Why this activity, here

First thing in the Status week. It puts status in the body before anyone has to speak a line of dialogue, so the concept arrives as a physical sensation rather than a definition. It feeds every scene block that follows today, where the class will be asked to raise and lower status inside conversation — and it plants the session cue, that the interesting thing is the distance between two people, not the label on either one.

## What students actually learn

- They feel status as a distance rather than a fixed rank — the gap moves even when neither person changes character.
- They stop needing permission to change. Swapping heights repeatedly makes shifting status feel routine, not like a big decision.
- Their legs and spine wake up, so later scenework has somewhere to go physically instead of living from the neck up.

## Setup

Clear floor, everyone standing, no chairs or props despite the name. Enough space that each pair has roughly two arm-spans. Know your count before you start so you can spot the odd person out fast. If anyone has knee, hip or balance trouble, tell them now that shallow counts as low and they can keep both heels down all game.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Line the room up roughly by height and pair off neighbours — quick, no negotiation. Pairs face each other, arm's length, no contact. Demonstrate the two extremes yourself in five seconds: up on the balls of the feet, down in a squat. |
| 1 | Call TALL and LOW. A grows, B sinks, hold three. Call SWAP. Run five or six exchanges, faster each round. Keep the calls sharp and don't explain between them. |
| 1 | Switch to numbers one to five. Call them quickly and out of order. Both partners hit the same number at once, silently. If they mismatch, keep calling — no fixing, no talking. |
| 1 | Remove the caller. Pairs run free, moving continuously, never matching each other's height for more than a beat. Side-coach eye contact and keep the room moving. Do not let it settle. |
| 1 | Call FREEZE. Hold five seconds. Tell them to look at the gap between them, not at their partner's face. Shake out legs. One line: the gap is the story. Move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| First TALL/LOW call | *"Commit to the extreme. No halfway."* |
| Third or fourth swap | *"Faster. Don't decide — just go."* |
| During the numbers section | *"Match in silence. Guess and commit."* |
| Pairs looking at the floor | *"Eyes up. Watch your partner, not your feet."* |
| Start of the free swing | *"Never the same height twice in a row."* |
| Free swing going flat | *"Widen the gap. Now close it."* |
| Anyone straining or wobbling | *"Shallow is fine. Small counts."* |
| On the freeze | *"Look at the distance between you. That's the scene."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Breathing hard, some laughing, legs shaking slightly. Pairs holding eye contact while their bodies move independently. In the free swing you should see genuine responsiveness — one person drops and the other rises without being told. The room is loud with movement and quiet with talk.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pairs chatting and coordinating out loud during the numbers round. | They think matching is a puzzle to solve rather than a guess to commit to. | Call over the top: "Silent. Guess and commit." Speed up the calls so there's no room to negotiate. |
| Everyone at half-height — nobody fully tall, nobody fully low. | Self-consciousness, plus a reasonable worry about knees. | Demonstrate the full range yourself once, badly and unglamorously. Then say aloud that shallow is fine if you commit to the reach — it's the difference that reads, not the depth. |
| The free swing stalls, pairs settle and stand still. | No caller means no structure, and this is the section that most often dies. | Step in as a metronome for fifteen seconds — clap a slow pulse and tell them one of them must change on every clap. Then withdraw. |
| One partner does all the moving, the other stays neutral. | The passive one is waiting to be told what to do. | Name it lightly across the room: "Both of you move. If your partner rises, you sink." |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | Five or six pairs. Run everything as written — you can see the whole room, so name individual pairs during the free swing to restart them. |
| **14–17** | Seven or eight pairs. Trim to four swaps in the TALL/LOW round so you keep to the minute. Stand centrally and turn as you call so no pair is behind you. |
| **18–20** | Nine or ten pairs. Split the floor into two halves and call for both at once — no need for separate groups. Drop the numbers round to five calls and keep the free swing to its full minute; it's the part that carries the learning. |

## Variations

**Heels down** — Remove the rise onto the balls of the feet and the deep squat. Tall is a full reach with flat feet; low is a soft bend at the knee.  
*Easier — keeps the whole range available to people with balance or joint limits, and stops the game becoming a fitness test.*

**Silent leader** — In the free swing, secretly tell one person per pair that they lead and the other follows. Don't tell the followers.  
*Harder — the follower must read the partner continuously, which is closer to what scenework demands.*

**Gap with a line** — During the free swing, each partner repeats one neutral phrase — "it's Tuesday" — every time they change height.  
*Different emphasis — shifts attention to how the same words change meaning as the physical gap swings.*

## Debrief

1. **When did the gap feel biggest?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone describes a specific moment — the pause after a freeze, or one partner dropping while they were still rising — rather than saying "when I was tall". They're describing distance, not position.

2. **Was it easier to be high or low?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Split answers, and someone noticing they defaulted to one end. Don't resolve it. The point is that each person has a home height and now knows it.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact at any point — say so at the start so nobody braces for it. Knees, hips and balance: tell the room before the first call that shallow squats and flat feet are a full-value option, and that anyone can stand and mark the game with arms alone. Offer a wall or chair back for balance to anyone who wants one. Height-based pairing can make people self-conscious; do it quickly and without comment, and let anyone swap silently if they ask. Stop anyone who is holding their breath in the squat.

## Why it works

Status modulation fails when students treat status as an identity they've been assigned. This block makes it impossible to hold that idea: you are high, then three seconds later you are low, then your partner is, and nothing about you has changed. What changes is the distance between two bodies. That is the session cue made physical — play the gap, not the person. Doing it fast and repeatedly, before any dialogue exists, means the shift gets encoded as a motor habit rather than a concept to remember mid-scene. The leg burn is useful too: it puts the change somewhere they can feel it.
