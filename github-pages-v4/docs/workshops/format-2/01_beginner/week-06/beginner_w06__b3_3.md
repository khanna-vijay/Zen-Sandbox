# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — One Notch

![One Notch](../img/beginner_w06__b3_3.jpg){ .infographic }

> A silent standing circle where people shift status a single notch at a time using only head, gaze and breath.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 06 run-sheet](../week-06.md)

## Why this activity, here

First thing in the week-six room, before anyone has to speak a line. It gives the class a physical vocabulary for status that the scene work later in the session will lean on, and it sets the week's cue — the gap between two people is the playable thing, not the person's fixed rank. Feeds directly into the paired status scenes that follow.

## What students actually learn

- That status is something you do with your chin and eyes, not something you are.
- That a one-notch change is visible to a room — you do not need to grovel or swagger.
- Which direction is harder for them personally, up or down, which they will need to know all session.

## Setup

Clear floor space for one circle of 10-20 standing adults with arm's length between each person. No chairs, no props, no music. Check nobody is standing behind a pillar or out of sightline. If anyone cannot stand for five minutes, seat them in the circle at the same eye level as possible and tell them the whole exercise works seated.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Circle up. Feet planted, arms loose, mouths closed. State the two rules: no speaking, no stepping, for five minutes. Lead three slow breaths together. Then define neutral — level gaze, still chin, quiet hands, weight even. |
| 1 | Count them into five seconds of complete stillness at neutral. Count it out loud. Let them settle back, then hold neutral quietly for another few seconds so they feel the baseline. |
| 1 | Call 'up one notch'. Wait ten seconds. Call 'down one notch'. Wait ten seconds. Keep alternating and occasionally repeat the same direction. Remind them once: neck up only. |
| 1 | Tell them to silently pick one person across the circle, no signalling. Call 'one notch below them'. Hold thirty seconds in silence. Then call 'one notch above them'. Hold thirty seconds. |
| 1 | Call 'back to neutral'. One shared breath. Ask by show of hands who found below easier than above. Count the hands aloud, say nothing about it, move to the next block. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| After the three opening breaths | *"Neutral. Level gaze. Nothing to prove."* |
| First 'up one notch' | *"One notch. Not a king. One."* |
| When shoulders and hips start moving | *"Neck up only. Let the body stay out of it."* |
| Mid alternating calls | *"Small is still visible. Trust it."* |
| As they pick their person | *"Keep the choice private. No deals across the circle."* |
| During the thirty-second 'below' hold | *"Play the gap, not the person."* |
| During the thirty-second 'above' hold | *"Hold the beat. Breathe slower than them."* |
| On the final call | *"Neutral. One breath. Done."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    A quiet circle where you can see the whole room drift a few degrees at once. Chins lift and drop by centimetres. Blink rates change. Nobody is performing a character. During the paired holds you can spot small mismatched pairs — two people quietly ranking themselves against each other from across the room — and the whole thing stays silent to the end.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| People adding whole-body postures — hands on hips, slouching, shoulders rolling forward. | They are reaching for a character they have seen rather than making a single adjustment. | Call 'neck up only' and repeat the neutral count for five seconds. Do not explain further; just reset and carry on. |
| Giggling and eye contact between two or three people. | Silence and being looked at is uncomfortable, and laughing releases it. | Say 'that's the nerves, keep going' without stopping. Lower your own voice for the next call. It usually settles within twenty seconds. |
| During the paired holds, people obviously staring at their chosen person or mirroring them. | They heard 'pick a person' as 'engage that person'. | Call 'keep it private, they don't need to know'. Remind them the adjustment is to themselves, not to the other person. |
| Nothing visibly changes at all — the circle looks identical on every call. | They are thinking about status instead of moving. | Call one exaggerated round — 'four notches up' — so they feel the range, then immediately return to one notch. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle, close spacing. Run the calls slightly slower, around twelve seconds apart, because there is less to look at. Keep both thirty-second holds. |
| **14–17** | One circle, arm's length spacing. Run exactly as written at ten-second intervals. Stand in the circle yourself so it does not become a demonstration. |
| **18–20** | One circle still — do not split, the size is the point. Widen the ring so everyone can see across. Shorten the alternating calls to eight seconds so you fit six or seven in, and keep the holds at thirty seconds each. |

## Variations

**Eyes only** — Remove chin and breath from the toolkit. Every notch must come from gaze alone — where they look, how long they hold, how often they blink.  
*Harder. Strips out the moves people already know and forces attention onto the smallest signal.*

**Called numbers** — Instead of 'up one notch', call a number from one to five and have them move to that level. Announce the number before each change.  
*Easier. Gives beginners an external scale rather than asking them to judge a relative step.*

**Two circles, one gap** — Split into an inner and outer ring facing each other. Each person holds one notch above the person opposite for thirty seconds, then swaps to one below.  
*Different emphasis — makes the gap explicit and mutual, so they feel status as a relationship rather than a private setting.*

## Debrief

1. **Hands again — who found going below easier than going above?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* You are not looking for words, just the split. A roughly even show of hands, or a clear lean one way that you can name in a sentence: 'about two thirds of you find down easier — remember that later.'

2. **What did you actually change to move one notch?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Concrete and small: 'my chin came down two centimetres', 'I stopped blinking', 'my breath got slower'. If someone says 'I felt more confident', ask what they did with their head.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity, so no consent step is needed for touch. Sustained eye contact is the pressure point: tell the room at the start that anyone can drop their gaze to the floor at any moment and stay in the exercise. Offer a seated version openly, not privately, so it does not read as a concession. If someone steps out of the circle, let them stand at the edge and watch; do not check in mid-exercise, catch them afterwards. Keep the five minutes strictly to time — the silence gets heavy past that.

## Why it works

Status modulation fails when people treat status as identity — a big personality or a small one. This exercise removes speech, movement and character, leaving only the adjustable signals, so status can only be experienced as a dial. The paired holds then attach that dial to another person, which is where the session's cue comes from: what plays is the distance between two settings, and a single notch of distance is enough to be read across a room. Doing it before anyone speaks means the physical habit is in the body before the scene work asks for it.
