# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Palms Up, Palms Down

![Palms Up, Palms Down](../img/beginner_w01__b3_3.jpg){ .infographic }

> A quiet circle where every invitation gets a visible yes or a visible pass before anything happens.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact negotiated` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 01 run-sheet](../week-01.md)

## Why this activity, here

First moving thing after the welcome and the name round. The room has heard that consent matters; this is where they see it operate before anything is asked of them on their feet. It feeds every partner exercise for the rest of the day — once the palms signal exists, you can call for it at any point without stopping the class.

## What students actually learn

- Saying no costs nothing here — they have watched someone else do it and watched the room carry on.
- Their yes is a thing they give, not a thing assumed of them.
- A pass is information, not a problem: the group adjusts around it in a second and moves on.

## Setup

Open floor, one circle, everyone standing at arm's length. No chairs in the way. No props. Know in advance whether anyone has flagged a shoulder, arm or touch limitation, and do not single them out. Stand in the circle yourself, not outside it.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Form the circle. Teach palms up and palms down. State that both answers are complete and no one explains their choice. Then make the first ask — one slow breath with eyes closed — and take palms. |
| 1 | Wait three seconds after palms go up. Run the breath. Palms-down people stay open-eyed and watch. Bring everyone back and name, plainly, that some passed and nothing broke. |
| 1 | Second ask: five seconds of eye contact with someone across the circle. Palms first, three-second pause, then people find each other. Palms-down people look at the floor in the centre. |
| 1 | Third ask: a hand on the right-hand neighbour's shoulder. Both palms must be up. One palm down means both keep hands down. Ten seconds, then call release. |
| 1 | Offer one re-run of whichever ask the room hesitated on. Say answers may change. Run it. Then hands down, one shared breath, and say the cue. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| As palms first go up on ask one | *"Palms where I can see them. No explaining."* |
| During the three-second pause | *"Take the time. Nobody is late."* |
| When the first palms-down appears | *"Good. That's the signal working."* |
| During the eye contact ask | *"Five seconds. Breathe through it."* |
| Before the shoulder ask | *"Two yeses, or no hands. Check your neighbour."* |
| If someone laughs off their own pass | *"You don't owe anyone a reason."* |
| Offering the re-run | *"Answer fresh. Changing your mind is allowed."* |
| On the final breath | *"Hands down. You can always say Cut."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Palms go up and down without hesitation and without commentary. At least one person passes on at least one ask, and the room does not turn to look at them. The shoulder ask produces a few hands-down pairs and no awkward negotiation. People are quiet. By the end the signal is being used before you ask for it.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Every single palm is up on every ask. | The group reads the exercise as a compliance test, or nobody wants to be first to pass. | Put your own palms down on the shoulder ask and say nothing about it. Then offer the re-run and note plainly that the room hasn't tested the pass yet. |
| Someone explains their pass out loud — an apology or a reason. | Habit. Adults are trained to justify refusal. | Say "no reason needed" once, lightly, to the room rather than to them. Do not repeat it — a second correction makes the pass a bigger event than the yes. |
| Nervous laughter and chatter during the shoulder ask. | Touch is being treated as the joke, which lets people avoid deciding. | Call for silence before the palms go up on that ask. Give the ten seconds a clear start and stop so it is a task, not a moment. |
| Palms drift up slowly, people copying their neighbours. | The three-second pause is too short, or you're rushing your delivery. | Say "eyes on me, not each other" and hold a full three seconds. Deliver the ask, pause, then look up. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Run all three asks and the re-run as written. You will have room for a genuine pause between asks — use it. |
| **14–17** | One circle still. Shorten your teaching of the signal to twenty seconds and cut the re-run to the shoulder ask only, so the timing holds. |
| **18–20** | Split into two circles of nine or ten, each with a nominated caller who repeats your asks. You call from between them. Drop the re-run and go straight to the shared breath and the cue. |

## Variations

**Palms only, no asks** — Skip the three asks. Call abstract invitations — "something loud", "something close" — and take palms without ever running the activity.  
*easier; removes all doing and drills the signal alone, useful with a very tense first-time group*

**Anyone can ask** — After your first ask, hand the asking round the circle. Each person proposes something the group could do; palms answer; you decide whether to run it.  
*harder; puts people in the position of hearing a no to their own offer*

**Cut in motion** — Run the eye-contact ask and the shoulder ask, and tell people they may drop to palms down mid-activity to stop it. Practise the withdrawal, not just the entry.  
*different emphasis; shifts from consent as a gate to consent as ongoing and revocable*

## Debrief

1. **What did you notice in your body just before you chose?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something specific and physical — a held breath, a glance at a neighbour, hands going up before they'd decided. Not a verdict on the exercise. Move on once two or three people have named a sensation.

2. **Did anyone's palms go down, and what happened next?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* "Nothing happened." Someone says the room just carried on. If a passer says they felt watched, say thank you and let it stand — do not argue them out of it.

3. **Where does this signal go for the rest of today?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone names a concrete moment — before touching a scene partner, before being volunteered. That's your handover to the next block. Do not let it become a discussion of consent in general.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    The shoulder ask is the only contact and it requires two visible yeses; one palm down cancels it for both people, with no negotiation permitted. State this before you make the ask, not after. Anyone with a shoulder, arm or mobility limitation may pass without flagging it, and passing is never queried. Palms-down people are given something to do — watching, or looking at the centre — so a pass never means standing exposed. If someone cannot raise hands to hip height, agree an alternative signal privately before the circle forms, such as a nod and a headshake, and teach the whole room both versions.

## Why it works

Boundary navigation fails in improv classes because refusal is invisible and untimed — people are asked to opt out of something already in motion, in front of witnesses, at a cost. This puts the answer before the action and gives it a fixed three-second window everyone uses at once. Nobody is refusing; everyone is answering. Because a pass is expressed in the same gesture and the same second as a yes, it carries no more social weight than agreement, and the group sees consequence-free refusal happen live rather than being promised it. Escalating from a private act, to a shared gaze, to touch means the stakes rise only after the signal is already reliable.
