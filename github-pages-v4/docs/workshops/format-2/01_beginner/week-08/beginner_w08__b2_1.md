# 😄 Laughter Blast — Wobble and Bow

![Wobble and Bow](../img/beginner_w08__b2_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone shakes their whole body while letting their voice wobble, then takes three enormous synchronised bows at nobody.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 08 run-sheet](../week-08.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 8 is the showcase. This is the first thing bodies do after arriving, before any scene work or run-through. It burns off the held breath people walk in with and puts voice, limbs and volume all above normal size, so the first scene of the day doesn't start small. The bows rehearse the taking of applause, which most of this group has never done and will do for real tonight.

## What students actually learn

- Their voice can be loud and unlovely in front of people and nothing bad happens.
- Deliberate laughter turns into real laughter if you hold it past the awkward part.
- Taking a bow is a physical skill you can practise, not a reward you have to earn.

## Setup

Clear floor, one circle, everyone at arm's length so limbs can swing without contact. No chairs in the ring. You stand in the circle, not outside it — you do every shake and every bow yourself. Nothing to hand out. Check the room next door isn't mid-silence, because this is genuinely loud.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Start alone. Shake your right hand hard, let a long wobbling 'aaaah' out, louder and sillier than feels dignified. Beckon the circle in without stopping. Wait until every voice is going. |
| 1 | Lead the countdown for the whole circle: eight shakes per limb with wobbling voice — right hand, left hand, right leg, left leg — then four each, then two, then one. Count out loud. |
| 1 | Keep the shaking. Call the sound changes: motorboat lip-flaps, then swooping siren, then low cartoon growl. Give each roughly fifteen seconds. Everyone switches on your call, nobody early. |
| 1 | All four limbs shaking at once, 'ha-ha-ha' on every wobble. It sounds fake for thirty seconds. Say so, and keep going. Hold it until real laughter arrives, then hold it a bit longer. |
| 1 | Call a dead stop. Three enormous slow bows to the empty middle on your count, arm sweeping. Roar and applaud after each. Then one question, two quick answers, move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| You're shaking alone at the very start | *"Copy me. Louder than me."* |
| During the limb countdown | *"Let the voice wobble — don't hold it steady."* |
| On each sound change | *"All of us, now. Motorboat."* |
| About fifteen seconds into the fake laughing | *"Yes, it's fake. Keep it fake. Don't stop."* |
| When the first real laugh breaks out | *"There it is. Stay with it."* |
| Just before the bows | *"Stop dead. Nothing moves."* |
| On each bow | *"Slower. All the way down. Sweep the arm."* |
| Straight after the third bow | *"Applaud yourselves. Louder than that."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    Volume above what the room normally makes, and nobody looking round to check whether that's allowed. Voices genuinely wobbling rather than a flat held note. During the fake-laugh section you should hear at least three or four people tip into real laughter and take others with them. The stop is clean and instant. Bows are slow, deep and unified, and the applause afterwards is loud rather than polite. People come out of it breathing hard and looking at each other.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Half the circle shaking with a quiet, steady note and no wobble. | You started at a moderate volume, so moderate became the ceiling. People match the leader. | Stop. Do ten seconds yourself at genuinely ridiculous volume, then restart. Say the note is supposed to break up. |
| The fake laughing dies out after ten seconds into embarrassed silence. | Somebody laughed at how fake it sounded and the group read that as permission to stop. | Name it as it happens: 'It's meant to sound stupid, that's the exercise.' Restart the ha-ha and count aloud through it. |
| Small, quick, apologetic bows. | People treat bowing as a claim they haven't earned, so they rush it. | Make them slower, not bigger. Count four beats down, hold two at the bottom, four back up. Slowness does the work. |
| Two or three people standing still with arms folded. | Being watched while making an undignified noise, on showcase day, with nerves already high. | Don't single them out. Move your own position so you're beside them and keep going. Most join by the sound changes. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle, and it will feel exposed because everyone can see everyone. Add ten seconds to the fake-laugh section — smaller groups take longer to tip. Keep the limb countdown to eight, four, two, one as written. |
| **14–17** | One circle, run exactly as written. This is the size the timings assume. Stand in the ring rather than the middle so nobody is performing at you. |
| **18–20** | One circle still — do not split, the volume is the point. Widen it so limbs clear. Cut the limb countdown to eight, four, one to protect the timing, and keep the bows at three. |

## Variations

**Sound only** — Drop the shaking. Everyone stands still and does the voice sequence — wobble, motorboat, siren, growl — then the laughter, then the bows.  
*Easier. Use it if the room has injuries, tired bodies, or a low ceiling of willingness. Removes the physical exposure and keeps the vocal warm-up.*

**Solo bow line** — After the three group bows, go round the circle: each person steps forward, takes one slow bow alone, and the room applauds them individually.  
*Harder. Adds three or four minutes. Rehearses taking applause with nobody to hide behind — useful the week before a showcase, brutal earlier in the course.*

**Partner mirror wobble** — Pair up facing each other. Each pair invents their own shake-and-sound and holds it for thirty seconds, then all pairs bow to each other rather than to the middle.  
*Different emphasis. Shifts from group volume to holding attention on one person, which is closer to the two-hander work later in the session.*

## Debrief

1. **What happened to the fake laughing?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Someone says it turned real, and roughly when. Someone else says they were embarrassed and then weren't. Two answers is plenty — take them and move.

2. **How did the bow feel with nobody watching?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* 'Ridiculous, then fine.' Or 'I wanted to make it small.' You're looking for them to notice the urge to shrink, not to resolve it. Don't discuss, just move on.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact at any point — set the circle wide enough that swinging limbs never reach anyone. Say at the top that any shake can be done small, seated, or with one limb only, and that mouthing the sounds counts as doing it. Anyone with a bad back, dizziness or neck trouble bows from the waist a few inches, or nods; say this before the first bow, not after. The volume is high — flag it for anyone sensitive to noise and let them step to the edge. Nobody is asked to be funny; the sounds are prescribed, not invented.

## Why it works

Stage presence and clarity fail because people arrive under-sized — quiet voice, small gesture, held breath — and then try to be interesting on top of that. This raises the physical baseline first. Shaking with a wobbling voice makes a steady, controlled sound impossible, which removes the option of doing it neatly. Sustained deliberate laughter works on a simple mechanism: the body cannot easily tell manufactured laughing from the real thing for long, and once one person tips, mirroring does the rest. The bows then attach that raised state to the specific act of receiving attention, which is exactly the moment on showcase night where beginners collapse. Take care of yourself first: get your own body loud and open before you owe anything to a partner.
