# 🔥 Warm-up cluster — Sigh Trade

![Sigh Trade](../img/beginner_w07__b3_2.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs pass feelings back and forth on the out-breath alone, so emotion lands in the body before anyone reaches for words.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 07 run-sheet](../week-07.md)

## Why this activity, here

First thing in the room on the week the voice carries emotion. It comes before any scene work and before the vocal-range block. It puts feeling in the diaphragm while the mouth is still shut, so that when words arrive later in the session they arrive already coloured rather than decorated afterwards.

## What students actually learn

- They notice their breath changing before they've decided what they feel.
- They stop treating emotion as something to announce and start treating it as something already happening.
- They read a partner's state off breath alone, without waiting for a line to interpret.

## Setup

Clear floor, people standing in pairs facing each other, arm's length apart. No chairs, no props. Nobody sits. Have a timer or a watch you can glance at — the rounds are short and you will run over if you don't watch. Know in advance whether anyone in the room has told you about breathing difficulty or anxiety around breathwork; offer them the quiet-sigh option before you start rather than in front of everyone.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Pair them. Demo once with a volunteer: you sigh a feeling out, they breathe in and sigh it back. Say the rule — breath only, no comedy faces. Start immediately. |
| 1 | Round one. Matching sighs, roughly one every three seconds. Call the pace once if the room drifts slow. Let it run without commentary for the last twenty seconds. |
| 1 | Round two. Return the sigh with a different feeling. Say it once, clearly, then stop talking. Do not let pairs negotiate what feeling to send. |
| 1 | Round three. One word rides on the out-breath. Remind them the word is a passenger, not the point. Let this round run longest in your head — it's where it lands. |
| 1 | Stop. Hands down, one ordinary breath together. Ask two pairs what they caught that they didn't intend to send. Take the answers, don't develop them, move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| Ten seconds into round one, when people are still smiling at each other | *"Breath only. Nothing on the face."* |
| When exchanges have gone slow and polite | *"Don't wait for permission. Send the next one."* |
| Mid round one, if sighs are all the same pleasant medium | *"Let it be less attractive."* |
| Opening round two | *"Take it in first. Then change it."* |
| Round two, when people are visibly deciding | *"Don't choose it. Let it arrive."* |
| Opening round three | *"Breath first. The word comes after."* |
| Round three, if the word is getting acted | *"Say it plain. The breath is doing the work."* |
| Just before the final ordinary breath | *"Let it go. Normal breath, together."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    The room gets quieter as it goes, not louder. Shoulders and ribs are moving — you can see the breath from across the space. In round two the changes are unforced and often surprise the person making them. In round three the words are ordinary and flat and still carry something. A few pairs are laughing at what came out of them; that's fine, as long as they didn't aim for it.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pairs performing broad emotions with big faces and shoulders — a pantomime of sadness, then a pantomime of rage. | They've read the exercise as acting rather than breathing, and are compensating for feeling exposed. | Call 'nothing on the face' and, if it persists, have them close their eyes for twenty seconds of round one. Removes the audience, keeps the breath. |
| Sighs so quiet and small you can't tell exchanges apart, exchanges drifting to one every ten seconds. | Self-consciousness about making noise in a room of near-strangers, plus no external pace. | Clap or click a slow three-second pulse for fifteen seconds. Drop out once they've caught it. Don't keep clapping — it turns into a drill. |
| In round two, partners stop and talk: 'what should I do, angry?' | They think the change has to be a named emotion decided in advance. | Say 'don't choose it, let it arrive' and keep the round running. Do not let them restart. The mid-flow correction is the lesson. |
| In round three the word is being delivered like a line reading and the breath has vanished underneath it. | Words pull attention forward into the mouth; it's the default habit the whole week works against. | Call 'say it plain'. If a pair still can't, tell them to sigh twice for every one word. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | Straight pairs, one facilitator circulating. Run all three rounds at the stated lengths. With eleven or thirteen, make one trio — the third person breathes with each partner alternately. |
| **14–17** | Straight pairs. Volume rises, so stand at the room's edge to call over it rather than walking between pairs. Shorten round one by ten seconds and give that time to round three. |
| **18–20** | Split into two halves at opposite ends of the room to keep the noise workable; run identical rounds in both simultaneously. Skip the second debrief pair — take one answer from each half. |

## Variations

**Eyes closed** — Run rounds one and two with both partners' eyes shut. Open them for round three.  
*Easier. Removes the sense of being watched, which is what drives the pantomiming.*

**Chain** — Stand in a circle. One person sighs to their neighbour, who takes it in, changes it, and passes on round the ring.  
*Harder. Everyone is watched, and the receiver must commit publicly with no partner to hide behind.*

**Sigh into a line** — After round three, pairs exchange one full sentence each, but must sigh first and let the sigh set the tone of the sentence.  
*Different emphasis. Bridges the breath work directly into spoken text, ready for the scene block.*

## Debrief

1. **What did you catch from your partner that they didn't mean to send?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Something specific and slightly unflattering — 'she sounded bored and I don't think she meant to' or 'I sent tired and got back irritated, and it made me irritated'. Not 'it was fun'.

2. **In round three, did the word change what you were feeling, or did the feeling change the word?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* They noticed the order. 'The breath was already annoyed, so 'right' came out annoyed — I didn't decide that.' If they say the word came first, that's honest too; note it and move on.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity, so no consent step is needed for touch. Breathwork can be uncomfortable for people with asthma, anxiety, or a history of panic — say up front that sighs can be small and quiet, and that anyone can drop to just watching their partner and nodding. Eye contact is optional throughout; offer looking at the partner's collarbone or the floor as an equal choice. Keep the total short and don't push volume; this is a warm-up, not a release exercise. If someone gets lightheaded, have them stop and breathe normally, standing.

## Why it works

Emotional fluidity — D1.S2 — is limited by how long it takes a performer to move from one state to another. Most beginners route emotion through decision: they name a feeling, then manufacture a voice for it. That loop is slow and it shows. Stripping out words removes the naming step; there is nothing to decide, only a breath to release and receive. Round two forces a change with no planning time, so the switch happens below the point where choice usually intervenes. By round three the word arrives on top of a state that already exists, which is the order the cue names: it changes the breath before it changes the words. The transfer is that in scene work the same order holds — the emotional shift is already audible on the in-breath, and the line that follows only has to not contradict it.
