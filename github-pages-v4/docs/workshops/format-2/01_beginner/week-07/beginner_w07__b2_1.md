# 😄 Laughter Blast — Kettle Boil

![Kettle Boil](../img/beginner_w07__b2_1.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room becomes a kettle at the same time — low hum, rattling lid, full whistle, then the steam comes out as laughing.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

⬅️ [Back to the Week 07 run-sheet](../week-07.md)

## Why this activity, here

Week 7 opens the voice up. The warm-ups before this were breath and hum; scenes after this ask for emotion carried on sound rather than clever lines. Kettle Boil is the bridge: it puts everyone's breath under pressure and lets the release come out as laughter, so the body already knows the cue — the change happens in the breath first — before any scene work asks for it.

## What students actually learn

- That a rising sound changes how you feel, not just how you sound — the emotion arrives after the breath, not before.
- That making an ugly, loud noise in front of thirteen adults is survivable, and takes about ninety seconds to stop mattering.
- That laughter can be started mechanically and still become real, which lowers the stakes on 'genuine' emotion later.

## Setup

One circle, standing, arms' length between people so rattling arms don't connect. Clear the floor of bags and chairs — people crouch and rise repeatedly. No props. Know before you start that you will be the loudest person in the room for five minutes straight; if you hold back, the room holds back.

## How to run it — 5 minutes

| Min | What you do |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Circle up. Say: we are all kettles. Demonstrate the full arc yourself at full volume — crouch, low hum, slow rise, rattle, whistle. No apology, no explanation of why. |
| 1 | Round one, everyone together. Crouch, low 'mmmm', rise over ten seconds, hum climbs into a breathy 'wheeeee'. Shake it out. Run it once, no notes. |
| 1 | Round two, add the lid. Same climb; from halfway up everyone rattles — knees, shoulders, hands. Hum wobbles with the shake. Call 'rattle' as they pass halfway. |
| 1 | Round three, slow boil. Twenty seconds climbing from the floor, hard rattle, then hold the whistle until breath runs out. Let them finish at different moments. |
| 1 | Boil over. Straight from whistle into puffed 'ha-ha-ha-ha', hands paddling steam up. Hold thirty seconds, loudest yourself. Cut clean. One question, two answers, move on. |

## Side-coaching script

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| As they crouch for round one | *"All the way down. Hum from the floor."* |
| Mid-climb, round one | *"Slower. Let the sound rise before you do."* |
| As they pass halfway in round two | *"Lid on. Rattle everything."* |
| If the rattle stays in the hands only | *"Knees. Shoulders. Let the hum wobble."* |
| At the top of round three | *"Hold it. Every last bit of air."* |
| The instant the whistles start to fail | *"Boil over — ha-ha-ha-ha."* |
| Fifteen seconds into the laughing | *"Paddle the steam up. Keep puffing."* |
| To finish | *"And off. Shake it out."* |

!!! success "What good looks like"
    By round three the room is genuinely loud and nobody is checking who is watching. Whistles end raggedly, at different times, and the laughing that follows starts as a forced puff and turns real for at least half the room within ten seconds. Faces are flushed. Someone laughs at how stupid it is, which counts.

## When it goes wrong

| You see | What's causing it | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Polite volume — everyone humming at conversational level, small movements, eyes flicking around the circle. | You demonstrated at half commitment, or you narrated the exercise instead of doing it. The room copies your ceiling. | Stop, say 'that was a warm-up of the warm-up', and run round one again at your absolute full volume. Do not explain. Volume is caught, not instructed. |
| The laughing section dies after eight seconds and turns into people looking at each other. | You went quiet or dropped out to observe. Forced laughter needs a constant loud source to lean on. | Stay in it, loudest, for the whole thirty seconds. Keep paddling your hands. It will feel far too long to you; that is the correct length. |
| One or two people crouch and rise but make no sound at all. | Voice exposure is the specific fear, and everyone hearing everyone makes it worse for them. | Say to the room, mid-round, 'breath only is fine' — it gives an honest option without singling anyone out. Most join the sound by round three. |
| People coughing or going hoarse after the whistle. | They are pushing the whistle from the throat rather than letting it thin out on breath. | Before round three say: 'the whistle is air, not throat — let it get breathy'. Demonstrate a thin, airy whistle once. |

## Scaling

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| **10–13** | One circle. Run exactly as written. With a small group the sound is thinner, so extend the boil-over by five seconds and stand inside the circle rather than on the rim so your volume reaches everyone. |
| **14–17** | One circle, as written. This is the size it works best at. Stand on the circle so people can see your rattle, and turn as you climb so every arc of the room gets your face. |
| **18–20** | Still one circle — do not split it, the mass of sound is the point. Widen the ring so rattling arms have room. Cut round two's rattle demonstration to five seconds to protect the timing, and keep the boil-over at thirty seconds. |

## Variations

**Half the room boils** — Split into two groups. One group boils while the other stands and watches, then swap. Two shorter cycles instead of three full rounds.  
*Easier — being watched by half the room is less exposing than being one voice among twenty, and the watchers see the shape before they do it.*

**Kettles at different speeds** — After round two, tell each person to pick their own boil length between ten and thirty seconds. Start together, finish whenever your kettle finishes.  
*Harder — no group rhythm to hide inside, so each person has to manage their own breath and commit alone.*

**Boil over into a feeling** — Instead of laughing, call a different release at the top: sobbing, groaning, cheering. Run one round each.  
*Different emphasis — shifts from laughter release to emotional range, and shows the same breath ramp can deliver any feeling.*

## Debrief

1. **Where in your body did you notice the change first?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* Anything below the neck — chest, ribs, stomach, throat tightening. If someone says 'I decided to feel silly' that is a head answer; ask 'and where did you feel it?' and take one more.

2. **Did the laughing at the end become real at any point?**
   > *A good answer sounds like:* 'About halfway.' 'When I heard someone else go.' Honest 'no, mine stayed fake' is also a good answer — take it, name it as normal, and move on. Do not chase agreement.

!!! warning "Safety & inclusion"
    No contact in this activity — keep arms' length so rattling doesn't reach anyone. Say up front that breath-only participation counts fully and people can sit out and stay in the circle. Warn that repeated crouching and rising can bring on dizziness; anyone who prefers to stay standing tall and boil from the chest gets the same result. Anyone with a voice injury or who is unwell should mouth it. Loud sustained noise is genuinely hard for some people — flag the volume before you start so nobody is ambushed by it.

## Why it works

Emotional fluidity fails when people try to decide on a feeling and then perform it. The kettle removes the decision. A rising hum forces a longer, higher, more pressurised breath, and a pressurised breath produces physiological arousal whether or not you intended it. By the time the whistle breaks, the body is already in a heightened state and the laughter has somewhere to come from. That is the cue made literal: breath changes first, feeling follows, words come last. Doing it as a whole room in unison also removes the audience — nobody is the one being watched — so the volume rises faster than it would in pairs.
