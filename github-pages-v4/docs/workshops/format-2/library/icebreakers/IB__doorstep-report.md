# Doorstep Report

![Doorstep Report — infographic](img/IB__doorstep-report.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone reports the mood they walked in with, in a few words and one gesture, then names one ordinary thing they're looking forward to.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

The room finds out who actually walked in today, in words small enough that nobody has to be clever. Moods get said out loud and stop leaking sideways into the work.

**Everyone has spoken within 90 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who reports flat and deadpan versus who expands past the word limit · Who arrived wired, who arrived depleted, and who is masking it · Who edits themselves before speaking and who says the first thing · How readable each person's gesture is without words

## Setup

Standing circle, everyone able to see everyone. No props. Clear enough floor that people can take a half-step in when they speak.

## How to run it

1. (40s) Brief it. Two laps of the circle. Lap one: the mood you arrived in. Lap two: one ordinary thing you're looking forward to later. No jokes needed, true only.
2. (70s) Pairs first. Turn to the person beside you. 35 seconds each: what you were doing twenty minutes before you got here. Call the swap. Odd number, make one three.
3. (90s) Lap one. Round the circle, each person gives one gesture and up to four words for the mood they walked in with. Clap the pace if it drags.
4. (90s) Lap two. Same order, opposite direction. Each person names one ordinary thing they're looking forward to after class, six words maximum. Bed counts. Toast counts.
5. (10s) Close. Name two moods you heard back to the room, then move straight into the next piece.

## Side-coach

- *"Four words. Gesture first, words after."*
- *"Don't make it interesting. Make it true."*
- *"Keep it moving — no one gets more than five seconds."*
- *"Ordinary is the goal. Toast is a perfect answer."*

## If it stalls

- If people are performing their mood instead of reporting it, drop to one word only and go again fast.
- If the first three answers are all 'fine, tired', model it yourself with something specific — 'rushed, wet socks' — and restart the lap.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose arrival mood surprised you?
2. Anything you heard that you'd want to ask about at the break?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle, both laps, four words each. You'll finish with time spare — let the pairs run 45 seconds each way. |
| 14–17 | One circle, both laps, but cap lap one at three words and skip the pair chat if you're behind at the two-minute mark. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of nine or ten, standing apart. Both run both laps at the same time. Stand between them and clap the pace. Take the closing report from one person per circle. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Anyone can say 'pass' and give only the gesture, or step back and stay silent for a lap; say this out loud before you start. Nobody is asked what caused their mood.

## Lineage

In the Company check-in circles used in rehearsal rooms and workshop practice tradition. Standard check-ins tend to sprawl — one person talks for a minute and the room cools. We hard-capped the word count, added a gesture so the body arrives too, and put a second lap on something ordinary and forward-looking so nobody leaves the circle sitting in a bad morning.
