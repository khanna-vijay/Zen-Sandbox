# Small Print

![Small Print — infographic](img/IB__small-print.jpg){ .infographic }

> Three fast go-rounds of tiny true facts about the last few days, answered before anyone has time to polish it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

The room finds out what everyone's actual week has been like in under five minutes, and the fast handover means nobody gets to rehearse.

**Everyone has spoken within 110 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who answers flat and short, and who cannot resist adding context · Who is already listening to the person before them, and who is drafting their own answer · Who is comfortable being unremarkable in front of the group · Who has arrived frazzled and who has arrived settled

## Setup

Standing circles, no chairs, shoulders roughly a metre apart. No props. You stand outside the circle where everyone can see you call the categories.

## How to run it

1. (0:45) Form the circle. Explain: you call a category, the circle answers one at a time, five words maximum, and the next person starts within two seconds. Demo one yourself.
2. (1:00) Round one: "The last thing you ate." Go clockwise. Keep the pace tight; cut long answers with a friendly "next".
3. (1:00) Round two: "Something you forgot this week." Start with the person to the left of whoever finished last, and go anticlockwise.
4. (1:00) Round three: "One small thing you're looking forward to." Start somewhere new in the circle. Same speed.
5. (0:45) Close: ask the group to name one answer they still remember from someone else. Take three or four, then move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Five words. Don't improve it."*
- *"Next — go, go, go."*
- *"True and boring beats clever and late."*
- *"Say it to the far side of the circle, not the floor."*

## If it stalls

- If people freeze on "forgot", swap in something concrete: "the last app you opened" or "what woke you up today".
- If answers keep growing into stories, run a round where you count aloud to three for each person and cut on three. The pressure makes it lighter, not harsher.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose answer are you still thinking about?
2. Did anything get easier by round three?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run all three rounds. Hold answers to five words so each round lands in about sixty seconds. |
| 14–17 | Two circles of seven to nine, side by side. Both run every category at once on your call. You stand between them and cue the next round when the slower circle finishes. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten. Same three rounds, but drop the five-word rule to three words so both circles finish inside the minute. Skip the closing recall if you are over time. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Anyone may say "pass" and stays in the circle for the next round with no comment from you. Keep categories about the mundane last few days, not health, money or family.

## Lineage

In the Workshop check-in go-rounds tradition. Ordinary check-in circles let one person talk for a minute and leave the quiet ones unheard. This version fixes a word limit, a two-second handover and three rotating categories so the whole room speaks three times inside five minutes.
