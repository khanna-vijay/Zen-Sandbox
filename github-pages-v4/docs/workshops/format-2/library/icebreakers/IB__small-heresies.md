# Small Heresies

![Small Heresies — infographic](img/IB__small-heresies.jpg){ .infographic }

> A fast round where everyone names one thing the world overrates or underrates, flatly, with no defence allowed.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

Twenty honest, low-stakes opinions land in the room in under five minutes, and the group finds out who is dry, who is contrarian and who apologises before speaking.

**Everyone has spoken within 90 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who states a view flat and stops, and who cushions it with three apologies first · Who reaches for the contrarian answer and who names something genuinely mundane · Who is still talking at second six and needs the pass cue · Who tracks the whole lap and reacts, and who is rehearsing their own line

## Setup

Standing circle, no chairs, no props. If you have eighteen or more, split into two circles at opposite ends of the room before you explain.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Explain: everyone will name one thing that is overrated, or one thing that is underrated. Real opinion, said plainly. No jokes required. No arguing afterwards.
2. (60s) Turn to the person on your right. In pairs, both talk at once: give each other one overrated and one underrated. Ten seconds each item. Everyone has now spoken.
3. (2m 45s) Back to the circle. Go round clockwise. Each person says 'Overrated:' or 'Underrated:' plus one thing. Five seconds. Group nods, no comment, next.
4. (30s) Close: ask for a show of hands from anyone who heard something they violently disagreed with. Count the hands. Say nothing more. Move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Five seconds. Name it and pass."*
- *"Flat delivery. Don't sell it."*
- *"No justifying — the next person is waiting."*
- *"Boring is fine. Toothbrushes, Tuesdays, socks."*
- *"Nods only. Save the argument for the break."*

## If it stalls

- If someone freezes, say 'pass' out loud and move on immediately; return to them at the end of the lap.
- If people start explaining themselves, cut in with a category: food, weather, transport, weekends. Narrow prompts speed the lap up.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose opinion do you still want to fight about?
2. Who said something you'd never have guessed from looking at them?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run two full laps — second lap must be the opposite category to whatever they said first. Keep the pair stage to 45 seconds. |
| 14–17 | One circle, one lap only. Tighten side-coaching to five seconds each or you will overrun; drop the closing show of hands if you are behind. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten at opposite ends of the room, running simultaneously, one lap each. No one watches. Skip the closing count and just call time. |

## Safety & inclusion

Opinions about things, not about people in the room, and nothing about anyone's body. Anyone may pass without explanation and rejoin at the end of the lap. No debating during the exercise.

## Lineage

In the Parlour opinion games and radio quick-fire segments tradition. The overrated/underrated call-and-response is common in chat formats. We added the simultaneous pair stage so nobody waits more than sixty seconds to speak, capped each turn at five seconds so no one gets a spotlight, and banned justification so the round stays a round rather than a debate.
