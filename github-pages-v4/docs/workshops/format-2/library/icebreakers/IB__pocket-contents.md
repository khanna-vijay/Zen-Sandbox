# Pocket Contents

![Pocket Contents — infographic](img/IB__pocket-contents.jpg){ .infographic }

> Each person names one thing they are actually carrying today and why it is on them.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

The room stops being performers and becomes fifteen people with keys, plasters and a bus ticket. Everyone has already spoken out loud before the first note is given.

**Everyone has spoken within 90 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who edits before speaking and who says the first thing on them · Who deadpans a mundane object and who builds a case for it · Whose reasons are practical and whose are sentimental · Who tracks and remembers other people's objects in the echo lap

## Setup

Standing circle, no chairs, bags left at the edge of the room. People use only what is on their body right now — pockets, wrists, hair, shoes. No rummaging in bags.

## How to run it

1. Circle up. Say: name one object you are carrying on you right now, and one sentence about why it is on you today. (40s)
2. Model it yourself first, badly and fast — object, then reason, under eight seconds. Then point left to start. (30s)
3. Go round the circle. Cap each person at eight seconds. Keep the pointing hand moving; do not let reasons become anecdotes. (150s)
4. Second lap, no words: each person holds up or touches their object again while the group says the object name back in unison. (50s)
5. Close by naming two objects you want to see again later in the set. Move straight on. (30s)

## Side-coach

- *"Object first, reason second."*
- *"Eight seconds. Boring is fine."*
- *"Keep hold of it — we say it back."*
- *"Anything on you counts. Shoelace counts."*

## If it stalls

- If someone freezes, say 'shoes' and move on — they can rejoin in the second lap.
- If reasons balloon into stories, cut in with the next person's name; the pace matters more than the content.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose object surprised you?
2. Which reason told you more than the object did?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run both laps as written, and let a couple of the eight-second caps stretch to twelve. |
| 14–17 | One circle, but strict eight-second cap and only one lap of speech; keep the unison echo lap short by doing it in halves. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of nine or ten, each with a starter you nominate. Run both laps simultaneously, then reform one circle for the close. |

## Safety & inclusion

No touching other people or their belongings. Anyone may pass on the object and simply say their name; passing is a valid answer, not a failure.

## Lineage

In the Studio check-in circles tradition. Common check-in circles ask how you feel; we anchor to a physical object already on the body so nobody has to invent or perform an inner state. The unison echo lap was added so the group hears itself before the set.
