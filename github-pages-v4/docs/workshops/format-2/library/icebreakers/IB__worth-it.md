# Worth It

![Worth It — infographic](img/IB__worth-it.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone names one thing they paid for this week and gives it a one-word verdict.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

The room hears twenty small true things in three minutes, and finds out who is blunt, who elaborates and who has strong feelings about parking charges.

**Everyone has spoken within 120 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who gives a flat two-word verdict and who builds a case nobody asked for · Who is precise — brand, price, date — and who is vague on purpose · Who admits an embarrassing purchase in week four and who plays it safe · Who watches the circle and reacts, and who is rehearsing their own answer

## Setup

Standing circles, no chairs, shoulders loose. Ten to thirteen people make one circle; more than that, split into two even circles far enough apart that they don't overhear each other. Nothing else needed.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Form the circle(s). Give the rule: name one thing you paid for in the last seven days, then say 'worth it' or 'not worth it'. No explaining. Demo yourself in six seconds.
2. (75s) Round one, clockwise, no gaps. Six seconds each. Cut anyone who starts justifying with a friendly 'verdict only'. Everyone has spoken by this point.
3. (30s) Ask each circle: point at the one purchase you'd have made yourself. Count the fingers. Loudest cluster wins nothing.
4. (90s) Round two, same order, new prompt: one thing you refuse to spend money on, ever. Still one breath each. Let the flat answers stay flat.
5. (60s) Bring circles back together. Name two contrasts you heard out loud — one deadpan verdict, one that ran long. Then move straight into the warm-up.

## Side-coach

- *"Verdict only. No case for the defence."*
- *"Six seconds. Say it and pass it on."*
- *"Small purchases count. A coffee counts."*
- *"Don't wait to be interesting — just be accurate."*
- *"Keep it moving, the circle is faster than you think."*

## If it stalls

- If someone freezes, feed them a category: food, transport, something for the house.
- If people keep explaining themselves, run round two with a two-word limit and enjoy the wreckage.
- If the room is very quiet, drop to one round and spend the extra minute on the pointing count — the disagreement wakes them up.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose verdict surprised you?
2. Who said the most with the fewest words?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Six seconds each keeps round one under 80 seconds. Run both rounds as written. |
| 14–17 | Two circles, seven and seven or eight and nine. Both run simultaneously. You stand between them and call the time; don't try to hear every answer. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten. Cut each answer to five seconds and say so before you start. Skip the pointing count in the second circle if you are running behind. |
