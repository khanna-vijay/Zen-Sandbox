# Small Hills

![Small Hills — infographic](img/IB__small-hills.jpg){ .infographic }

> Each person names one trivial thing they are quietly firm about, and the room votes with their feet.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

The room finds out who is stubborn about small things, and how many people quietly agree with them. Everyone has said something true before the class starts.

**Everyone has spoken within 80 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who states it flat and who performs it · Who steps in eagerly for everything and who commits to almost nothing · Who is happy to be the only one standing on their hill · Who edits their answer before saying it

## Setup

Standing circle, arm's length apart, enough floor for one step forward each. No props. If you have 18 or more, form two circles at opposite ends of the room before you explain.

## How to run it

1. 0:00-0:40 Explain: a small hill is a trivial thing you're firm about. Give your own — "tea before milk, always". Say the rule: household-scale, not politics.
2. 0:40-1:20 Everyone turns to the person on their left and tells them their hill. All pairs talk at once. Call time at 40 seconds. Everyone has now spoken.
3. 1:20-4:15 Go round the circle. One sentence each, six to eight seconds. After each, anyone who agrees takes one step in, then steps back. Keep it moving.
4. 4:15-4:45 Name the hill that pulled the most people in. That person gets one sentence of defence. No debate.
5. 4:45-5:00 Close the circle and move straight into the next piece.

## Side-coach

- *"One sentence. Say it like it's obvious."*
- *"Step in if you agree — don't think about it."*
- *"Keep it small. Toast, not politics."*
- *"Next. Don't wait for a laugh."*
- *"Say it to the far side of the circle."*

## If it stalls

- People reach for big opinions. Cut in and give three categories: food, transport, tidying up. Take the next one from there.
- Someone freezes. Say "come back to me" and go on. Return to them at the end of the lap.
- Nobody steps in for anyone. Step in yourself once, honestly, and the rest follow.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose hill surprised you?
2. Did anyone stand completely alone and not mind?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run the full lap, then a lightning second lap where each person names one hill they'd happily steal. |
| 14–17 | One circle. Cap each statement at six seconds and drop the defence step at 4:15 — go round, then close. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten at opposite ends. You run one, appoint a confident regular to run the other. Both start the lap at 1:20 and stop at 4:15 whether finished or not; drop the defence step. |

## Safety & inclusion

Keep hills trivial and domestic. If someone offers a political or identity-based opinion, thank them and ask for a smaller one. Anyone may pass by saying "pass" — no follow-up. Stepping in is optional; standing still is a valid answer.

## Lineage

In the Ensemble circle warm-ups tradition. Built from the familiar step-in-if-this-is-true circle. Changed so the speaker supplies the statement rather than the facilitator, and added an opening simultaneous pair pass so every voice is used inside eighty seconds rather than only at the end of a slow lap.
