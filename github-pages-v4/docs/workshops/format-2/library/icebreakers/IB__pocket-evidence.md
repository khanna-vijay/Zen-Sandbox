# Pocket Evidence

![Pocket Evidence — infographic](img/IB__pocket-evidence.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone shows one real object they're carrying and gives the honest reason it's on them today.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

Real belongings put real lives in the room in under five minutes, and the group finds out who elaborates and who states the facts.

**Everyone has spoken within 90 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who over-explains and who lands a flat one-liner · Who is organised and who is carrying chaos · Who listens to their pair and who was rehearsing their own answer

## Setup

Standing circle, bags and coats within reach. Everyone finds one object they are actually carrying right now — pocket, bag, wrist, phone case. No one passes objects to anyone else.

## How to run it

1. 0:00-0:30 Circle up. Say: find one thing you are genuinely carrying today. Not the most interesting thing. The first thing your hand lands on.
2. 0:30-1:30 Turn to the person on your left. Thirty seconds each: what it is and the true reason it's on you today. Call the swap at fifteen seconds.
3. 1:30-4:15 Whip round the full circle. Hold the object up, one sentence, ten seconds each: what it is, why it's there. Move fast, no commentary.
4. 4:15-4:45 Say: hold it up if you'd notice within an hour that it was gone. Then: hold it up if you couldn't say when you last used it. Look round both times.
5. 4:45-5:00 Everything back in pockets. Name one object you want to hear more about later, then move straight on.

## Side-coach

- *"First thing your hand touches — don't shop for a better one."*
- *"Ten seconds. What it is, why it's there."*
- *"Hold it up so the far side of the circle can see it."*
- *"Boring is fine. A charger is an answer."*

## If it stalls

- Someone is carrying nothing: let them use their phone lock screen, a coat pocket, or the last thing they put in a bag this morning.
- If people start explaining for thirty seconds, cut in with the next name. The pace is the point.
- If the room stays flat, do a second lap where each person says only whether they'd replace it if they lost it today.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose object told you something you wouldn't have guessed?
2. Who explains, and who just states the fact?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run the whip round twice — second lap, one word each for how long they've owned it. |
| 14–17 | One circle, single whip round. Keep to eight seconds each and cut long talkers on the third one. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of nine or ten for the whip round. Run them at the same time; you float and keep both moving. Skip the second lap. |

## Safety & inclusion

Nobody hands over or touches another person's belongings. Anyone can describe an object without showing it, or pass and name something in their bag instead. No wallets, medication or anything private needs disclosing — say so before you start.

## Lineage

In the Show-and-tell object warm-ups common in improv and drama training tradition. Stripped the storytelling round down to ten seconds a person, added the simultaneous pair pass so everyone has spoken inside ninety seconds, and replaced the discussion ending with two silent hands-up questions so the group sees itself without anyone being put on the spot.
