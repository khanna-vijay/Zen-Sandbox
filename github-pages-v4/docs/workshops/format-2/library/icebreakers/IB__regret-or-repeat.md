# Regret or Repeat

![Regret or Repeat — infographic](img/IB__regret-or-repeat.jpg){ .infographic }

> A fast lap of the circle where everyone names the last thing they spent money on and delivers a one-word verdict on it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

The room hears twenty small true things about each other's actual week in under five minutes, and the verdicts land as personality rather than performance.

**Everyone has spoken within 75 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who states the bare fact and stops, and who cannot resist a justification. · Who admits to regret in public and who defends everything they bought. · Who edits themselves toward the acceptable answer and who says the embarrassing one first.

## Setup

Standing circle, bags and coats already down. No props. If you have more than seventeen people, mark two circle spots on the floor before they arrive.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Circle up. Set the task: the last non-essential thing you paid money for this week. Not rent, not bills. Give one example from your own week, five words, then a verdict.
2. (60s) Turn to the person on your left. Thirty seconds each way — tell them what it was and what happened after. Call the swap at halfway.
3. (100s) Round the circle. Each person: the thing in five words or fewer, then either 'again' or 'never again'. No explaining. Keep it moving.
4. (60s) Second lap, faster. One word only: how you felt at the moment you paid. Three seconds each.
5. (35s) Hands up if you'd buy the item the person on your right named. Count the hands out loud, thank them, move straight into the warm-up.

## Side-coach

- *"Five words, then the verdict."*
- *"No justifying — the verdict does the work."*
- *"Next. Keep the lap alive."*
- *"One word for the feeling. First word that arrives."*

## If it stalls

- People start telling the whole story. Cut in warmly: 'Verdict only.' Then take the next person immediately.
- Someone genuinely can't think of a purchase. Let them use the last thing they ate, downloaded or borrowed.
- A big laugh derails the lap. Let it land for two seconds, then say the next person's name.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose verdict surprised you?
2. Did anyone hear their own week in someone else's answer?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run a third lap: one word for whether you'd tell your family about it. Total stays five minutes by trimming the pair stage to 40 seconds. |
| 14–17 | One circle. Cut the pair stage to 40 seconds and hold both laps at three to four seconds per person. Skip the hands-up count if the second lap overruns. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of nine or ten on the marked spots. Appoint a timekeeper in each and run both laps simultaneously. Stand between them. Finish with one verdict called out from each circle to the whole room. |

## Safety & inclusion

Money is uneven in any room. Say up front that no amounts get mentioned and no purchase needs defending. Anyone can substitute a free thing they acquired, or pass with a raised hand and rejoin on the second lap.

## Lineage
