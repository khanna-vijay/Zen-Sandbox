# Correctly Rated

![Correctly Rated — infographic](img/IB__correctly-rated.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone delivers snap verdicts on ordinary things — overrated, underrated, correctly rated — and the room finds out who hedges and who commits.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

Real preferences land in the room in under a minute, and the group hears who states a thing flatly and who needs a paragraph of cover first.

**Everyone has spoken within 60 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who commits to a verdict flat and who hedges it with caveats · Who plays to the room for a laugh and who says the dull true thing · Who shouts 'why?' — the people willing to poke the group · Who has a niche obsession they've been waiting to be asked about

## Setup

Standing circle, no chairs. At 18-20 split into two circles of roughly ten before you explain. No props.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Explain. Three verdicts only: overrated, underrated, correctly rated. Subject must be an everyday thing — a food, a film, an appliance, a season. Real opinions, not jokes.
2. (60s) Pairs. Everyone talks at once. Thirty seconds each way, three verdicts each. Call the swap. Everyone has now spoken.
3. (15s) Back to the circle. Tell them each person gives one verdict, six words maximum, no reason attached.
4. (2m 30s) Whip round the circle. Anyone may shout 'why?' after a verdict; that person gets five seconds to justify, then you move on. Keep it fast.
5. (30s) Show of hands on the two verdicts that split the room hardest. Note the split out loud and move into the warm-up.

## Side-coach

- *"Six words. Verdict only."*
- *"Don't sell it — just rule on it."*
- *"If you want a reason, shout for it."*
- *"Next. Next. Keep it moving."*
- *"'Correctly rated' is a legal answer. Use it if you're stuck."*

## If it stalls

- If people freeze on choosing a subject, feed the category yourself: kitchen appliances, breakfast, the month of January, queuing.
- If justifications run long, cut on five seconds without apology. The pace is the point, and cutting people is a kindness here.
- If the whole room goes ironic and nothing is true, stop and name it: 'Give me one you'd actually argue about.'

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose verdict did you disagree with hardest, and did you say so?
2. Who in the circle needed a caveat before they'd commit?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. After the first lap, run a fast second lap where the only permitted verdict is 'underrated'. |
| 14–17 | One circle, single lap, and cap the 'why?' calls at five for the whole round so you finish on time. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of roughly ten. Run the pairs stage together, then send them apart. You take one circle; nominate a confident participant to keep the other moving and to cut at five seconds. |

## Safety & inclusion

Keep subjects to objects, food, weather, media. Rule out verdicts on people in the room, politics and appearance before you start. Anyone may say 'correctly rated' with no subject at all and pass through.

## Lineage

In the Studio warm-up circles and the overrated/underrated segment common to talk radio and podcasts. tradition. Written from scratch. Added the compulsory paired opening so nobody waits for the circle to speak, the six-word cap so no one holds the floor, and the audience-called 'why?' so justification is earned rather than volunteered.
