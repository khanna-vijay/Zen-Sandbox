# Tuesday at Seven

![Tuesday at Seven — infographic](img/IB__tuesday-at-seven.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone reports where they actually were last Tuesday evening, then boils it down to a three-word headline for the circle.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

The room finds out what each other's actual lives look like on a Tuesday, and hears how differently people edit the same size of story.

**Everyone has spoken within 45 seconds.**

**What it reveals about the group:** Who compresses to three flat words and who cannot stop at three · Who reaches for the flattering version of their evening and who offers the dull truth · Who listens hard in the pair and who is rehearsing their headline · Who is happy to be ordinary in front of strangers

## Setup

Standing circle, no chairs. Everyone needs a partner within arm's reach, so pair off odd numbers into one three. No props.

## How to run it

1. (0:30) Stand the circle. Give your own answer first, in one plain sentence: where you were last Tuesday at seven in the evening, and what you were doing.
2. (1:00) Pair everyone off. Both partners tell their real Tuesday at seven — thirty seconds each, you call the swap. Detail, not summary. Boring is allowed and encouraged.
3. (2:00) Back to the circle. Round the ring, each person gives their Tuesday as a three-word headline about themselves. Five seconds each. Keep the ring moving; don't stop for laughs.
4. (0:45) Fast show of hands: who had a completely typical Tuesday, who was somewhere new, who cannot remember Tuesday at all. Note the split out loud.
5. (0:45) Debrief on your feet. One question, two or three answers, then move straight into the warm-up.

## Side-coach

- *"Real Tuesday. Not a better Tuesday."*
- *"Three words. Now the next person."*
- *"Boring is fine — boring is information."*
- *"Say it to the far side of the circle, not to your shoes."*

## If it stalls

- Blank memories are common. Offer the escape hatch immediately: any recent evening you can actually picture, or yesterday at seven.
- If headlines drift into punchlines, ask the next three people for the flattest possible version. The room recalibrates within two turns.
- If the circle round drags, click your fingers on a five-second beat and keep clicking until it lands.

## Ask afterwards

1. Whose Tuesday do you still want to know more about?
2. What did three words leave out that mattered?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. After the headline round, go a second time round with one detail the headline hid — you have the time. |
| 14–17 | One circle, single headline round, strict five seconds each. Skip the second pass and keep the hands-up vote. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of nine or ten for the headline round, running at the same time. Stand between them. Bring both circles back together for the hands-up vote so the whole room sees the split. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Nobody has to disclose where they were — 'at home, doing nothing' is a complete and acceptable answer, and say so before you start. Anyone can substitute a different evening.

## Lineage

Written for this slot. Ordinary-detail check-ins are common in ensemble warm-ups; the three-word headline and the typicality vote are added here to make the round fast enough for twenty people and to surface who compresses and who expands.
