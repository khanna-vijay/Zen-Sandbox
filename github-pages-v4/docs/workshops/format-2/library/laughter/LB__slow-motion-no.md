# Slow-Motion No

![Slow-Motion No — infographic](img/LB__slow-motion-no.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room drops the same enormous film-trailer "nooooo" in slow motion, together, three times over.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

Twenty adults making one enormous ridiculous sound together, breath open, bodies loose, nobody in charge of being any good at it.

**Childhood borrow:** Playing out an over-the-top action-film death in slow motion in the playground, then rewinding it and doing it again.

## Setup

Everyone stands in one circle, arms' length apart, facing in. No chairs, no props. Clear anything people could knock over as they reach.

## How to run it

1. (0:30) Stand in the circle. Demonstrate once, full size: knees bend, arms reach up, a long low "noooooo" stretched over five slow seconds. Go bigger than you want to.
2. (1:00) All together on your count of three. Three reps. Everyone at the same time, everyone at their own volume. Reset to standing between reps.
3. (1:00) Add the reach. On "nooooo", both hands stretch to the ceiling, knees soften, chin lifts. Three more reps, all at once.
4. (1:00) Rewind. Same movement backwards, sound backwards too: a fast high squeaky "oooon" as you fold back to standing. Three reps.
5. (1:00) Free-for-all. Twenty seconds of everyone doing slow-mo and rewind at whatever speed they like, overlapping. Call "freeze" and hold two seconds.
6. (0:30) One question, then move straight on. Do not discuss it further.

## Side-coach

- *"Low and slow — let the breath do it, not the throat."*
- *"Reach higher than that. Ceiling."*
- *"Rewind — squeaky, fast, silly."*
- *"Nobody's watching you. Everyone's busy."*
- *"Freeze. Hold it. Breathe."*

## Getting past the cringe

Demonstrate before you explain, and go to full size on the first attempt — arms up, knees bent, five-second sound, no apology and no grin that says you know it's daft. Then count them straight in. Adults resist a description; they follow a facilitator who has already committed and is waiting for them.

## Opt-down

Stay in the circle, keep the arm reach, and mouth the sound with no voice. From outside the circle it looks identical, and the breath still does most of the work.

## If it stalls

- It usually stalls because you were too small. Do the next rep twice the size and hold the sound two seconds longer than feels sensible.
- If the room is quiet and stiff, drop volume instead of adding it: whisper the whole "nooooo" in slow motion. Whispering it as a group nearly always tips people over.
- If they laugh instead of finishing the sound, that's the point. Call the next rep immediately, don't wait for the room to settle.

## Ask afterwards

1. Where in your body is your energy now compared to two minutes ago? One word each, round the circle, fast.

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run all four rounds exactly as written. |
| 14–17 | One wider circle — push the walls. Cut the free-for-all to fifteen seconds; the noise builds faster with more voices. |
| 18–20 | Two concentric rings, inner ring facing out, outer ring facing in. Both rings go simultaneously on your count — no one watches, everyone sounds. Drop one rep from each round to stay inside five minutes. |

## Safety & inclusion

Sound comes from the breath, not a strained throat — no shouting. Anyone with a sore throat or vocal condition mouths it silently and keeps the movement. Keep the knee bend shallow; nobody goes to the floor. Arms' length spacing so no one is struck by a reach. No contact at any point.

## Lineage

In the Laughter yoga and physical clown warm-ups tradition. Laughter-yoga practice uses sustained deliberate vocalising in unison, and clown training uses slow-motion exaggeration of a single gesture. Combined the two and added the fast rewind, which is where the real laughter tends to arrive. Written from scratch.
