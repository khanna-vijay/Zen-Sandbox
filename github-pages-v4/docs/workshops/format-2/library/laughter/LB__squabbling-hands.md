# Squabbling Hands

![Squabbling Hands — infographic](img/LB__squabbling-hands.jpg){ .infographic }

> Everyone turns both hands into bickering puppets and lets them shout nonsense at each other for five minutes.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

Adults arrive holding their voices in. Five minutes of squeaking nonsense at their own hands drops the guard on the voice and puts the room in a loud, unbothered, playground state.

**Childhood borrow:** Making your hand talk and having it argue with your other hand.

## Setup

Standing circle, arm's length apart, chairs pushed back. No props. Everyone needs both hands free and room to hold them out in front of their face.

## How to run it

1. (30s) Say nothing first. Make your own hand talk — thumb against fingers, high squeaky gibberish, full volume. Then: both hands out, copy me.
2. (60s) Everyone at once: left hand and right hand argue in gibberish. Squeaky voices. Nobody watches anybody. You keep yours going the whole time.
3. (60s) Call the escalation: the hands are now furious. Louder, faster, more nonsense. Hands interrupt each other. Keep your own two hands shouting throughout.
4. (60s) Call: your hands find it hilarious. Both hands laugh — 'ha ha ha' in puppet voices, wobbling. Deliberate at first; it goes real fast.
5. (60s) Turn to the neighbour on your right. Puppets squabble across the gap in gibberish. No touching. Both people talk at once — nobody takes turns.
6. (30s) Call: hands are exhausted. They yawn, slump, fall asleep. Shake both arms out. Ask the one debrief question, take two answers, move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Higher voice — squeakier!"*
- *"Both hands talking at once, don't take turns."*
- *"Your hands are outraged. Let them shout over each other."*
- *"Now they think it's funny. Let them laugh."*
- *"Hands asleep. Shake it out."*

## Getting past the cringe

Start before you explain. Hand up, squeaky voice, full nonsense, ten seconds of it while they stare. Keep going through the silence without commenting on it. When you finally say 'both hands, copy me', you have already been sillier than anyone will need to be, so the floor is set low and nobody has to be first.

## Opt-down

Keep your hands low at chest height and murmur the gibberish instead of shouting. The hand movement carries it. Still in the circle, still doing the same thing, just at a quarter of the volume.

## If it stalls

- Your own hands go louder and dafter. Do not explain again — just keep your puppet arguing until theirs join in.
- If the room stays quiet, drop the words entirely: hands only make one sound each, 'bap bap bap' versus 'wee wee wee'. Easier to start than gibberish sentences.

## Ask afterwards

1. One word for how the room feels now — go.

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run all rounds as written. |
| 14–17 | One wide circle. For the neighbour round, count off pairs first so nobody is left facing a gap. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles at opposite ends of the room before you start. Run the same rounds in both simultaneously; you stand between them and call the changes loud enough for both. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact — puppets stay in your own airspace. Squeaky voices come from the front of the mouth, not the throat: tell them not to push volume through a raw voice. Anyone with vocal strain or a cold mouths it silently with full hand movement.
