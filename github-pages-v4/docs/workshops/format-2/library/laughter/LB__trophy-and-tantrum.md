# Trophy and Tantrum

![Trophy and Tantrum — infographic](img/LB__trophy-and-tantrum.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room swings between full-body victory celebration and full-body playground strop on your call, faster and faster, for five minutes.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

A room that arrived guarded ends up stamping and roaring in unison, with the switch happening fast enough that nobody has time to monitor how they look.

**Childhood borrow:** The playground tantrum after losing a game, and the lap of honour after winning one.

## Setup

Standing circle, arm's length between people, bags and chairs pushed to the walls. No props. You stand in the circle, not outside it.

## How to run it

1. (40s) Frame it: two settings only. WON means arms up, running on the spot, roaring. LOST means stamping, folded arms, wailing NOT FAIR. Demonstrate both yourself at full size first.
2. (40s) Call WE WON. Everyone celebrates at once for the full forty seconds. Keep roaring with them. Nobody watches anybody; everyone is busy.
3. (40s) Call WE LOST. Everyone strops at once. Stamp, huff, wail NOT FAIR. Go louder and sulkier than anyone in the room.
4. (80s) Now alternate. Call WON and LOST at random, starting five seconds apart and squeezing down to one second apart. Bodies flip mid-movement. That's the point.
5. (60s) Split the circle down the middle. One half won, one half lost, facing each other, both at full volume for twenty seconds. Swap. Swap again.
6. (40s) Final call: everyone won AND lost at the same time, ten seconds of both. Then shake out arms and legs. Ask the one debrief question. Move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Louder than you think is sensible."*
- *"Whole body, not just the face."*
- *"Stamp the floor, don't jump."*
- *"Flip it now — no transition, straight in."*
- *"Sulk like you're eight and it's raining."*

## Getting past the cringe

Start mid-strop before you finish explaining. Stamp, fold your arms, wail NOT FAIR at full volume, then say the rules from inside it. Do not look around checking whether people joined. Never comment on anyone holding back — just be the loudest person in the room for the first thirty seconds and they arrive.

## Opt-down

Play it seated or from standing with arms and face only — same volume, no stamping. Stay in the circle and keep the sound; the noise is the participation.

## If it stalls

- You dropped first. Go back to your own full-size version and hold it for ten seconds without commenting on the room; commitment is contagious and silence about it stops people self-monitoring.
- If the celebration stays polite, drop it and stay on the strop — adults find sulking easier to commit to than joy, and the laugh comes from there.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which one was easier to commit to — winning or sulking?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run exactly as written. |
| 14–17 | One wider circle; step back so everyone has a full arm span. In the split section, split into two arcs facing across the circle. |
| 18–20 | Two facing lines down the length of the room instead of a circle. Add one extra swap in the split section (four swaps of fifteen seconds) so nobody waits. |

## Safety & inclusion

Stamping only, no jumping — hard floors and knees. Keep an arm's length of space and no contact at any point. Anyone with a voice, back or knee issue takes the opt-down version, which is a normal way to play, not sitting out.

## Lineage

In the Deliberate-laughter practice and clown emotional-dial work tradition. Written from scratch. It borrows the sustained-deliberate-laughter principle from laughter yoga and the full-body emotional switch from clown training, but the win/lose flip and the playground strop are ours. Chosen for a short-form week because it puts competitive noise in the room without anyone competing.
