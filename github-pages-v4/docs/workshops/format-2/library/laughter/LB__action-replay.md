# Action Replay

![Action Replay — infographic](img/LB__action-replay.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room celebrates a goal that never happened, then replays the celebration in slow motion.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

A room that arrived from work carrying its own volume limit finds out the limit isn't real, and does something loud together without anyone deciding what it should mean.

**Childhood borrow:** Kids in a playground scoring an imaginary winner, then doing the action replay in slow motion with their own commentary.

## Setup

Clear the floor. Spread everyone into a loose blob facing you, an arm's length apart in every direction. No chairs, no props, shoes on.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Tell them: a goal has just gone in. You go first, alone — arms up, full roar, knee slide mimed on the spot. Then check everyone has elbow room.
2. (45s) Count down from three. Everyone celebrates at full volume at the same moment, five seconds, then you call 'FREEZE'. Repeat twice more. Nobody watches anybody.
3. (60s) Call 'REPLAY'. Everyone repeats the same celebration in slow motion, roar dropped to a low groan. You groan loudest. Freeze at the end. Run it twice.
4. (60s) Call 'FAST FORWARD'. Same celebration at double speed, voices high and squeaky, five seconds. Freeze. Run it three times, tighter each go.
5. (60s) Now alternate on your call: 'SLOW' — 'FAST' — 'SLOW' — 'FAST', roughly every eight seconds. Keep switching until the room is wheezing. Call 'FINAL WHISTLE'.
6. (30s) Shake it out. Ask the one debrief question. Take a few answers, no discussion, move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Arms up, not out — keep your neighbour's space."*
- *"Roar from the belly, not the throat."*
- *"Slow means slow. Slower than that."*
- *"Squeakier. Ridiculous is the target."*
- *"Freeze. Hold it. And again."*

## Getting past the cringe

Do the first celebration entirely on your own, before you count anyone in, at a volume that is slightly too much for the room. Don't explain it and don't apologise for it. Then say "that's the level" and start the countdown. The permission comes from your body, not from asking them to commit.

## Opt-down

Do it at half volume with arms only, staying in the blob and hitting every freeze on the same beat as everyone else. Silent is fine — the timing is the participation.

## If it stalls

- Your own volume has dropped. Go louder than anyone and the room follows within one round.
- Cut the full-speed rounds and live in slow motion — the low groan gets people laughing faster than the roar.
- Add a rule that everyone must end each freeze pointing at the ceiling. A shared shape beats a shared feeling.

## Ask afterwards

1. What did your face do that it hasn't done all week?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One blob. Run the sequence as written, ending with four alternating calls. |
| 14–17 | One blob, but spread to two arms' length. Cut one full-speed round to keep the total at five minutes. |
| 18–20 | Two blobs at opposite ends of the room, both facing you, both doing every round at the same time. Cut one fast-forward round. Nobody sits out and nobody spectates. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact, no pile-ons, no hugging strangers in the excitement — say this before round one. Knee slides are mimed standing up; nobody goes to the floor. Keep knees soft on the freezes. Anyone with a sore voice or a bad back uses the opt-down. Watch for people drifting into each other's space during slow motion and reset the spacing between rounds.

## Lineage

Built from scratch out of two things adults already know how to do: shout at a screen when a goal goes in, and watch the replay. Slow motion was added because a low collective groan makes people laugh harder and faster than a cheer does, and because it slows the room enough to keep the space safe.
