# Slow-Motion Sneeze

![Slow-Motion Sneeze — infographic](img/LB__slow-motion-sneeze.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room builds one enormous sneeze together, over and over, in different sizes.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

Loud breath, loose bodies, and a room that has already made an ugly noise in front of each other — so the next hour costs less.

**Childhood borrow:** Fake-sneezing at the dinner table to make someone spit out their drink.

## Setup

One circle, everyone at arm's length with space to fling their arms. No chairs in the way. Nothing else needed.

## How to run it

1. (0:30) Stand in the circle. Say nothing clever. Do one enormous full-body sneeze yourself — long shaky build-up, huge wet CHOO, total flop. Then say: everyone, together.
2. (1:00) Round one, slow build. Lead the count: four seconds of rising 'aaah', arms lifting, heads tilting back, then all sneeze on your signal. Repeat three times.
3. (1:00) Round two, mouse sneeze. Same build, but the release is tiny and squeaky, whole body shrinking to a pinprick. Three of these. You squeak loudest.
4. (1:15) Round three, opera sneeze. Build on a rising sung note, arms wide, then sneeze in full voice and collapse forward from the waist. Three times, each bigger.
5. (0:45) Rapid fire. Five sneezes in a row, any size, no build-up, everyone at once, on your count of five. Let it become noise.
6. (0:30) Stop. One question, quick answers, no discussion. Move straight on.

## Side-coach

- *"Ugly is correct — nobody is grading the noise."*
- *"Let the build-up take four whole seconds. Wait for it."*
- *"Loose neck. Flop forward, don't snap."*
- *"Again, bigger, and don't apologise for it."*
- *"All together — nobody is watching anybody."*

## Getting past the cringe

Do the first sneeze yourself, at full disgusting volume, before you finish explaining. Don't look for permission and don't smirk. Then count them in immediately — no gap for anyone to decide it's embarrassing.

## Opt-down

Sit or stand still and do sound only — the build-up breath and a quiet release, no arms, no flop. Still on the count, still with everyone.

## If it stalls

- The room is being polite and quiet. Do three on your own at full volume without commenting on theirs, then count them in again.
- Nobody commits to the build-up. Slow the count down out loud — 'one, two, three, four' — so the release has somewhere to come from.
- Laughter arrives late. That's normal; it usually lands in the opera round. Don't chase it, just keep the sizes changing.

## Ask afterwards

1. One word for how your body feels right now.

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run all rounds as written. |
| 14–17 | One wide circle. Check arm's-length spacing before you start; the opera round needs room for wide arms. |
| 18–20 | Split into two lines facing each other, three metres apart. Same rounds, but the two lines sneeze at each other. Cut one rapid-fire sneeze to stay on time. |

## Safety & inclusion

Turn your head away from neighbours on the release and cover with an elbow. Keep the neck loose — flop, never whip the head. Anyone with neck, back or dizziness issues does it seated and small. No contact at any point.

## Lineage

In the Clown and physical-comedy warm-ups; deliberate-laughter practice tradition. Clown training uses the exaggerated sneeze as a commitment exercise, usually one person at a time in front of the group. Rewritten so the whole room sneezes simultaneously, with fixed sizes called by the facilitator, so there is no performance and nothing to judge.
