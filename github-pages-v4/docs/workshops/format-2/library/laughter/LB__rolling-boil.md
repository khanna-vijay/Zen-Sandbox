# Rolling Boil

![Rolling Boil — infographic](img/LB__rolling-boil.jpg){ .infographic }

> The whole room crouches down as kettles, hums up through the pitches and boils over into laughing.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 1/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## The point

A room breathing hard, red-faced and grinning, having made one loud stupid noise together where nobody was keeping score.

**Childhood borrow:** Making a machine noise with your whole body — sirens, kettles, rockets — just to hear how loud you can get.

## Setup

One circle, arm's length apart, chairs pushed back. Everyone needs room to crouch and stand. No props.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Stand in the circle. Demonstrate first, alone and full volume: crouch low, hum your lowest note, rise slowly, let the hum climb, hit a kettle whistle at the top, then laugh it out.
2. (45s) Round one, everyone together. Crouch. Lowest hum. Count them up over eight seconds. At the top, whistle, then shake out. Round one is usually thin. Say so, cheerfully.
3. (60s) Round two, slower climb, louder. Hold the whistle until breath runs out, then everyone drops into laughing for ten seconds. You laugh loudest and longest.
4. (60s) Round three. Same climb, but at the top nobody stops — keep the belly laugh going for a full twenty seconds while bouncing on the spot. Count the seconds out.
5. (60s) Simmer. Everyone stays standing and bubbles: small random 'pff' and 'ha' noises, no pattern, all at once, until the room is one soft rolling boil. Cut it with a clap.
6. (30s) Shake hands out. One question, answers shouted over each other, then move straight on.

## Side-coach

- *"Lowest note you own — from the belly, not the throat."*
- *"Climb slower. You're not there yet."*
- *"Let the whistle crack. Cracking is the point."*
- *"Keep laughing past the point it feels real — it comes back."*
- *"Bubble, don't perform. Small noises, all of you."*

## Getting past the cringe

Go first, alone, before explaining anything. Crouch on the floor and hum the ugliest low note you have, then whistle and laugh at full volume while they're still standing there. Don't apologise, don't grin sheepishly, don't wait for permission. Then say 'that's it, all together' and count them in immediately. The instruction is your own commitment, not a request.

## Opt-down

Stay standing, keep the hum quiet, and raise your hands from floor to ceiling instead of your body. Breathing out audibly on the laugh counts as laughing.

## If it stalls

- If the hum stays polite, drop to a crouch yourself and go twice as loud as anyone else. Volume is contagious; instruction isn't.
- If people laugh at each other instead of together, close the circle tighter and tell them to look at the floor, not around the room.
- If round three dies at eight seconds, keep counting to twenty on your own with your own laugh running. It restarts.

## Ask afterwards

1. Shout one word for how your chest feels right now.

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Three climbing rounds plus the simmer, as written. |
| 14–17 | One wider circle. Drop round two to a forty-second version so the timings still fit. |
| 18–20 | Two concentric rings, inner facing out, outer facing in, everyone doing every round simultaneously. Nobody watches. Volume carries itself; cut your own count shorter. |

## Safety & inclusion

Crouching is optional — bend at the knees only as far as is comfortable, or stay standing and lower your hands instead. Sustained loud laughing makes some people light-headed; tell them to stop and breathe normally if so, and to stay standing still rather than walking off. No contact at any point.

## Lineage

In the Laughter yoga and choral vocal warm-ups tradition. Laughter practices commonly use gradually escalating deliberate laughter, and singers use sirens to climb pitch. Rules written from scratch here. The kettle image was added because it gives adults an object to be rather than an instruction to be silly, which lowers resistance, and the simmer phase was added to land the room rather than cut it dead.
