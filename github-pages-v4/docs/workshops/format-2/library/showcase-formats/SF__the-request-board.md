# The Request Board

![The Request Board — infographic](img/SF__the-request-board.jpg){ .infographic }

> Eight short games chalked on a board, chosen by the audience, run to a bell, with a rota so nobody has to volunteer.

`Players 10–20` · `~35 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact incidental` · `Props: required`

## The show

Eight game names are chalked on a board where the audience can read them, and the audience picks which ones get played. A host takes a suggestion, rings a bell after three minutes, and the next batch of players steps up. It runs about an hour: fast, loud, no long scenes, and nobody in the audience is dragged onstage.

## The night — 65 minutes

| Min | Segment | What happens |
|---:|---|---|
| 10 | **Doors and cards** | Front of house welcomes people, hands out cards and pens, asks each person to write one location, object or relationship. Cards go in a bowl by the host chair. |
| 4 | **Welcome and house rules** | Host names the cast, points at the board, explains that the audience picks the games and that the bell ends each one. Warns that the bell is loud. |
| 6 | **Opening game — Photo Album** | Whole cast in rotating groups of four freeze into holiday photos while a narrator captions them. Gets every performer onstage in the first ten minutes. |
| 15 | **Board round one** | Three games chosen by audience shout or by card. Host takes one suggestion each, calls the rota, rings the bell at three minutes. |
| 5 | **Stretch break** | House lights up. Audience stands, drinks, adds more cards to the bowl. Board keeper crosses off played games. Hosts swap if there are two. |
| 16 | **Board round two** | Three more games from the board, same mechanic. Save Freeze and Swap or Genre Rewind for the last slot — they carry the most energy. |
| 7 | **Closing game and bow** | Whole cast plays Baton Story on a final audience suggestion, then lines up. Host thanks the audience by name where possible and names every role, including tech and door. |
| 2 | **Foyer** | Cast walks out into the foyer still in costume to meet their guests. House lights up, music on, no lingering backstage. |

## Jobs

- Host — takes suggestions, reads the board, rings the bell, keeps the night moving
- Board keeper — chalks the eight games, crosses off played ones, tracks the rota out loud
- Bell and lights — cues blackouts or lights-down endings, holds the bell when the host is playing
- Front of house — door, cards, seating, latecomers, counting heads
- Card wrangler — sorts unusable suggestions out of the bowl before the host reaches in
- Cast — everyone plays, everyone has a printed rota slot

## Setup

Chalkboard or flipchart facing the audience with eight game names on it. A hand bell, a host chair, a printed rota, and one everyday household object. Audience in a shallow arc; cast in a line upstage on chairs.

## How to run it

1. Chalk eight game names on a board the audience can read. Set a bell, a host chair, and a rota sheet naming who plays each round. Cast waits in an upstage line.
2. Each round: an audience member picks a game off the board. Host takes one suggestion, repeats it loudly, calls the rota names. Bell at three minutes. Everyone applauds; players sit.
3. Photo Album: four players freeze into a pose on a clap; a narrator names the holiday photo. Sound Booth: two play a scene, two at the edge voice every sound.
4. Emotional Dial: a pair does a household chore; the host calls an emotion and a number one to ten, and they play it at that strength until the bell.
5. Freeze and Swap: a pair moves through a scene; anyone calls freeze, replaces one player in their exact shape, and starts a fresh scene from that position.
6. Baton Story: five stand in a line telling one story. Host points; that teller stops mid-word, the next continues. Fumbles get a bow and applause, and play continues.
7. Double Talk: one plays an expert speaking invented language on the audience's topic; a partner translates. Prop Parade: cast queues and mimes new uses for one household object, no naming.
8. Genre Rewind: a pair plays ninety seconds, then replays the same scene in a genre the audience names. Close with the whole cast, a bow, and the host's thanks.

## Side-coach

- *"Say the suggestion out loud before you start playing."*
- *"Play the task, not the joke."*
- *"Face the back row when you speak."*
- *"Bell means finish your sentence and stop."*
- *"If you are lost, copy what your partner is doing."*

## How block 9 builds towards it

1. Week 1 (30 min): teach Photo Album, Sound Booth and Emotional Dial. Everyone plays each once. No clock, no audience, stop and fix rules as you go.
2. Week 2 (35 min): teach Freeze and Swap, Baton Story and Prop Parade. Introduce the bell at three minutes and practise ending cleanly on it.
3. Week 3 (35 min): teach Double Talk and Genre Rewind. Then everyone takes the host chair for one round — taking a suggestion, repeating it, calling names.
4. Week 4 (35 min): half the class sits as audience and shouts suggestions, including bad ones so hosts practise asking for another. Swap halves at the midpoint.
5. Weeks 5–6 (35 min each): full board with roles rotating each round. At the end of week 6, vote on the final eight games, lock the rota and assign every offstage job.
6. Week 7 (40 min): dress run in the performance space, doors to bow, with another class as audience. Notes on logistics and volume only — no acting notes.

## Looking after the audience

Two students on the door greet everyone by name where they can, hand out a card and pen, and explain the one-word suggestion in a sentence. Seat people in a shallow arc close to the playing space; keep the front row within three metres. The host says plainly at the top that nobody will be brought onstage and that the bell is loud. At the close, house lights come straight up, music plays, and the cast comes out to the foyer rather than disappearing backstage.

!!! warning "What makes it fail"
    Admin eats the night. A nervous host explains each game for ninety seconds, takes three suggestions, forgets the rota, and the audience watches more rule-reading than play. The second failure is the cast chasing laughs: players stop doing the task, start commenting on the game, and the round dies long before the bell — which nobody rings, because ringing it feels rude. Rehearse the host's fifteen-second game explanations and rehearse ringing the bell early.

## If it stalls

- A round goes flat: host rings early, thanks the players, crosses the game off the board, moves on.
- The audience will not shout: read from the cards collected at the door instead.
- Cast crowds the edges: send half of them to seats offstage until their rota round.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which game gave you the most to actually do?
2. Where did you start performing at the audience instead of playing with your partner?
3. What does the rota or the running order need before next week?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Six games plus the opener and closer. One host for the whole night. Each player appears in three rounds. Front of house doubles as board keeper after the doors close. |
| 14–17 | All eight games. Two hosts, swapping at the stretch break. Each player appears in two rounds plus the whole-cast opener and closer. Two on front of house, one on bell and lights. |
| 18–20 | All eight games, cast split into two teams: Team A plays rounds one to three, Team B rounds four to six. Each player appears once in a small game plus both whole-cast games. Three on front of house. |

## Safety & inclusion

Freeze and Swap uses a spoken call, not a tap; anyone who prefers no proximity says so in rehearsal and the replacement stands a metre clear. Any player may pass on a rota slot on the night with no explanation. The host filters suggestions and says "give me another" to anything cruel or personal, and practises saying it. Warn the room that the bell is loud.

## Lineage

In the Short-form improv game nights and impro match formats tradition. Source: *Impro for Storytellers (1999)*. The competitive scoring, judges and losing team are removed — beginners perform worse when ranked. Added a visible board so the audience chooses, and a printed rota so no one has to volunteer in front of their family.
