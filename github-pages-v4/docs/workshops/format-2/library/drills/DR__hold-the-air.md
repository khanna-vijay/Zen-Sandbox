# Hold the Air

![Hold the Air — infographic](img/DR__hold-the-air.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs play three-line scenes with an enforced silence before every line, so students get repeated practice at holding a pause in front of watching people without filling it.

`Players 10–20` · `~26 min` · `Complexity 4/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it isolates

Holding a silence in front of watching people without filling it. Lines are capped at three per scene and eight words each, and the facilitator controls when speech is permitted, so there is no plot to build, no game to find and no way to escape into talking. All that remains is the wait and what the player does inside it.

**Reps per person:** At 14 people: two scenes each, six enforced silences per scene, so about twelve held pauses. At 20 people: one guaranteed scene each plus a four-pair volunteer round — fewer reps, tell them that at the start so nobody is waiting for a second go.

## Setup

Chairs in a shallow arc facing a small playing area, two chairs on the playing area. You stand at the side of the playing area, in both players' peripheral vision without them turning their heads. No props.

## How to run it

1. Two minutes. Set the rules: three spoken lines per scene, shared between the pair, eight words maximum each. While your hand is raised, nobody speaks. Watchers stay silent.
2. Three minutes. Everyone stands and holds stillness for thirty seconds, twice. Name the fills you see out loud as they happen: shifting weight, smiling, scratching, checking the room.
3. Two minutes. Demo one scene yourself with a volunteer. Hold your hand up five seconds, drop it, take one line, raise it again. Point out every urge to fill.
4. Nine minutes. Pass one. Each pair takes the chairs. Give them a relationship in two words. Five-second floor before each of the three lines. Cap any scene at ninety seconds.
5. Eight minutes. Pass two, new partners, ten-second floor. After each scene, watchers vote on three: thumb up if something was happening in the silence, flat hand if it was just waiting.
6. Two minutes. Debrief seated. Take answers from watchers first, players second.

## Side-coach

- *"Don't fill. Feet still, breath low."*
- *"Let them see you thinking about it."*
- *"Wait until you actually want the line."*
- *"Watchers: no rescuing, no laughing them out."*
- *"Longer than feels sane. Keep looking at your partner."*

## Build it up

- Raise the floor: five seconds, then ten, then fifteen, called mid-block without stopping.
- Take the chairs away. Players stand, hands at sides, no leaning on anything.
- Silence one player entirely — only their partner may use the three lines, and all three must be answered without words.

## The same drill under load

Run pass two with the watchers permitted to make noise: shifting in chairs, coughing, one nervous laugh each. Players must hold the floor anyway and may not speak early. Then add a second load: the player not speaking must hold eye contact with one named watcher throughout.

## If it stalls

- Giggling is normal on the first two pairs. Name it once, reset them, carry on. Do not make it a discipline issue.
- If a pair goes vacant, give each of them a private one-word feeling before they start and tell them to keep it alive without showing it.
- If a pair never uses all three lines, that is a pass, not a failure. Say so and move on.

## Ask afterwards

1. What was actually happening in you during the ten seconds?
2. Which silences looked longer from the chairs — yours or the ones you watched?
3. How long will a room genuinely wait before it needs something?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Five or six pairs. Both passes fit comfortably. Add a third pass with the same partner at a fifteen-second floor and only two lines allowed. |
| 14–17 | Seven or eight pairs. Run exactly as written and keep the ninety-second cap strictly, or pass two will overrun. Give relationships fast, no discussion. |
| 18–20 | Nine or ten pairs. For pass one, split into two half-rooms with their own audience arc; brief a hand-signaller in each for thirty seconds. Reconvene and run pass two with four volunteer pairs while everyone else watches. Everyone gets one scene, four get two. Say that up front. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Sustained stillness and eye contact can bring feelings up quickly; tell the room that any player may end a scene by lowering their gaze and walking to a seat, with no explanation asked for or given. Watchers do not comment on anyone's body or face during feedback — only on whether the silence felt occupied.

## Lineage

In the Actor-training stillness work and British improv status teaching tradition. Source: *Impro (1979)*. Status-through-stillness and do-nothing exercises are long-standing in actor training. We stripped out the status content and the scene content entirely, capped the words so no plot can form, and added a facilitator-controlled floor on the pause length so students cannot cut a silence short. The audience thumb vote was added because this course is about playing to a room.
