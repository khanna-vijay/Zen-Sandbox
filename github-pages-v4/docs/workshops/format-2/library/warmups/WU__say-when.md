# Say When

![Say When — infographic](img/WU__say-when.jpg){ .infographic }

> In pairs, one person talks about something ordinary while the other decides the exact moment to cut them — and then holds the silence.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention on a partner's rising energy, and the nerve to act on it. Trains the felt sense of the top of a beat and the discipline of not filling the gap afterwards — the two decisions the session is about, rehearsed at conversational scale before anyone touches a scene.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Pairs standing face to face, roughly a metre apart, spread around the room so voices don't collide. No props. If numbers are odd, make one trio and have the two listeners share the cut.

## How to run it

1. Brief and demo (45s). Talker speaks about something dull and true: the journey in, breakfast, a queue. Listener raises a flat palm to cut, then says three words naming why.
2. Round one (60s). A talks, B watches for a rise in energy or interest and cuts there. Not at a full stop. Three words. Then swap and reset.
3. Round two (60s). B talks, A cuts. Push cutters to go earlier than feels polite — mid-sentence, on the lift, before the story flattens.
4. Held beat (75s). Same again both ways, but after the palm goes up, both hold eye contact in silence for three slow seconds before the three words land.
5. Silent cut (60s). Final pass, either direction. Palm only, no words, then three seconds of shared quiet. Stop the room on the third second.

## Side-coach

- *"Cut on the rise, not the fall."*
- *"Don't wait for a full stop — that's already late."*
- *"Palm up. Three words. Then quiet."*
- *"Let the quiet one breathe. Count it."*
- *"Talkers, keep going until the hand stops you."*

## Build it up

- Cutters go silent: palm only, no three words, and hold eye contact for a full four seconds before releasing.
- Talker must resume after the cut and land one closing sentence in a single breath.
- Make trios: two listeners, either can cut, and they must not signal each other first.

## If it stalls

- Cutters being polite and never cutting: give a hard rule — every cut inside fifteen seconds, no exceptions.
- Talkers drying up: hand them a topic out loud — the last thing you queued for, the state of your kitchen.
- Silence collapsing into giggles: shorten the hold to two seconds and count it aloud for the whole room.

## Ask afterwards

1. What told you it was time to cut — the words, the volume, or something in their face?
2. Which felt harder: cutting early, or holding the three seconds afterwards?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | All pairs work at once. Run all five steps as written; the room stays quiet enough to hear your side-coaching. |
| 14–17 | All pairs at once, spread to the walls. Ask talkers to drop to conversational volume so palms and cuts stay readable across the noise. |
| 18–20 | Nine or ten pairs plus one trio, split to opposite ends of the room. Drop the silent-cut round to fifteen seconds a side to hold five minutes. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. The cut signal is a flat palm raised near the cutter's own shoulder, never pushed toward a partner's face. Topics stay mundane — nobody is asked to disclose anything personal, and anyone can swap topic mid-round.

## Lineage

Sits in the same family as long-form edit drills, where students practise calling an ending rather than waiting for one. Built here as a paired exercise so the decision is made about a real person in front of you, with a mandatory held silence bolted on — the cut and the beat after it trained as one unit rather than two.
