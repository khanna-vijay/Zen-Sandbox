# Come Back Bigger

![Come Back Bigger — infographic](img/WU__come-back-bigger.jpg){ .infographic }

> Three standing groups relay one move through three beats, each return larger and later than the last.

`Players 8–24` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Legs, lungs and voice come up fast, and peripheral attention widens across three groups. It puts Format Literacy in the body: the room feels a beat two as the same material later and bigger, before anyone has to build one in a scene.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Clear the floor. Split the room into three groups and stand them in a wide triangle, each group facing the middle with arm's length between bodies. No props.

## How to run it

1. (0:30) Form the triangle. Everyone jogs or marches on the spot and keeps that engine running for the whole exercise. Explain: three groups, three beats, one move.
2. (0:45) Demonstrate. Make a two-second move with a sound — a shove and a grunt. Say: beat two is the same move, later and bigger. Beat three, bigger again.
3. (1:00) Group A invents a move on your clap; all of them do it together. Clap again: Group B performs A's move heightened. Clap: Group C heightens it further.
4. (1:00) Repeat with Group B originating, then Group C. Everyone keeps jogging between beats. Clap the beats about four seconds apart.
5. (1:00) Faster round. Clap every two seconds. Same rule: return the move, do not invent a new one.
6. (0:45) Final pass. Group A fires its original move again; all three groups perform it at full size together, then stop dead. Hold the silence for three seconds.

## Side-coach

- *"Keep the engine running — jog between beats."*
- *"Same move. More of it."*
- *"Don't invent. Return."*
- *"Later and truer, not louder and different."*
- *"Full size on beat three — no half measures."*

## Build it up

- Halve the gap between claps so groups return the move on instinct rather than discussion.
- Ban the originating group from repeating themselves — beat three must come only from watching beat two.
- Add a fourth beat where all three groups perform the move simultaneously at maximum size, then freeze.

## If it stalls

- Groups drift into inventing new moves. Stop, name the original out loud — 'the shove and the grunt' — and run that chain again from beat one.
- Moves get too intricate to copy. Cap them at two seconds and one sound.
- This one gets messy the first time through and usually lands on the second chain. Say so and keep going rather than re-explaining.

## Ask afterwards

1. What made a move easy to return — and what made one impossible?
2. Where did heightening stop being bigger and start being different?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Three groups of three or four. Run four chains: each group originates once, then a bonus chain from Group A. |
| 14–17 | Three groups of five or six. Three chains, one per group, plus the fast round. Drop the bonus chain. |
| 18–20 | Four groups in a square, so each chain runs four beats instead of three. Cut the fast round to keep to five minutes. |

## Safety & inclusion

Jogging on the spot is optional — marching or standing weight-shifts count. Keep an arm's length between bodies and no travelling across the floor. Moves stay above the knee and off the neck. No contact at any point.

## Lineage

Written for this slot. The relay of a single move through escalating beats is built from Harold beat logic rather than from any published game; the standing triangle and the return-don't-invent rule are ours.
