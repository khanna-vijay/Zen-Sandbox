# Pass Is a Full Answer

![Pass Is a Full Answer — infographic](img/WU__pass-is-a-full-answer.jpg){ .infographic }

> Two facing lines trade small gestures across a gap, and everyone has to say "pass" at least once so refusing stops feeling rude.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Wakes the arms and shoulders gently, gets eyes onto one other face, and settles the group pulse into paired attention. It feeds D2.S6 Boundary Navigation directly: people practise saying no and receiving no in a low-stakes physical exchange, so the word "pass" is already in the room before any scene work. It also gives the first honest reading of the session essence — an offer can be declined and the exchange keeps going.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Clear a corridor down the middle of the room. Two lines face each other, one partner opposite each person. No props. No touching at any point.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Form two facing lines. Tell each pair to set their own gap — arm's length or three metres, whatever both want. Demonstrate one small gesture: a shrug, a wave, a stamp.
2. (60s) Line A offers a gesture. Line B either copies it back, or says "pass" — then A offers a different one. Keep offering until time. Rule: everyone in B must pass at least once.
3. (60s) Swap. Line B offers, line A copies or passes. Same rule — every A passes at least once, whether they want to or not.
4. (75s) Line A shifts one place to the left; the end person walks to the other end. New partner. Now either side can offer at any moment, and either side can pass. Add a sound to each gesture.
5. (60s) Call stop. Partners face each other and each says one sentence: what it felt like to hear "pass". Then step back into a loose circle for the next activity.

## Side-coach

- *"Small gestures. Nothing you'd need a warm-up for."*
- *""Pass" is a complete answer. No reason after it."*
- *"If you get a pass, offer something else straight away."*
- *"Check the gap. Move back if you want more room."*
- *"Nobody has run out of ideas. Repeat one you've already used."*

## Build it up

- Offers become two-step sequences — a gesture and then a sound. The partner can accept the whole thing, or pass on the sound only and copy just the gesture.
- Add a spoken endowment: "you're carrying something heavy." Partner takes it on or says pass. Boundaries now cover what someone is willing to be made into.
- Silent round. No word for pass — refusal is a held stillness. Everyone has to read the refusal rather than hear it.

## If it stalls

- Nobody is passing. Stop the round, name it, and make the next round pass-only for thirty seconds — every offer gets refused.
- Gestures get big and performative. Cap it: hands stay below the shoulders.
- Pairs go silent and awkward. Give them a fixed menu — wave, point, shrug, nod, stamp — and let them recombine those five.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which was harder — saying pass, or hearing it?
2. Did anyone move their gap during the exercise?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One corridor, two lines of five to seven. One shift in step 4. |
| 14–17 | One corridor, lines of seven to nine. With an odd number, make one trio — the offerer plays to two people and needs a yes from both to continue. |
| 18–20 | Two separate corridors, side by side, lines of five each. Run both at once and coach from between them. Two shifts in step 4 instead of one, thirty-five seconds each. |

## Safety & inclusion

No physical contact at any point; the gap between lines is set by the pair, not by you, and either person can widen it mid-round without asking. Anyone who would rather not be looked at directly can stand at the end of a line and keep time for the room instead. "Cut" stops everything immediately, from anyone, no explanation.

## Lineage

Originally authored for this slot. It borrows the two-line corridor shape common in movement classes and the consent check-in used widely in contemporary improv teaching, but the mechanic — a compulsory refusal, so that declining carries no social cost — was built here. Rules written from scratch.
