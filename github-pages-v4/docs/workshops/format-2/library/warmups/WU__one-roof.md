# One Roof

![One Roof — infographic](img/WU__one-roof.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs swap a small detail from the week, name what the two have in common, then merge into fours and find one word that covers all of it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention on one partner, a low talking voice, and the specific muscle the week needs: holding several unrelated concretes at once and finding the honest umbrella over them rather than a lazy one. That is what a callback is doing structurally.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Standing pairs scattered round the room, facing each other, close enough to talk quietly. No chairs, no props. Leave space for pairs to merge into fours later.

## How to run it

1. (30s) Pair everyone up. Brief it: each person names one small, concrete thing from their week. A queue, a smell, a broken kettle. Nothing confessional.
2. (60s) Partners swap details, 30 seconds each. Listener says nothing back. Just looks and takes it in.
3. (60s) Each pair agrees one sentence out loud to each other: 'Both of ours are about ___.' Concrete word, not 'life'.
4. (90s) Join each pair to another pair. Four details on the table. The four must land on a single word that honestly covers all four.
5. (60s) Go round the groups. Each calls its word to the room. Point out any two groups that landed near each other, then move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Small and concrete. Nobody needs your biggest week."*
- *"Look at them while they talk. You'll need the detail later."*
- *"Not 'life'. Not 'people'. Something you could draw."*
- *"Thin links count. Say it anyway and see if it holds."*
- *"One word from the four of you. Argue quickly."*

## Build it up

- Second round: forbid the word the group used first. They must re-cover the same four details with a different word.
- Merge the fours into eights. Eight details, one word, sixty seconds.
- Add a rule that the word must be a physical object, not a concept — the group has to map, not label.

## If it stalls

- Groups reach for enormous abstractions to make everything fit. Ban 'life', 'time' and 'people' out loud and make them go again.
- A pair genuinely finds nothing. Tell them 'both happened indoors' is a legal answer and to keep moving.
- Silence in the fours. Give them the sentence stem: 'The odd one out is ___, unless we call it all ___.'

## Ask afterwards

1. Did the word arrive from the details, or did you pick a word and shove the details under it?
2. Which group's word made you re-see your own detail?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Five or six pairs, then three groups of four (one group of six if the number is odd). Do the full share round; you have time for a second lap of words if a link is interesting. |
| 14–17 | Seven or eight pairs, then four groups. Keep the pair sentence internal to the pair only — do not share it to the room. Share round is one word per group, no commentary. |
| 18–20 | Ten pairs, then five groups of four. Cut the pair sentence to fifteen seconds and run the final share as a fast lap with no facilitator comment except naming one repeat. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Detail-sharing is voluntary: anyone who would rather not talk about their week can invent a detail or describe something in the room instead, and say so to their partner without explanation.

## Lineage

Written for this slot. The move of bridging two unrelated items with a third idea is common to association and A-to-C drills; the merge into fours and the single-word constraint are added here so the synthesis has to survive more data than a pair can supply.
