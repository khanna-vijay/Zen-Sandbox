# Stock the Shelf

![Stock the Shelf — infographic](img/WU__stock-the-shelf.jpg){ .infographic }

> In pairs, players place small true details on an imaginary shelf between them, then answer each other detail for detail until the pair has stock worth building on.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention on one partner at close range, and the ear for concrete material. It rehearses the exact action of an opening — put real things out, take what someone else put out, and leave the room holding stock the next twenty-five minutes can draw on.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Clear floor, no chairs needed. Pair everyone off, partners facing at arm's length. With an odd number, make one three and have them rotate the placing.

## How to run it

1. (30s) Pair up and face your partner. Hold both palms up between you. That gap is a shelf. You are going to stock it with real, small, specific things.
2. (60s) Round one. Alternate placing items: one concrete detail from your last week, five words or fewer. No jokes, no explaining, no reacting. Eyes on your partner.
3. (90s) Round two. Now when your partner places an item, answer with a detail it pulls out of you. A thing, not a comment. Keep the chain moving without pausing.
4. (45s) Stop. Each pair agrees on the one item on their shelf with the most heat — the one you'd happily build twenty-five minutes from.
5. (75s) Round the room, pair by pair, one item each, five words, nothing added. Do not repeat or praise any of them. Let them sit.

## Side-coach

- *"Details, not opinions."*
- *"Five words. Place it and stop."*
- *"Answer with a thing, not a thought."*
- *"Eyes on your partner, not the floor."*
- *"Boring and true beats clever and vague."*

## Build it up

- Ban this week. Every item must come from a place you haven't stood in for ten years.
- Each new item must belong to the same world as the last one. The pair builds one location, item by item.
- On a clap, swap partners without stopping and open by placing the last item you were given.

## If it stalls

- Round one often comes out flat and listy. Say so, and push on — round two is where it starts connecting.
- If a pair goes abstract, ask for something they touched, smelled or paid for.
- If a pair is playing for laughs, name it once and ask for the dull true version instead.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which of your partner's items would you rather build a whole piece from, and what made it usable?
2. What changed when you answered with a detail instead of a comment?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Five or six pairs. Run exactly as written; the room share takes about 30 seconds. |
| 14–17 | Seven or eight pairs. Trim round two to 75 seconds so the share still fits inside five minutes. |
| 18–20 | Nine or ten pairs. Split the share: two clusters of five pairs, each cluster sharing to itself at the same time, 40 seconds total. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact — palms face each other but never touch. Details can come from the room, the street or the last hour; nobody has to disclose anything personal. Anyone can pass on placing an item and just receive.

## Lineage

In the Longform opening practice of bringing in real, unpolished material, crossed with paired word-association drills. tradition. Common association warm-ups run in a circle with one voice at a time. Rebuilt as simultaneous pairs with a shared physical shelf so every player both deposits and mines, and so the room ends holding a short list of usable items rather than a memory of a good joke.
