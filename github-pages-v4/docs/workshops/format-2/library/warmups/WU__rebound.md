# Rebound

![Rebound — infographic](img/WU__rebound.jpg){ .infographic }

> In pairs, fire a sharp move-and-sound at your partner and get it thrown straight back at full size, with no gap to think in.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Wakes the shoulders, legs and breath, and raises the pulse fast. Feeds D2.S1 Active Listening by making reception physical: you cannot rebound something you did not actually watch, and the pace leaves no room to rehearse a response.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Clear the floor. Pairs standing about an arm's length apart, facing each other, with a body's width between pairs. No chairs, no props.

## How to run it

1. (30s) Pair everyone up. Demo once: throw a sharp gesture with a sound at your partner; they throw the same gesture and sound straight back at full size.
2. (60s) Round one. A fires, B rebounds it exactly, A fires a new one. Continuous. No talking, no pauses, no discussing.
3. (30s) Stop. Swap. B now fires and A rebounds.
4. (60s) Round two. Drop the leader. Whoever just rebounded owns the next throw. It should ping back and forth without anyone deciding.
5. (45s) Speed round. Halve the gap between throws. Call 'faster' twice. Accuracy over invention — they copy what actually happened.
6. (45s) Whole body. Every throw and every rebound must use legs and breath, not just hands and face.
7. (30s) Call stop. Shake the arms out, three deep breaths, then say the cue: you cannot plan and listen at the same time.

## Side-coach

- *"Rebound it before you understand it."*
- *"Same size as they gave it — no shrinking."*
- *"Legs and breath, not just hands."*
- *"If you paused, you were planning. Throw anyway."*
- *"Copy the thing that happened, not a better version of it."*

## Build it up

- Every rebound comes with one step towards your partner. When you run out of floor, reset and start again.
- Add a gibberish word to each throw. It must come back at the same pitch and volume, not just the same shape.
- Rebound at 120 per cent — slightly bigger every time — until the pair is at full stretch, then call stop.

## If it stalls

- People go small and polite. Stop for five seconds, tell them to double the size, restart mid-round.
- Someone hunts for a clever action and freezes. Tell them to throw a nonsense noise and a shrug — the drill runs on the rebound, not the invention.
- A pair falls into careful turn-taking. Stand beside them and clap a fast pulse for ten seconds to set the tempo.

## Ask afterwards

1. What happened to your throws when you were busy watching theirs?
2. Where in your body did you feel yourself receive the offer?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Straight pairs across the room. If odd, make one trio: the throw travels round the three of them in order. Run all seven steps as written. |
| 14–17 | Split the pairs into two facing lines down the room so the noise does not collide. Cut the speed round to 30 seconds and give the extra 15 seconds to the whole-body round. |
| 18–20 | Pair up along both long walls, ten pairs a side, everyone playing. Skip the swap step: instead clap once mid-round one and have leaders switch on the spot. Keeps you inside five minutes. |

## Safety & inclusion

Sharp movements, no contact. Keep a body's width between pairs and check the space behind you before throwing. Anyone who does not want to jump or drop weight into the knees keeps it to the upper body — say so out loud before you start.

## Lineage

In the Mirror and repetition warm-ups from mid-century actor training tradition. Mirror work is usually slow and continuous, and repetition work is usually verbal and seated. Made it fast, physical and standing so it functions as activation rather than focus, and dropped the leader in round two so the pair has to share control.
