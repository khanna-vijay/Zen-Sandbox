# Snap Into Someone

![Snap Into Someone — infographic](img/WU__snap-into-someone.jpg){ .infographic }

> On each clap the room throws itself into a random body shape, then lets whatever feeling that shape suggests take over for eight seconds.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Raises the pulse and loosens hips, spine and shoulders, then puts each body into a form it did not choose. Feeds D1.S2 by making the emotion a consequence of posture rather than a decision — the fluidity comes from resetting to neutral and letting a completely different person arrive fifteen seconds later.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Clear the floor. Scatter everyone so each person can stretch both arms without touching anyone. Everyone faces you; no circle, no partners, no props.

## How to run it

1. Explain and demo (45s): on your clap, throw your body into a shape you did not plan. Feet stay under you. Show one shape, hold it, then drop back to neutral.
2. Shape only (60s): clap, everyone snaps into a shape, holds three counts, then shakes out. Four rounds. No moving, no sound yet — just landing.
3. Add the person (90s): six rounds. Clap, snap into a shape, hold two counts, then let the shape start moving for eight seconds as whoever it is. Clap to reset to neutral.
4. Add breath (60s): four more rounds, same as before, but the moving body breathes out loud — sighs, huffs, grunts. No words.
5. Land it (45s): two final rounds. Move as the person for eight seconds, then on the clap freeze and drop straight to neutral, feet flat, shoulders down.

## Side-coach

- *"Shape first. The feeling catches up."*
- *"Do not decide who they are — read the shape and follow it."*
- *"Different height every clap. High, low, twisted."*
- *"Let the breath come from the body, not from your idea of it."*
- *"Neutral means neutral. Shoulders down, weight even."*

## Build it up

- Shorten the reset: clap straight from one moving person into the next shape with no neutral in between.
- Call an emotional dial number with the clap — a 3 or a 9 — and make the shape carry that intensity without changing what the body is doing.
- Final two rounds: the moving person crosses the room and passes two others, holding the feeling through eye contact, no words, no touching.

## If it stalls

- If shapes repeat, call the constraint out loud before the clap: low to the floor, or one shoulder leading, or everything asymmetrical.
- If people glance sideways and copy, have everyone face a different wall for two rounds.
- If it turns into gag poses, drop the eight-second move and run four shape-only rounds again. Comedy shapes give nothing to feel.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which shape gave you a person fastest, and what did the shape do that made it happen?
2. Did any feeling arrive that you would never have chosen on purpose?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One loose scatter. Run all sixteen rounds together as written. |
| 14–17 | One scatter, tighter spacing. Cut the move to six seconds so all sixteen rounds still fit; call spacing once before you start. |
| 18–20 | Split into two halves. Half plays, half stands at the wall and watches for eight rounds, then swap. Watchers look for shapes that read clearly from a distance. |

## Safety & inclusion

Feet stay on the floor — no jumping, no dropping to knees, no shapes that load the neck or lower back. Anyone with a joint or back issue plays the whole thing seated or keeps shapes above the waist; say so before the first clap. No contact at any point; if the room drifts together, stop and respace.

## Lineage

In the Physical-first character work from European movement training, where an outer form is taken before any inner life is named tradition. Movement schools build character from a held form over long stretches. Compressed here into eight-second bursts on a clap so it works as a five-minute activation, with the emotion arriving unnamed rather than assigned by the teacher.
