# The Fourth Breath

![The Fourth Breath — infographic](img/WU__the-fourth-breath.jpg){ .infographic }

> A word is offered, the room breathes four times in silence, and only the fourth association gets spoken.

`Players 8–24` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Drops the room's pulse and narrows attention onto one word at a time; feeds D4.S3 by putting a measured gap between the suggestion and the offer, so discarding the first association becomes a felt habit rather than an instruction.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

One circle, standing or seated — offer chairs to anyone who wants them. No props. Eyes closed is invited, not required; softening the gaze to the floor works the same.

## How to run it

1. (40s) Explain: you give a word, the room breathes four slow breaths in silence. On each out-breath, let one association go. Only the fourth gets spoken.
2. (50s) Practise with 'BREAD'. Lead four audible breaths, then take three or four voices at random. Point out how far four breaths travels.
3. (70s) Round one. Give a word. Four breaths, led by you. Then go round the circle: one word or short phrase each, quiet, no explaining.
4. (70s) Round two, new word. Same shape. Add the rule that no one repeats an association already spoken — breathe once more and offer something else.
5. (50s) Round three, new word, two breaths only. Faster round, same discipline. Notice out loud if the room slips back to first thoughts.
6. (20s) Stop. Eyes open. Say the cue: never play A, go to C. Move straight into the session.

## Side-coach

- *"Let the obvious one go on the out-breath."*
- *"Don't hunt for something clever. Wait for what arrives."*
- *"Fourth one only. Bin the first three."*
- *"Quiet voices. This is not a performance."*
- *"If you've got nothing, breathe again and come back on the way round."*

## Build it up

- Require the spoken offer to be concrete — an object, a place, a time of day. No abstract nouns.
- No repeats in a round: if someone ahead of you says yours, breathe once and find another.
- Final round — the last three speakers must build their offer from two things already said in the circle.

## If it stalls

- If people are clearly speaking their first thought, stop and have the whole room say the obvious one aloud in unison, then run the four breaths from there.
- If the silence feels heavy and people fidget, cut to two breaths and add a round rather than stretching one.
- If offers get abstract and vague — 'freedom', 'nostalgia' — insist on things you could point at: an object, a place, a time of day.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which breath did your usable idea arrive on?
2. What did you have to let go of before anything new turned up?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle, full round of voices in every round. Run all three rounds as written. |
| 14–17 | One circle. In rounds one and two, only alternate people speak — swap which half speaks each round. Everyone speaks in round three. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of about ten. You lead the breaths for both from the middle of the room; appoint one person in each circle to start the speaking round. |

## Safety & inclusion

Closing the eyes while standing makes some people sway — say sitting is fine and mean it. No contact at any point. Anyone can pass on a turn without explaining.

## Lineage

Written for this slot. The counted-breath timing is borrowed from ordinary stillness practice, used here only as a clock — it gives the discard a physical duration so players can feel how long the obvious answer takes to clear.
