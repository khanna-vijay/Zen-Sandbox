# The Same Door Twice

![The Same Door Twice — infographic](img/WU__the-same-door-twice.jpg){ .infographic }

> The circle agrees on one invisible door, then everyone has to use it at exactly the same height and weight.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Drops the room's energy into precise, quiet attention on one shared point in space. Feeds D1.S3 directly: height, weight and swing have to survive twenty different bodies, which is exactly what a location must do in a scene.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

Standing circle with a gap of about two metres in the ring — that gap is the doorway. No props. Everyone stays on their feet and stays silent while others work.

## How to run it

1. 45s. Form the circle. Point at the gap. Tell them one invisible door lives there and the whole room is responsible for keeping it the same door all the way round.
2. 45s. Take the first pass yourself. Reach, find the handle, turn it, feel the weight, step through, close it. Name aloud the handle height and which way it swings.
3. 2m30. Going clockwise, each person walks to the door, uses it and rejoins the circle. Everyone else watches in silence and tracks whether the handle stayed put.
4. 60s. Reverse direction. The door is now stiff and needs a shoulder. Last few people go through, then hold still for three seconds before you close.

## Side-coach

- *"Find the handle before you turn it."*
- *"Same height as the person before you."*
- *"Let the door have weight — don't wave at it."*
- *"Watching in silence is the work, not the wait."*

## Build it up

- Add a second feature to the doorway — a step down, or a low frame that makes tall people duck. Both must survive the lap.
- Remove the audible cues: no sighs, no grunts, no sound effects. The body alone has to prove the door.
- Two people go at once from opposite sides and must negotiate who holds it open, still in silence.

## If it stalls

- If the handle drifts, freeze the room and have everyone point at where they think it is. Take the average, restate it, carry on.
- If people rush, ask for one full second of contact with the handle before it turns.

## Ask afterwards

1. What told you the handle had moved?
2. What was harder — making your own door real, or matching someone else's?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One door, two full laps — second lap in reverse with the stiff door. |
| 14–17 | One door, one lap plus a reverse pass for the five people whose handle drifted most. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of nine or ten with their own doors, at opposite ends of the room. Run both at once; you walk between them. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Nobody reaches through the doorway while another person is in it. Anyone who doesn't want to be watched solo can pass through alongside a partner.

## Lineage

In the Space-object work from the mid-century North American improvisation training tradition. tradition. Standard space-object drills are usually solo or in pairs. This version makes one object the property of the whole circle, so consistency becomes a group obligation rather than a private skill, which suits a focus slot better than a free-for-all.
