# Then Also

![Then Also — infographic](img/WU__then-also.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs take one odd fact about an imaginary neighbour and chain out its consequences, face to face, so the room arrives already asking what else must be true.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention and partner-focus, plus the mouth's willingness to speak without editing. It feeds D3.S1 directly: players practise holding one unusual thing fixed and deducing outward from it, which is the exact move a two-person scene needs in its first minute. It also builds pair connection through sustained eye contact and turn-taking without any physical contact.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Pairs, standing, facing each other at arm's length, spread around the edges of the room so voices don't collide. No props, no chairs, no walking. If the number is odd, make one trio.

## How to run it

1. (30s) Call pairs. Tell them to face their partner and stay put — this one happens standing still, in eye contact, at conversational volume.
2. (45s) Demo with one participant. You say a small odd fact: 'My neighbour irons his money.' They reply 'Then also…' and add one consequence. You add the next. Three rungs, stop.
3. (60s) Round one. A starts with a small odd fact about an imaginary neighbour. B answers 'Then also…'. They alternate consequences until you call time. Small facts, not wacky ones.
4. (60s) Round two. Swap. B opens with a new odd fact, same chain. Tell them to keep each consequence in a different area of the neighbour's life — work, food, family, sleep.
5. (75s) Round three. Fast. New fact, one short sentence per person, no pauses to think. Every reply must start 'Then also'. Call 'next rung' if a pair goes quiet.
6. (30s) Stop everyone. Take two chains from the room, out loud, ten seconds each. Name the pattern: the first fact never changed, everything after it obeyed.

## Side-coach

- *"Keep it small — a fridge, not a spaceship."*
- *"Say it to their face, not the floor."*
- *"Don't top it. Follow it."*
- *"Next rung."*
- *"If that's true, what else is true?"*

## Build it up

- Alternate strictly every line, one sentence each, no overlaps — the pair has to share the pace.
- Each consequence must land in a different area of life: work, food, family, sleep, money. No repeats.
- Last rung of every chain starts 'And that's why…' and ties back to the opening fact.

## If it stalls

- The opening fact is usually too big. Give the pair a boring one — 'she never sits down' — and watch it chain immediately.
- If a pair keeps restarting, ban new facts for the rest of the round. They only get consequences.
- If the room goes quiet at speed, drop to alternating every other line and rebuild the pace.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which fact gave you the most to say — the strange one or the small one?
2. Did you ever want to change the fact instead of following it?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Five or six pairs, one trio if odd — in a trio the chain passes round the three of them in order. Demo with a participant. Take two chains in the closing thirty seconds. |
| 14–17 | Seven or eight pairs. Push them out to the walls before you demo, or round three turns into a wall of noise. Take two chains at the end, not more. |
| 18–20 | Nine or ten pairs, ringed round the room, each pair facing inward to each other. Shorten your demo to two rungs. Take one chain at the end and move on. |

## Safety & inclusion

Verbal only, no contact, no travel. Keep facts about an invented neighbour, not about anyone in the room or anyone's actual family. Anyone who prefers not to speak can stand with a pair and listen, or scribe two chains to read out at the end.

## Lineage

In the Chicago-lineage game-of-the-scene coaching, where players are taught to treat the first unusual detail as fixed and ask what it forces to be true next. tradition. The teaching question usually arrives mid-scene, once players are already stuck. Pulled it out of scene work and into a static two-person drill so the whole room gets thirty reps before anyone steps out. Added the 'Then also' sentence stem to stop players inventing a second unrelated fact, which is the failure mode. Honest caveat: pairs who choose an enormous opening fact often produce two rungs and dry up — the fix is a duller fact, not a cleverer partner.
