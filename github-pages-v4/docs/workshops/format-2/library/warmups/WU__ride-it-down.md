# Ride It Down

![Ride It Down — infographic](img/WU__ride-it-down.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room supplies a manufactured laugh wave and the player holds still until it hits the floor, then speaks.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Drops the room's pulse into precise, quiet attention and puts every eye on one player. It feeds D5.S2 directly: performers rehearse holding a beat while the room reacts, and speaking on the bottom of the fade instead of trampling it — the exact skill a showcase crowd exposes.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

Stand the group in a tight arc facing one clear playing spot, close enough to touch shoulders but not touching. No chairs, no props. You conduct from the side of the arc.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Explain the wave: when your hand goes up the whole arc gives a warm laugh, when it lowers the laugh fades to silence. Practise one wave together, up and down.
2. (30s) Demo. Take the spot yourself. Say one flat everyday sentence — 'I've brought the wrong keys.' Take the wave, stand still through it, speak your next sentence on the exact bottom of the silence.
3. (90s) Round one. Players take the spot one at a time, two plain sentences each with your conducted wave between. Nobody tries to be funny. Rotate fast, no comment between turns.
4. (90s) Round two. Same rotation, but the player conducts the wave with their own raised hand and must choose when to cut it and speak. You stay silent.
5. (45s) Last pass. Vary it without warning: some players get a huge wave, some get a tiny one, some get nothing at all. They hold and continue either way.

## Side-coach

- *"Hold. Let it get all the way to the floor."*
- *"Eyes on the crowd, not the ceiling."*
- *"Don't talk over it and don't top it."*
- *"Breathe out, then speak."*
- *"No laugh? Same stillness. Carry on."*

## Build it up

- The arc chooses the wave size themselves, unannounced, and the player must match their next sentence's volume to what they got.
- Two players share the spot. Both must hold through the wave and only one speaks after it — they work it out silently.
- The player must make their second sentence acknowledge the size of the reaction without commenting on the exercise.

## If it stalls

- Fake laughter goes dead fast. Swap the laugh for warm applause, or tell the arc to laugh at how ordinary the sentence is, not at the person.
- If players rush the silence, stop and run one turn where you make the pause three seconds too long. Let everyone feel it, then continue.

## Ask afterwards

1. What did you do with your body while the wave was running?
2. Which was harder to play — the huge laugh or the silence?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One arc. Everyone takes the spot once in round one and once in round two; run the final pass with four or five players only. |
| 14–17 | Split into two arcs at opposite ends of the room, roughly eight each. Appoint a conductor in the far arc. Everyone gets one turn per round; cut the final pass to three players per arc. |
| 18–20 | Two arcs of ten. Each player gets one turn total, in round two only — skip round one after your demo and give the extra minute to the varied final pass. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Say out loud that the manufactured laugh is aimed at the sentence, never at the person saying it. Anyone can stay in the arc for the whole exercise and never take the spot.

## Lineage

In the Short-form show craft tradition. Written from scratch for this slot. Performers usually learn to ride a laugh only in front of a real audience, which is a bad place to learn it. This manufactures the wave so the timing can be drilled without anyone needing to be funny.
