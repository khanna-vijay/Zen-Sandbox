# Every Tuesday, Until

![Every Tuesday, Until — infographic](img/WU__every-tuesday-until.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs build a dull shared routine line by line, then one line breaks it and the other makes it make sense.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention on one partner, conversational voice, and the ear for concrete detail. It gives the room the week's vocabulary in miniature — build a base reality, break it once, make the break make sense — before they do it at scene length.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Pairs, standing and facing each other, spread around the room so voices don't collide. No chairs, no props. Odd number: make one trio and have them cycle lines round the three.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Pair everyone up, face to face. Explain: you alternate lines starting 'Every Tuesday we…', building one shared ordinary routine. Keep it dull and concrete. Demo two lines with a volunteer.
2. (60s) Round 1. Pairs build the routine. After about 40 seconds call 'Until'. The next speaker breaks the routine in one line. Their partner answers with one line that makes it make sense.
3. (60s) Round 2. New routine, other partner starts. Call 'Until' after about 40 seconds. Same one line to break, one line to land it.
4. (60s) Round 3. Everyone finds a new partner, hands up if unpaired. Build, break, justify. Call 'Until' after 40 seconds.
5. (45s) Fast round with the same partner: only three lines of routine, then you call 'Until' immediately. Break, justify, stop.
6. (30s) Call stop. Two pairs shout their favourite break and its justification across the room. Move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Duller is better — times, objects, exact places."*
- *"Use their detail in your next line."*
- *"One line to break. One line to make it true."*
- *"Don't explain the joke — just say why it makes sense."*
- *"Look at your partner, not the floor."*

## Build it up

- The breaker stays silent after breaking; the partner must justify with no help.
- Call 'Until' after three lines only, so the platform is thin and the justification has to work harder.
- After justifying, add one more line beginning 'and ever since…' that reincorporates an earlier detail.

## If it stalls

- Pairs go strange in line one. Stop them, restate: nothing unusual happens until I say 'Until'.
- If a justification won't come, hand them the opener 'which is why we always…' and let them finish it.
- At full volume nobody hears their partner. Drop to two rounds and ask everyone to halve their volume.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which routine gave you the most to break, and why?
2. Did the justification arrive from the break, or from something you'd already set up?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Five to six pairs, three full rounds plus the fast round as written. One trio if numbers are odd. |
| 14–17 | Seven to eight pairs. Push them to the room's edges before you start; call 'Until' with a raised hand as well as your voice. |
| 18–20 | Ten pairs is loud. Cut to two build rounds plus the fast round, and split the space in half so each half hears one call. Take one example per half at the close, not four. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Keep routines mundane and invented — nobody has to share anything real about their week. Anyone who wants to observe can stand out and listen to a nearby pair.

## Lineage

In the Platform-and-break work from the Johnstone tradition, crossed with story-spine sequencing. tradition. Source: *Impro (1979)*. The tradition uses routines broken on stage inside scenes. Rewritten as a 40-second spoken pair drill with a facilitator-called break and a forced one-line justification, so a class of twenty all get reps in five minutes.
