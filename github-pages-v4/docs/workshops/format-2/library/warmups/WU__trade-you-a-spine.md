# Trade You a Spine

![Trade You a Spine — infographic](img/WU__trade-you-a-spine.jpg){ .infographic }

> Pairs hand each other one physical change at a time and let the feeling that arrives ride back across.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Warms the shoulders, spine and breath, and puts two people in sustained eye and body contact. Feeds Emotional Fluidity directly: participants practise receiving a feeling from a physical change instead of deciding one, and moving it up a notch on demand — the exact move needed when a scene asks for a shift.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Clear a floor space. Pairs stand facing each other, roughly arm's length apart, spread around the room. No props, no chairs.

## How to run it

1. 30s. Pairs face off. Label A and B. Tell them: one change to one body part only — a jaw, a shoulder, a hip, a hand. No faces pulled on purpose.
2. 60s. A makes one change and holds. B copies it exactly, then says "I'll be straight with you" in whatever feeling the copied body hands them. Swap. Repeat.
3. 90s. Keep the same shape and trade it back and forth. Each time you receive it, add one notch more of the same thing. Same line each pass.
4. 90s. New partner. Silent round. Pass a change, copy it, add a notch — feeling comes out only through breath and one sound. No words.
5. 30s. Last pass. Drop the shape, shake the arms out, face front and breathe normally twice.

## Side-coach

- *"Copy the body first. The feeling arrives second — don't pick it."*
- *"One part only. Where exactly is the change?"*
- *"Take whatever feeling turns up, even a bland one."*
- *"One notch bigger, not five."*
- *"Let the sound come out of the ribs, not the mouth."*

## Build it up

- Ban the spoken line. The feeling has to read from the body alone; the partner names what they received afterwards.
- Two changes at once, from different halves of the body, that don't obviously agree with each other.
- Add a walk. Receive the shape, take three steps in it, then hand it back one notch stronger.

## If it stalls

- People are copying approximately. Stop them and have each pair name the joint out loud before copying.
- Everyone jumps to rage or grief. Restrict the next round to changes below the waist and see what quieter people turn up.
- A pair freezes with nothing arriving. Tell them to breathe in the shape three times and take the first thing after that, however small.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which change gave you a person fastest — and which part of the body was it?
2. Did copying your partner's shape hand you the same feeling they had, or a different one?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Pairs throughout; one trio if odd, rotating who receives. Run both rounds as written. |
| 14–17 | Pairs plus one trio. Cut the silent round to 60s and use the 30s saved to swap partners twice instead of once. |
| 18–20 | Form two facing lines rather than scattered pairs — space gets tight. Everyone works simultaneously; for the second round, one line shifts three places along. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact at any point. Changes must stay inside a comfortable range — no forcing joints, no locking knees, no holding a strained position. Anyone who prefers not to distort their posture can copy their partner's breathing pattern instead and take the feeling from that. Anyone may step out and watch without explaining.

## Lineage

In the Mirror and echo work from mid-century theatre-games training tradition. Source: *Improvisation for the Theater (1963)*. Classic mirroring runs continuously and stays neutral. We broke it into discrete single-joint trades with a forced escalation each pass, and required the copier to report a feeling rather than just match movement — so the body leads the emotion, which is the week's whole argument.
