# Last of Its Kind

![Last of Its Kind — infographic](img/WU__last-of-its-kind.jpg){ .infographic }

> A single small object travels the circle in silence as the last one in existence, and whoever holds it when you clap says what losing it would cost.

`Players 8–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact incidental` · `Props: required`

## What it warms

Drops the room's pulse into concentration after the noisier warm-ups, and puts attention on one shared point. It feeds D3.S4 by making the class attach a cost to something worthless — a key, a coin — before they have to do it for a character.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

Standing circle, no gaps. You need one small unbreakable object per circle — a key, a coin, a marker pen. Room quiet, no music.

## How to run it

1. 45s: Circle up. Hold up the object. Tell them it is the last one of its kind on earth. Rules: silence, both hands, eye contact at every handover, no throwing.
2. 30s: Offer the opt-out. Anyone who does not want hand-to-hand contact holds a flat open palm to receive, or lets the object be set down in front of them and picks it up.
3. 75s: Send it round. Silent. Each holder stays still with it for two full seconds before passing. Coach the pace down until the room stops rushing.
4. 2m: Keep it moving. Clap roughly every fifteen seconds. Whoever holds it says one quiet sentence: "If I lose this, I lose ___." Then the object continues.
5. 30s: Take the object back. One line from you naming the cue — that is the question under every scene today. No applause, straight on.

## Side-coach

- *"Slower. Two seconds in your hands before it leaves."*
- *"Eyes up at the handover. See who you're giving it to."*
- *"Quieter than you think. Let the room stay still."*
- *"Don't be clever. Say the true thing and pass it on."*
- *"Track it. Know where it is without turning your head."*

## Build it up

- Add a second object going the other way. Both holders speak on the clap.
- Drop the clap. The holder chooses their own moment to speak, and the circle waits for it.
- Change the sentence: the line must name a person, not a thing. "If I lose this, I lose my sister."

## If it stalls

- If the handovers speed up and go giggly, stop the object dead and restart from you at half the speed. Name the two-second hold again.
- If the spoken lines all come out as jokes, take one yourself: "If I lose this, I lose the flat." Then ask for the boring true answer, not the funny one.
- If someone freezes on the clap, let them pass it on in silence. Do not wait them out.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which line in the circle made you actually care about a key?
2. What changed in your body once the object mattered?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle, one object, roughly eight claps in the two minutes. |
| 14–17 | One circle, one object for the first lap, then add a second object travelling the opposite way. Clap freezes both; two people speak, nearest first. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten, one object each, in opposite corners. Your clap serves both — both holders speak, one circle at a time, so everyone hears. |

## Safety & inclusion

Physical contact is a hand-to-hand pass only, and opting out is offered before the object moves. Use nothing sharp, breakable or valuable. No throwing, no reaching across the circle.
