# Twenty True Things

![Twenty True Things — infographic](img/WU__twenty-true-things.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room builds a quiet inventory of dull, literal facts, then holds its attention on the single one that isn't ordinary.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention, narrowed from the whole room to one screw-head. Drops the pulse after the physical warm-ups and trains the exact move the session needs: assemble the ordinary first, then hold the exception long enough to learn what it is, rather than escalating off it immediately. Feeds D3.S2 by making exploration the slow part.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

Standing circle, no walking, no chairs moved. The room itself is the material — walls, floor, fittings, sounds, whatever people are holding. Nobody speaks above a low, flat voice.

## How to run it

1. (0:30) Circle up. Tell them: name only true, visible, boring things about this room. No jokes, no metaphors, no comments on anyone's body. Thirty seconds of silent scanning.
2. (1:15) Lap one. Round the circle, each person says one true detail in a flat, low voice. Keep it moving, roughly three seconds each. Pass is allowed.
3. (1:15) Lap two, same order. Each person names a smaller, more specific detail than they gave first time — a mark, a screw, a hum, a scuff.
4. (1:15) Pick one detail from the laps and say it aloud. Everyone looks at it in silence for twenty seconds. Then take three quiet sentences beginning: "The odd thing about it is..."
5. (0:45) Everyone holds their eyes on that one thing. Silence, three slow breaths. Say the cue: establish normal, then find the one thing that isn't. Move straight on.

## Side-coach

- *"Dull is correct. Don't improve it."*
- *"Smaller. Go smaller than that."*
- *"Flat voice. You're not selling it."*
- *"Keep looking at it. Don't decide what it means yet."*
- *"One thing only. Stay with it."*

## Build it up

- Add a third lap where every detail must be within two metres of where the speaker stands.
- Run lap two with eyes closed — sound, smell and temperature only, still literal.
- After the exception is chosen, take one more round of single clauses: "...which means someone must have...". One clause each, no story.

## If it stalls

- If details turn into jokes or opinions, stop the lap and reset with an example: "Grey skirting board, chipped at the left end." Restart from the same person.
- If the room is bare, widen the field to sounds, temperature, light and objects people are holding — still literal, still visible or audible.
- If nobody offers an "odd thing" sentence, offer the first one yourself, deliberately mundane, and wait. Silence for eight seconds usually produces two more.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which detail only became interesting after the room had listed twenty boring ones?
2. What happened to your attention when you had to stay on one thing for twenty seconds instead of moving on?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Both laps in full, three exception sentences. Comfortably fits the five minutes. |
| 14–17 | One circle. Lap one in full; for lap two, take the first ten people only and cut it there — say so before starting so nobody feels skipped. |
| 18–20 | Split into two lines of nine or ten facing each other. Both lines run laps one and two at the same time under your timing calls. Reconvene as one group for the exception; you choose the detail. |

## Safety & inclusion

Standing still can leave people light-headed — say sitting is fine at any point. Rule out details about other people's bodies, clothing or possessions; the room only. No contact.

## Lineage

In the Sensory observation drills from mid-century theatre-games training tradition. Classic 'seeing the room' observation work usually ends at heightened awareness. Restructured as two narrowing laps followed by a held exception, so the group practises building a base of ordinary detail before staying with the one thing that breaks it.
