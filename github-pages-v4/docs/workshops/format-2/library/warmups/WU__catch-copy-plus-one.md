# Catch, Copy, Plus One

![Catch, Copy, Plus One — infographic](img/WU__catch-copy-plus-one.jpg){ .infographic }

> A shape and sound flies across the circle; the catcher copies it exactly, adds one thing, and throws it on.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Raises the pulse and puts the whole body and voice into play within a minute, while drilling the exact move the session needs: take what you are handed as true, reproduce it faithfully, then add one thing. It also warms peripheral awareness, since everyone must track where the shape is in the circle.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Standing circle, arm's length apart, bags and chairs pushed well back. No props. You stand in the circle too and play the first shape yourself.

## How to run it

1. 45s. Circle up. Explain: a shape plus a sound gets thrown across the circle. The catcher copies it before doing anything else. Demo one throw with a volunteer.
2. 60s. Copy round only, no additions. Throw a big shape and sound to someone opposite. They copy it whole and throw the same one on. Keep it moving fast.
3. 90s. Add the rule: copy exactly, then add exactly one thing — a stamp, a lean, a second sound. After four additions call 'fresh' and start a new shape.
4. 75s. Two shapes travel at once. Both catchers copy and add on their own beat. Call 'fresh' every twenty seconds so nothing gets too long to carry.
5. 30s. Freeze the last shape. Whole circle performs it together three times, bigger each time, then drop the arms and breathe out.

## Side-coach

- *"Copy first. Hands, feet, face, sound."*
- *"One addition. Only one."*
- *"Throw to eyes, not to the floor."*
- *"Bigger than feels sensible."*
- *"Catch it before you judge it."*

## Build it up

- Silent version: physical shape only, no sound. Copies have to be far more precise.
- The catcher names an emotion as they copy, and their one addition must serve that emotion.
- Two shapes travelling in opposite directions, and anyone who breaks the copy simply calls 'fresh' and starts a new one — no pause, no apology.

## If it stalls

- If people add three things at once, freeze them mid-shape and have the circle name each addition out loud. Then restart with one.
- If copies are vague and the chain dissolves, slow the throws right down for thirty seconds and insist on exact feet and exact volume.
- If nobody makes eye contact before throwing, add the receiver's name to every throw.

## Ask afterwards

1. What was harder — copying what you were given, or resisting the urge to change it?
2. Did anything get better because someone copied you accurately?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle, one shape travelling. Run four or five 'fresh' chains in total. |
| 14–17 | One circle. Start the two-shape version at step 3 instead of step 4 so nobody waits long between catches. |
| 18–20 | Split into two circles of nine or ten, three metres apart. Each runs its own chain. Stand between them and call 'fresh' for both at once. |

## Safety & inclusion

Shapes stay standing — no jumps, floor drops or spinning. Anyone with a mobility or voice limit can play seated or with arms and face only, and that counts as a full copy. Nobody touches anyone.

## Lineage

In the Circle pass-the-energy warm-ups and mirroring drills common in improv and physical theatre training tradition. The familiar pass-a-shape circle usually rewards invention. We flipped the emphasis: exact copying is the graded part, and invention is capped at one addition, so the drill trains receiving rather than topping. The four-addition reset is ours, added because uncapped chains become memory tests and the pulse drops.
