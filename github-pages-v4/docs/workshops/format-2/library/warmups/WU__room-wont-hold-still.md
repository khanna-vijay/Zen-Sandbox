# The Room Won't Hold Still

![The Room Won't Hold Still — infographic](img/WU__room-wont-hold-still.jpg){ .infographic }

> Call changes to the space and the whole room adjusts its body on the spot — low ceilings, deep mud, narrow ledges — so the floor gets warm and space work starts before anyone speaks.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact incidental` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Legs, spine, breath and the floor of the room. It puts weight, resistance and distance into the body before anyone opens their mouth, which is exactly what D1.S3 needs — and the freeze trains peripheral awareness of how other people are building the same place.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Clear the floor and push bags to the walls. Scatter everyone across the space, arm's length apart, facing no particular direction. You stand at the edge where all of them can hear you.

## How to run it

1. (30s) Explain: you call out what the space is doing; they keep moving through it and let their body answer. No sound effects, no commentary, no talking. Bodies only.
2. (90s) Call changes every 15 seconds and keep them moving: ceiling at shoulder height, floor is thick mud, floor is ice, sideways wind, a corridor two feet wide, ceiling back to normal.
3. (60s) Call 'freeze'. Everyone holds. Point out two or three bodies, name what their shape tells you about the space. Restart with the same call and let them match each other's version.
4. (90s) Harder calls: a door they must open and close behind them, stairs going down, a low beam every four steps, something heavy in both hands the whole time.
5. (30s) Call 'freeze' one last time. Have them set down whatever they were carrying, shake out the hands, and stand still for three breaths before the next activity.

## Side-coach

- *"Ceiling is at your shoulders — keep walking anyway."*
- *"Weight in your feet, not in your face."*
- *"Show me where you are. Don't tell me."*
- *"Slower through the mud. Mud is not fast."*
- *"Same room, everyone. Match the person nearest you."*

## Build it up

- Stack two conditions at once: low ceiling and deep mud, then narrow corridor and something heavy in both hands.
- Stop calling. Point at one participant; whatever they do to the space, everyone else joins within three seconds.
- Add a temperature to each call — freezing corridor, boiling stairwell — and make them keep the location intact while the temperature changes them.

## If it stalls

- If people go small and polite, shrink the space instead of asking for more energy: narrow corridor, then lower ceiling, then both. Constraint gets bigger bodies than encouragement does.
- If they mime with their faces and hands only, freeze the room and ask everyone to redo the last call using knees and spine, nothing above the neck.
- Expect a few clichés — the classic glass-wall press. Name it lightly, call a change, move on.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which call made your whole body work, and which one did you fake from the shoulders up?
2. When you looked around at the freeze, did we all seem to be in the same room?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One group, whole floor, all four minutes of calls. Add two extra calls at the end — waist-deep water, then a ledge with a long drop. |
| 14–17 | Split into two halves. Each half plays 45 seconds while the other watches from the wall, then swap, twice through. Watchers say one thing they believed. |
| 18–20 | Two halves as above, but three swaps of 30 seconds each so nobody is stuck queuing. Keep calls to one per 15 seconds and drop the door and stairs. |

## Safety & inclusion

Moving bodies with changing eyelines — remind them eyes up, no running, and steer round each other rather than through. Brief contact when the corridor narrows is possible; anyone who does not want to be crowded can play at the edge of the space, and say so before you start. Knees and backs: mud and low ceilings are an invitation to bend, not to strain — take any call at whatever depth is comfortable.

## Lineage

In the Physical theatre and mime-based improv training, where environment calls are used to load the body before scene work. tradition. The common version is a leader-led environment walk. We fixed it to five minutes, added the mid-point freeze so the room can see whether it agrees on the ceiling height, and banned sound effects and speech so the evidence has to be physical.
