# Barometer

![Barometer — infographic](img/WU__barometer.jpg){ .infographic }

> In pairs, one person silently holds a room-state while the other names it out loud and chooses a response that isn't obedience.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 3/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention and partner focus, with the pulse held low and steady. It rehearses the session skill directly: take an accurate reading of a room's state, say it out loud, and then make a choice that answers it rather than copies it.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Pairs standing face to face, about an arm's length apart, spread around the room. No props, no chairs. With an odd number, make one trio: two readers, one barometer.

## How to run it

1. 40s: Pair up. Explain: A silently holds a house-state with breath, posture and face only — warm, restless, wary, flat, buzzing. No mime, no words.
2. 70s: B watches five seconds, then says aloud: 'You're restless — so I'm going to slow right down.' Name the state, then a choice that isn't matching it. Repeat three or four times.
3. 70s: Swap. B holds the state, A reads and chooses. Same two-part sentence every time: what's true, then what you'll do about it.
4. 80s: Round three. The barometer shifts state mid-round without warning. The reader must catch it and update aloud mid-sentence, then choose again.
5. 40s: Stop. Ask two people what their partner did that they did not expect, then say the cue and move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Name the state, then your choice. Two halves, every time."*
- *"Don't copy them. Reading is not obeying."*
- *"Read the breath before the face."*
- *"Barometers — hold it. Let them work for it."*
- *"If it changed, say so out loud and pick again."*

## Build it up

- Barometers stop holding one state and drift slowly between two. Readers must name the direction of travel, not the state.
- Readers drop the naming out loud and only say the choice. Barometers afterwards say whether they were read correctly.
- Rotate partners every 30 seconds so nobody gets comfortable with one person's tells.

## If it stalls

- Readers who narrate instead of naming: give them five words to pick from — warm, restless, wary, flat, buzzing.
- Barometers who overact into mime: tell them to change only breath and spine, nothing else.
- Pairs matching each other's energy without noticing: freeze the room and make every reader choose the opposite of what they just did.

## Ask afterwards

1. What did you read off your partner before they gave you anything obvious?
2. When did you feel the pull to just mirror them, and what did you do instead?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Five or six pairs. Run all three rounds as written, then in the last 40s let two pairs show one exchange to the room. |
| 14–17 | Seven or eight pairs. Skip the demo — use the final 40s for two spoken answers and the cue. |
| 18–20 | Nine or ten pairs, plus one trio if odd. Shorten round three to 60s and add that 20s to round one. No demo. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Sustained eye contact at close range gets intense for some people — offer looking at the collarbone instead, and let anyone widen the gap between them and their partner without explaining why.

## Lineage

Originally authored. It borrows the shape of standard energy-calibration and mirroring pair work, then breaks it deliberately: the reader is forbidden from matching, which is the whole point for this week.
