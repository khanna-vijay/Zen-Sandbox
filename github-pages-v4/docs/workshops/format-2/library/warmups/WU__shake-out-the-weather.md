# Shake Out the Weather

![Shake Out the Weather — infographic](img/WU__shake-out-the-weather.jpg){ .infographic }

> A counted eight-shake of the limbs that a called emotion floods through, so the feeling lands in the breath and body before anyone speaks.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Wakes the limbs and lifts the pulse fast, opens the breath under load, and pulls the whole group onto one count. It feeds Emotional Fluidity by making people switch feeling on an external call while a fixed rhythm keeps running, so they learn the switch happens in breath and body weight, not in a facial expression or a clever line.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Spread the room out so everyone has an arm span clear on all sides, all facing you. No props. Knees soft, shoes on, bags off the floor.

## How to run it

1. 0:30 — Set the shape. Shake right hand eight counts, left hand eight, right foot eight, left foot eight. Then four each, two each, one each. Count out loud with them.
2. 1:00 — Run it once neutral, no emotion. You count, they count back. End on a still breath. Fix spacing and soft knees now.
3. 0:40 — Call an emotion: DELIGHT. Everyone takes one loud breath in that emotion first, then runs the full countdown with it in the arms, feet and voice.
4. 0:40 — Call GRIEF. Breath first, then the countdown. The shake gets heavier and slower without losing the count.
5. 0:40 — Call FURY. Breath first, then the countdown. Sharp, fast, still on the beat.
6. 0:40 — Call one more emotion from the room. Breath first, full countdown, everyone counting aloud in that emotion.
7. 0:50 — Final round: no words, only breath and the shake. Land on stillness. Ten seconds of quiet standing, then move on.

## Side-coach

- *"Breath first. Let it change your breath before it changes your words."*
- *"Same count, different weather."*
- *"Let it into the feet, not just the face."*
- *"Louder counting — the number carries the feeling."*
- *"Stop. Stand. Keep breathing it."*

## Build it up

- Call the next emotion mid-countdown, on count four, so they change without stopping the rhythm.
- Cut your counting entirely — the room holds the count aloud and you only call emotions.
- Call two at once: fury in the arms, delight in the feet.

## If it stalls

- If people are only making faces, cover your own face with a hand and say the feeling has to be visible from the knees down.
- If the counting drifts, drop the emotion for one round, rebuild the rhythm, then add it back.
- If a called emotion gets a flat response, swap it for a bodily one — panic, glee, disgust — which lands faster than abstract ones.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which emotion arrived in your breath fastest, and which one you had to fake first?
2. What changed in the count itself when the feeling changed?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One block facing you. Run four emotion rounds instead of three, and take two calls from the room. |
| 14–17 | One block facing you, spacing checked before you start. Three emotion rounds plus one call from the room, as written. |
| 18–20 | Split into two facing blocks about three metres apart. Blocks alternate rounds — one shakes, one watches and breathes along — then both do the final silent round together. |

## Safety & inclusion

Shaking only, never whipping. Keep knees soft, keep the head still, and stay within your own arm span. Anyone with wrist, shoulder, knee or back trouble shakes small or just breathes the emotion standing — say this out loud before round one. No contact at any point.

## Lineage

In the Movement and dance-class shake-outs on a counted 8-4-2-1 pattern, widely used as a physical warm-up. tradition. The counted shake-out is common studio property. We layered a called emotion on top and put a single audible breath before each round, so the feeling enters through the body and lungs rather than the face — which is what this week's material needs.
