# Clear the Frame

![Clear the Frame — infographic](img/WU__clear-the-frame.jpg){ .infographic }

> Two people freeze in a silent picture and the circle watches until exactly one person steps in to end it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention and stillness. It drops the pulse after the activation games and puts the whole room on one shared object. It feeds Support Work by making the edit a service to the two people in the frame — you end their picture at its best, and you don't grab a call someone else has already started.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

Standing circle, no chairs, a clear space of two or three metres in the middle. No props. Ask for quiet before you start and keep it quiet throughout.

## How to run it

1. Brief it (45 seconds). Two people step in and freeze in a picture together. No words, no touching. The circle watches in silence and reads when the picture is finished.
2. Give the rule (30 seconds). Anyone may end it: step forward, raise one hand, say 'clear'. Only one person may do that. Two callers means both step back and the picture holds.
3. Run it (2 minutes 30). The pair clears. The caller stays in and freezes a new picture with one fresh volunteer. Aim for eight to ten pictures.
4. Tighten it (45 seconds). Tell them a picture may last no longer than a slow count of eight. Run three more pictures under that limit.
5. Close (30 seconds). Stop on a clean one. Say nothing about who was funny. Move straight into the next thing.

## Side-coach

- *"Watch the picture, not your friend."*
- *"If someone else is already moving, let them have it."*
- *"Call it a beat before you're certain."*
- *"Clear means clear — step all the way back."*
- *"Two callers, no blame. Reset and breathe."*

## Build it up

- Allow the frozen pair one very slow movement each, so the picture keeps changing and the right moment moves.
- The caller must replace the pair alone — step in and hold a solo picture until the next person joins.
- Remove the word. The call is a silent step forward with a raised hand only.

## If it stalls

- Nobody calls. Count eight out loud yourself and clear it for them once, then hand the job back.
- Everyone calls at once, repeatedly. Drop the word — the call becomes a silent step forward with a raised hand.
- Pictures get elaborate and jokey. Say 'one shape, hold it', and take the next two yourself to model plainness.

## Ask afterwards

1. What told you the picture was done?
2. When you pulled back to let someone else call it, what did that cost you?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Run ten pictures and everyone gets in at least once — track who hasn't and give them the next volunteer slot. |
| 14–17 | One circle, eight pictures. Say out loud that anyone who has already called sits out calling for one round, so the quiet ones get a turn. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten, running independently in opposite corners. You stand between them and watch. No swapping between circles. |

## Safety & inclusion

Pictures are made without contact — near is fine, touching is not. Anyone can decline to step in; staying in the circle and watching is a full contribution, and calling 'clear' counts as taking part.

## Lineage

In the Ensemble group-mind circle games tradition. Borrows the shared convention from group-counting exercises that only one person may act at a time, and two at once means reset. Applied here to the edit instead of a number, so the drill trains when to end something rather than how to speak in turn.
