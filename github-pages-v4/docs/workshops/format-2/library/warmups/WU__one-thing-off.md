# One Thing Off

![One Thing Off — infographic](img/WU__one-thing-off.jpg){ .infographic }

> Small groups freeze an ordinary picture with one thing wrong in it, and the watching room names the wrong thing out loud in ten seconds.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention and stillness after a loud cluster: eyes scanning a whole stage picture, bodies holding a shape without fidgeting. It feeds D3.S1 by drilling the first move of the session \u2014 spot the one thing that isn't normal, say it in plain words, then take one step of consequence off it.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

Clear a playing end of the room. Groups of four or five stand at that end; everyone else watches from a loose line or seated on chairs. No props, no contact, nobody on the floor.

## How to run it

1. (40s) Split into groups of four or five. Give each group a dull location out loud: bus queue, lift, waiting room, photocopier. Send one group to the playing end.
2. (30s) Tell every group: agree your picture, all stand still in it. The person nearest the door does one small thing wrong. Everyone else plays it completely straight.
3. (45s per group) Group freezes. Count ten seconds of silence while the room looks. Snap your fingers. Watchers say the odd thing at once, in a short phrase.
4. (inside the same 45s) Ask the odd player: if this is true, what else is true? They answer in one sentence, still frozen. Then release them and call the next group.
5. (3 min total) Run every group this way. Keep the silence honest — no commentary while the picture is up.
6. (50s) Last group only: two people are wrong. Watchers name both, then vote which one is more interesting to watch. Hand straight over to the next piece of work.

## Side-coach

- *"Smaller. One thing, not a costume."*
- *"Straight faces in the background. You have not noticed anything."*
- *"Look at the whole picture, not the funny one."*
- *"Name it in five words."*
- *"Stay frozen while you answer."*

## Build it up

- Two odd people per picture; watchers name both and pick the stronger one.
- Ban the obvious: the oddity must be a direction, an eyeline or a feeling, not an object.
- After the naming, unfreeze the picture for five seconds of movement and see whether the oddity survives motion.

## If it stalls

- Oddities go cartoonish — someone plays a dinosaur and the round dies. Stop, say 'wrong direction, wrong object, wrong feeling', and re-run that group.
- Watchers cannot spot it: ask the group to hold the freeze and take five more seconds rather than giving the answer away.
- A group cannot agree a picture in thirty seconds: assign the positions yourself and move on.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which odd thing was easiest to name, and why?
2. Did the straight players make the odd one easier or harder to see?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Three groups of four. Run all three, then re-run group one with a fresh oddity as the two-wrong-people round. |
| 14–17 | Four groups of four. One round each, no repeats. Make the fourth group the two-wrong-people round. |
| 18–20 | Five groups of four. Cut the silent look to seven seconds and drop the 'what else is true' sentence on rounds three and four so you still finish in five minutes. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Everyone chooses their own standing position; no kneeling, crouching or lifting. Anyone who does not want to be the odd one passes it to the next person in their group without explaining, and can stand as a background figure instead.

## Lineage

Written for this cluster. Frozen-picture work is common across devising practice; the specific beat here \u2014 the room naming the unusual element out loud, then one consequence spoken from inside the freeze \u2014 was built to point at game identification rather than image-making.
