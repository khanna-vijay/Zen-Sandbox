# Soft Eyes, Loud Feet

![Soft Eyes, Loud Feet — infographic](img/WU__soft-eyes-loud-feet.jpg){ .infographic }

> A bouncing circle passes impulses it can only catch out of the corner of the eye, so the room wakes up wide instead of narrow.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Legs, breath and pulse through continuous bouncing; voice gets a short open call on each impulse. Attention goes wide rather than pointed, which is exactly the muscle long-form asks for — holding the whole stage picture while your head stays where the scene needs it. Feeds D4.S1 directly and gives the room a shared tempo before scene work.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

One circle, arm's length apart, everyone facing in. Clear the centre. Nobody moves their feet out of place for the whole thing — the energy goes up, not around.

## How to run it

1. (40s) Everyone bounces gently on the spot, heels light, and fixes a soft gaze on a point above the heads opposite. Heads stay still. Keep bouncing until you call stop.
2. (50s) Teach the impulse: arms shoot up, then drop with a 'HUP'. Send one round the circle. Receivers copy the moment they catch it sideways, never by turning to look.
3. (60s) Now the impulse can jump anywhere — across the circle, back the way it came. Keep it moving. One impulse live at a time. Heads still, feet still bouncing.
4. (60s) Add a second impulse from the opposite side. Two live at once. If they land on the same person, that person sends both out again in different directions.
5. (50s) Drop your calling. Anyone may start a new impulse whenever the room feels thin. Aim for three live at once without anyone turning their head.
6. (40s) Call stop. Feet still, gaze still soft. Three slow breaths, and ask them to notice how much of the circle they can still feel.

## Side-coach

- *"Blink. Soft eyes, not a stare."*
- *"Keep the feet going — don't watch, bounce."*
- *"Don't turn your head. Catch it wide."*
- *"Late is fine. Stopping is not."*
- *"Feel the far side of the circle."*

## Build it up

- Silence the impulse — arms only, no 'HUP'. Now the catch is purely visual and purely peripheral.
- Add a reversal: any receiver may send it straight back to the person who sent it, without looking at them.
- When two impulses land on the same person, the whole circle freezes for one beat, then resumes together.

## If it stalls

- Heads keep swivelling: give each person a fixed spot on the far wall to hold and re-run one impulse only.
- Impulses die on arrival: drop back to one, and name the sender out loud each time until it travels clean.
- Energy sags: speed the bounce for fifteen seconds, then restart the impulse from you.

## Ask afterwards

1. What did you notice when you stopped hunting for the impulse and let it arrive?
2. How far round the circle could you feel without looking?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle, tight. Two live impulses in step 4 is plenty; skip the third in step 5. |
| 14–17 | One circle, wider. Run three live impulses in step 5 and expect a beat of delay across the diameter — that's fine. |
| 18–20 | One large circle, not two. Allow four live impulses in step 5 and cut step 3 to 45 seconds so the busier steps get the time. |

## Safety & inclusion

Bouncing is optional: offer heel lifts, a hand tap on the thigh, or standing still with the arm movement only. Say so before you start, and don't single out who takes it. Soft focus means relaxed eyes and normal blinking, not a fixed stare — if anyone's eyes feel strained, look down and rejoin. No contact.

## Lineage

In the Ensemble impulse-passing circles, plus soft-focus peripheral drills common in actor movement and stage-combat training. tradition. Standard pass-the-clap circles let people turn and stare at the sender, which trains narrow attention. We pinned the head still, added a continuous bounce so it works as activation rather than focus, and stacked multiple live impulses so nobody can track them one at a time.
