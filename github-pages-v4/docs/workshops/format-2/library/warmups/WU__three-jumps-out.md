# Three Jumps Out

![Three Jumps Out — infographic](img/WU__three-jumps-out.jpg){ .infographic }

> The room jumps a word three associations away from itself and freezes in the shape of whatever lands third.

`Players 8–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Legs, breath and volume, plus the group pulse of a shared count. It feeds Suggestion Deconstruction by making the distance between word one and word three physically visible — and by exposing how often the whole room's first association is identical.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Standing circle, arm's length apart, floor clear of bags and shoes. No props. Everyone needs room to jump and then throw a shape without hitting a neighbour.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Circle up. Explain: you call a word, everyone jumps three times on your count, shouting their own next association on each landing. Third landing, freeze in that word's shape.
2. (30s) Demo once at half speed with 'beach'. Land one, shout. Land two, shout what one made you think of. Land three, shout and freeze. Point out the spread of shapes.
3. (2m) Run six rounds at pace. Call a fresh word, count three, no gap between rounds. Words: hospital, wedding, snow, bank, dog, birthday.
4. (1m) Add the rule: word three must not belong in the same shop, building or afternoon as word one. Three rounds: airport, phone, gym.
5. (45s) Final two rounds silent on jumps one and two, shouted only on three. Listen to how many people land on the same word. Sit down.

## Side-coach

- *"Land it and say it — don't decide it."*
- *"Shape first, tidiness never."*
- *"If three is still obviously about one, you stopped too early."*
- *"Louder on the landing than on the jump."*
- *"Same word as your neighbour? Notice it. Don't fix it."*

## Build it up

- Extend to five jumps and five words; freeze on five. The obvious ones burn off by jump three.
- Ban nouns on jump three — it must be an action, a job or a moment in time.
- Call two words at once. Chains must start from both and meet at a single shape on jump three.

## If it stalls

- People are silently editing before jump one. Speed up your count so there's no room to choose; a bad third word is worth more than a clever pause.
- Everyone freezes into the same shape. Say the overlap out loud, then rerun the same word and ban the shape you just saw.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which jump was the one where your word stopped being predictable?
2. How often did the room land on the same word three, and what does that tell you about word one?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Six rounds in step 3 as written. |
| 14–17 | One wider circle; step back so everyone has jump room. Cut step 3 to five rounds so the shouting stays crisp. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten, each with a clear jumping radius. You call the word once for both and count for both. Run one extra round in step 3 so quieter voices get heard. |

## Safety & inclusion

Jumping is optional. Offer heel-raises, a knee bend, or a punch of the arm on each count for anyone with knees, backs, joints or a recent injury — say this before the demo, not after. Keep a full arm's length between people so the freeze shapes don't collide. No contact at any point.

## Lineage

In the Word-association chain drills common in improv training tradition. Association chains are usually seated and verbal. We put them on the feet and added a frozen physical shape on the third beat so the exercise raises the pulse and makes the third association readable across the room rather than private.
