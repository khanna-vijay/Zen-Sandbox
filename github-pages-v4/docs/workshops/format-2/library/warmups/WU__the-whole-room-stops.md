# The Whole Room Stops

![The Whole Room Stops — infographic](img/WU__the-whole-room-stops.jpg){ .infographic }

> A fast shake-out in a circle where any one person can freeze the entire room with one word.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Legs, shoulders, breath and pulse, plus the group's shared attention on a single voice. It feeds Boundary Navigation by making the Cut call a physical habit at high energy, before anyone has to use it in a scene.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

One circle, everyone at least an arm's length from their neighbours, facing in. Clear the floor of bags and chairs. No props, no partners, nobody leaves their spot.

## How to run it

1. (30s) Form the circle. Say the rule: you call out moves, everyone does them on the spot, and anyone may shout 'Cut' at any moment to freeze the whole room.
2. (45s) Start easy. March on the spot, then add arm swings, then shoulder rolls. Call each change clearly. Tell them a smaller version of any move is always correct.
3. (30s) Rehearse the stop. Tell the room you want two Cuts in the next round from anyone who fancies it. Practise the freeze once now: call 'Cut', everyone stills, then resume.
4. (90s) Escalate: knee lifts, reaching high, taps to the floor, quarter-turns. When someone calls 'Cut', everyone freezes. The caller says 'swap that one' or 'carry on'. Restart within three seconds.
5. (60s) Final burst. Twenty seconds of the room's favourite move at whatever size each person chooses, then call 'Cut' yourself and hold the freeze for five full seconds.
6. (45s) Shake hands and feet out. Two slow breaths. Say the cue plainly: nothing in this room happens without a yes, and 'Cut' always works.

## Side-coach

- *"Smaller version is the correct version."*
- *"Freeze means everyone, instantly."*
- *"Say 'Cut' loud enough for the far side."*
- *"Breathe out while you move."*
- *"Back in on three, two, one."*

## Build it up

- Let the person who called Cut choose the next move for the room.
- Run it silent: you signal moves by demonstrating, so people watch the circle instead of listening for you.
- After a Cut, restart at double speed for ten seconds, then settle back.

## If it stalls

- Nobody calls the first Cut unprompted. Expect that. Ask one person before you start to call it, so the room hears it work.
- If the moves stay tiny, drop your own energy rather than pushing theirs, and rebuild from marching.
- If a freeze collapses into chatter, restart the count immediately rather than commenting on it.

## Ask afterwards

1. How did it feel to stop twenty people with one word?
2. Did anyone want to call Cut and not call it? What stopped you?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Ask for two Cuts in step 4. |
| 14–17 | One circle, widened to two arm-spans between people. Ask for three Cuts in step 4 so more voices get used. |
| 18–20 | Split into two arcs facing each other across the room, ten each. Same calls, same time. Ask each arc for two Cuts; a Cut from either arc freezes both. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Everything is on the spot, so keep an arm's length clear. Offer a low-impact version of every move: marching instead of jumping, hands to knees instead of the floor. Anyone with a knee, back or hip issue drops to the smallest version without explaining themselves, and stepping out to the edge is a normal choice, not a failure.

## Lineage

In the Common studio shake-out and freeze-game practice tradition. Standard shake-outs put the stop button in the facilitator's hands. Here it is handed to every participant on the first morning, and the first Cuts are pre-arranged so nobody has to be brave to test it.
