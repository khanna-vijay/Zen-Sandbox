# Land and Leave

![Land and Leave — infographic](img/WU__land-and-leave.jpg){ .infographic }

> One at a time, people step into an empty space, hold one readable shape for a breath, and leave without decorating it.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Narrows attention after a loud cluster and settles breath. Trains the single readable choice and a clean entrance and exit — the two things that carry across a 40-minute public set — and puts the eyeline on the back row before anyone plays.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

Stand the group in a wide horseshoe with the open side facing the far wall; that wall is the back row. Leave an empty patch of floor about two metres square in the mouth of the horseshoe. No props.

## How to run it

1. 45s. Set the horseshoe. Explain: step in, stop, breathe once, make one clear physical shape, hold it for that breath, walk out. No words, no exit gag.
2. 30s. Demo it yourself once. Then demo it badly — fidget in, three ideas, a shrug on the way out — so the difference is visible.
3. 2min. Round one. Everyone goes once, in horseshoe order, no gap between people. Silence from the watchers throughout.
4. 1min. Round two. Same shape rules, but each player fixes their eyeline on the far wall before shaping. After each, point at one watcher for a one-word read.
5. 45s. Last pass. Two people enter together. The second does less and stands where they make the first easier to read. Four or five pairs, then stop.

## Side-coach

- *"Stop first. Shape second."*
- *"One thing, not three."*
- *"Eyes to the far wall before you move."*
- *"Hold it for the whole breath."*
- *"Walk out clean. No apology."*

## Build it up

- Add an emotional state to the shape, still held for one breath only, still with no words.
- Watchers call the one-word read in unison; if the room splits on the word, the choice was not clear.
- The player enters already inside a shape and must land it before their second footstep.

## If it stalls

- If people start adding jokes on the exit, name it once and reset: the exit is not a beat. Comedy on the way out is the most common failure here.
- If shapes come out vague and small, shrink the round — five volunteers only, watched properly, rather than everyone at speed.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which shapes read from where you were standing, and what made them read?
2. What did you add that nobody needed?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One horseshoe. Everyone goes twice in round one, once in round two, and run six pairs at the end. |
| 14–17 | One horseshoe. Everyone once in round one; in round two take the first twelve only, then move on. |
| 18–20 | Split into two horseshoes at opposite ends of the room, each facing its own wall. They run simultaneously and self-pace; you watch one, then the other. Round two: eight players per horseshoe. |

## Safety & inclusion

Shapes are held standing or crouching only — no drops to the floor, no jumps, no weight on knees. Nobody touches anyone. Anyone can pass by stepping in, standing neutral for one breath, and stepping out; that counts as a turn.

## Lineage

Written for this slot. Entrance-and-neutral work is common in physical actor training; the one-word read from a watcher and the two-person final pass were added so the drill tests clarity for an audience rather than form for its own sake.
