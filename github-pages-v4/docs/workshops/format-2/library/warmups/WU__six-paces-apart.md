# Six Paces Apart

![Six Paces Apart — infographic](img/WU__six-paces-apart.jpg){ .infographic }

> Two lines face each other and change the physical distance between them, so status becomes a gap you can step into rather than a personality you put on.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy medium` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention on one other person, plus the small motor control of holding or breaking a gaze. It feeds D2.S2 by making status literal: the distance between two bodies, adjustable by either of them, changed by a step rather than by attitude. Sets up the session cue directly.

**Role in the cluster:** connection

## Setup

Clear a strip of floor. Form two lines facing each other, six paces apart, each person opposite a partner. No props, no chairs, no talking once it starts.

## How to run it

1. 30s. Set the lines and pair everyone across the gap. Say the rules out loud: no touching, no talking, and anyone who wants out stands to the side and watches.
2. 45s. Silent eye contact. Line A holds a steady gaze. Line B looks away and back whenever they want. Call a swap at halfway.
3. 75s. You clap a slow pulse. On each clap, Line A steps one pace in or out. Line B answers with a step of their own. Eyes stay up.
4. 75s. Both lines move freely, no clap. Tell Line A to seek two paces, Line B to seek five. Still silent, still no contact. Nobody wins.
5. 45s. Freeze. Everyone holds the distance they ended on and keeps eye contact. Let them feel who conceded ground and who took it.
6. 30s. One question, fast answers around the room, then hand over.

## Side-coach

- *"Play the gap, not the person."*
- *"Eyes up — the feet do the arguing."*
- *"Move because they moved, not because I clapped."*
- *"Small steps count. Half a pace is a whole answer."*
- *"Freeze. Notice where you settled."*

## Build it up

- Add a consequence: whoever breaks eye contact first must give up a pace.
- Speed the clap to double time so the answering step becomes instinct, not calculation.
- Give each line a secret instruction mid-round — one line wants the gap, the other wants it gone — and let them fight it out in silence.

## If it stalls

- Giggling pairs: name it, let the laugh finish, then restart the round with eyes on foreheads instead of eyes.
- Nobody moves in round three: call out one number — "two paces" — and let them race to it, then release them again.
- Room too tight: start at four paces and shrink the step to a half-pace.

## Ask afterwards

1. Who moved first in the last round, and what did their step tell you?
2. When the gap closed, did you feel higher or lower — and why?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One pair of lines, six paces apart. Everyone plays. Odd number: make one trio who hold a triangle and manage three gaps at once. |
| 14–17 | Two facing sets along opposite walls, four paces apart to fit. You clap once for both sets. Cut round three to 60 seconds so the freeze still lands. |
| 18–20 | Three facing sets of three to four pairs each. Keep four paces. Cut rounds two and three to 60 seconds each and hold the freeze for 30 so the total stays at five minutes. |

## Safety & inclusion

No physical contact at any point. Sustained eye contact is intense for some people — offer the forehead as a target and a visible opt-out spot at the side, and say both before you start. Watch that backward steps don't put anyone into a wall, a chair or another pair.

## Lineage

In the Keith Johnstone's status work, where status is treated as a transaction between two people rather than a fixed trait. tradition. Johnstone's status exercises usually run through dialogue and played scenes. We stripped out words entirely and made the only variable the physical distance, so an advanced beginner can feel the seesaw without having to invent a character or a line.
