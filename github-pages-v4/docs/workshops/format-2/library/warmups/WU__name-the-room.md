# Name the Room

![Name the Room — infographic](img/WU__name-the-room.jpg){ .infographic }

> The circle goes quiet and players take turns saying out loud, in one sentence, what state the room is actually in.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy low` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Attention and the group pulse, with the body dropped to near stillness. Match play rewards players who can tell what the crowd already has before they add to it; this makes reading the room a named, spoken, checkable act rather than a vague instinct.

**Role in the cluster:** focus

## Setup

One standing circle, wide enough that everyone can see every face. No chairs, no props. At 18-20, form two circles side by side and run them at the same time.

## How to run it

1. 45s. Circle up. Tell them: stand how you honestly are right now. Tired is fine. Do not perform a mood, and do not fix your face when someone looks at you.
2. 60s. Call "Scan" — ten seconds of silence, everyone looking slowly round the circle. Call "Read" and tap one player. They say one sentence describing the room's real state.
3. Circle answers silently: palm flat on chest means that's true, palm turned outward means not quite. No talking, no defending. Move straight on.
4. 2min. Rapid rounds. Five-second scan, tap a new reader, one sentence, silent verdict. Cut any reading that is a compliment or a joke and ask them again.
5. 60s. Final reader names the room, then gives one instruction to shift it — "drop your shoulders", "three seconds of shaking out". Room does it. Scan again. Did it move?
6. 15s. Silence. Everyone stands still and notices the new state before you hand over.

## Side-coach

- *"Name the room, not a person."*
- *"Say what's there, not what you'd like to be there."*
- *"Two seconds. First reading, out loud."*
- *"Don't fix your face when someone looks at you."*
- *"Silent verdict only — chest or palm out."*

## Build it up

- Readers close their eyes and read by sound and breath only.
- Reader must say what the room wants next, not what it is now.
- Five words maximum, delivered within two seconds of the call.

## If it stalls

- Readings go vague ("good energy"). Ban the word energy and ask for a location: which side, how many people, doing what.
- Readers start flattering the group. Say it plainly: nobody has to be nice here, only accurate. Take the same reader again.
- Verdicts get sluggish. Speed the scan to three seconds and go straight round the circle instead of tapping.

## Ask afterwards

1. Which reading landed as most true, and what was the reader actually looking at?
2. When you're on stage under a clock, what could you scan in three seconds?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | One circle. Everyone reads once; the rapid rounds will cover the whole group. |
| 14–17 | One circle, eight readers. Pick them from opposite sides of the circle so the room gets sampled from different angles. |
| 18–20 | Two circles of nine or ten, running at once. Move between them and call the scans for both. Bring everyone into one circle for the final adjustment round. |

## Safety & inclusion

No contact. Readings describe the group's state only — never an individual's body, looks or personal life. Anyone can say "pass" instead of reading, no explanation needed.

## Lineage

Originally authored for this cluster. The silent palm verdict is borrowed from the shape of a group check-in rather than from any published game, so no one has to argue their reading out loud.
