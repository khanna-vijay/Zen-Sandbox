# Charge and Plant

![Charge and Plant — infographic](img/WU__charge-and-plant.jpg){ .infographic }

> Groups jog to a downstage line, stop dead, and send one plain sentence to the back wall without shrinking.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Legs, breath and voice, and the specific nerve you need in a showcase: arriving in front of people, holding still, and speaking plainly to the back row. Feeds D5.S3 by separating the entrance from the line, so clarity survives adrenaline.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Clear a lane about five metres long. Mark a downstage line with a row of shoes or an existing floor line, at least two metres clear of any wall or chairs. Whole group stands behind the back wall end; you stand at the far end as the back row.

## How to run it

1. (45s) Show the line. Everyone bounces on the balls of the feet at the back, knees loose, breathing out audibly. Explain: charge, stop on the line, hold, then walk back down the side.
2. (60s) In waves of five, jog to the line, stop dead, plant feet shoulder-width, drop the shoulders, eyes on the far wall. Hold three slow counts. Walk back along the side.
3. (60s) Same waves. On the plant, each person says their own name at full volume to the far wall, one at a time down the line. No rush between names.
4. (75s) Same again, but instead of the name each person says one plain true sentence about the room: the floor is cold, the lights are on. No jokes, no cleverness.
5. (60s) Final pass. Watchers stay silent and blank on purpose. Speakers finish the sentence anyway, hold two more seconds, then walk off unhurried.

## Side-coach

- *"Stop dead. Feet planted, weight down."*
- *"Eyes to the back wall, not the floor."*
- *"Say it to the furthest person in the room."*
- *"Finish the line, then hold. Don't leave early."*
- *"Walk off like you meant to."*

## Build it up

- Add a two-second stillness before anyone speaks — no shuffling, no smiling it away.
- Send waves in from alternating sides so people arrive from an unfamiliar angle and still find the front.
- Have watchers murmur or look at their shoes while the line speaks; speakers keep volume and finish.

## If it stalls

- If people rush the last round to escape the silence, run it once more and count the two-second hold out loud yourself.
- If the jog turns into a race, halve the lane and call it a fast walk. Speed is not the point; the stop is.

## Ask afterwards

1. What did your body want to do the second nobody reacted?
2. Which was easier to send to the back wall — your name or the plain sentence?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Waves of five, so two waves per round. Everyone gets two passes at steps 3 and 4. |
| 14–17 | Waves of six, three waves per round. Run the final silent-watchers pass once each, not twice. |
| 18–20 | Waves of five, four waves per round. Combine steps 3 and 4 into one pass — name then sentence — to keep the waves moving. Watchers stand along the sides, not behind. |

## Safety & inclusion

Jog, do not sprint. Set the stop line at least two metres clear of walls, chairs and radiators. Anyone who prefers to walk fast walks fast — same drill, same value. No contact; keep an arm's width between people in a wave.

## Lineage

Built from scratch out of standard entrance-and-freeze presence work, with the deliberately unresponsive final round added so a showcase week rehearses the silence as well as the laugh.
