# Lift the Room

![Lift the Room — infographic](img/WU__lift-the-room.jpg){ .infographic }

> Two facing lines trade places as crowd and stage, scaling noise and body size to a called dial and cutting it dead on command.

`Players 10–20` · `~5 min` · `Complexity 2/5` · `Energy high` · `Contact none` · `Props: none`

## What it warms

Raises pulse and gets arms, legs and voice working at full size within a minute. It feeds D5.S2 directly: players practise reading a room's volume and scaling to it, then cutting a wave dead instead of milking it — the exact muscle behind 'serve the show, not your bit'.

**Role in the cluster:** activation

## Setup

Two lines facing each other, roughly two metres apart, everyone standing. No props. Push chairs to the walls so both lines have room to swing their arms.

## How to run it

1. 30s. Split the room into two lines facing each other. Line A is the crowd. Line B is the stage. Name them out loud so nobody is guessing.
2. 30s. Teach the dial. On your call of one to five, the crowd claps and stomps at that size. Run it up and down once.
3. 75s. Line B each pick one big repeating action — digging, waving, punching air. Call numbers at random; Line B scales their action to the crowd's level, not their own.
4. 30s. Add the landing. On 'Land it', the crowd cuts to silence in one beat and Line B freezes clean. Practise twice.
5. 75s. Swap. Line A takes the stage, Line B becomes the crowd. Same dial, same landing, faster calls.
6. 60s. Last round, no calls. The stage side pushes or pulls on its own; the crowd answers honestly. Call 'Land it' once within twenty seconds and stop.

## Side-coach

- *"Match the room, not your own idea of big."*
- *"Crowd — respond, don't perform."*
- *"Bigger body, same clarity."*
- *"Land it. Silence in one beat."*
- *"You are riding their level, not setting it."*

## Build it up

- Call a number for the crowd and a different number for the stage; players must justify the mismatch and keep going.
- Remove your voice — one player on the stage side sets the level by body alone and the crowd must find it.
- Call two landings in quick succession, then restart at level five with no run-up.

## If it stalls

- If the crowd stays polite at every number, drop to level one for ten seconds so five has somewhere to go.
- If stage players just get louder rather than bigger, cut sound and run one round on body only.

## Ask afterwards

1. When did you notice yourself playing to your own plan instead of to the noise in the room?
2. What made a landing feel clean rather than dropped?

## Scale

| Class size | What changes |
|---|---|
| 10–13 | Two lines of five or six. One round each way, plus the free round. Stand closer, about a metre and a half. |
| 14–17 | Two lines of seven or eight. Same rounds, but hand the dial to one person from each line for the second round so you can watch. |
| 18–20 | Make four lines of five: two facing pairs at opposite ends of the room, running at once. Call numbers for both. Skip the free round if time runs short. |

## Safety & inclusion

Jumping and stomping is optional — anyone can play the whole thing seated or with arms only, and say so before you start. Keep the lines two metres apart so nobody claps into a neighbour's ear. Anyone sensitive to noise can cap themselves at level three or stand at the end of the line; no explanation needed.

## Lineage

In the Short-form comparison and match formats, where crowd noise is part of the machinery tradition. Applause-metre bits and the one-to-ten emotional dial are both long-standing. We fused them and reversed the flow: here the crowd sets the number and the players have to follow it, which is the skill a showcase actually needs. The clean landing was added because riding a wave is easy and stopping one is not.
